## Regulatory Updates This Week

### [DRAFT] RBI Proposes to Ban NBFCs from Offering Revolving Credit

**What:** The RBI released draft amendment directions to the NBFC Credit Facilities framework proposing that NBFCs can only offer term loans and must stop offering revolving credit products.

**Issuer:** RBI, Department of Regulation
**Date:** 6 August 2026 (draft released)
**Feedback deadline:** 28 August 2026
**Reference:** Draft RBI/2026-27/__ DOR.STR.REC.__/21-07-001/2026-27

**In Plain English:**

The RBI wants NBFCs to stop offering credit products where borrowers can draw, repay, and re-draw within a sanctioned limit. Under the draft, NBFCs can only offer term loans, defined as:

- Fixed principal amount
- Disbursed in one or more instalments
- Repaid on a predetermined amortization schedule (periodic instalments or bullet payment)
- Once disbursed, the sanctioned limit cannot be restored or replenished upon repayment

Any fund-based facility that doesn't meet this definition gets classified as "revolving credit" and is prohibited for NBFCs. The only exception is NBFCs specifically authorized by the RBI to issue credit cards.

**Impact on NBFCs:**

- Flexi loans, credit lines, overdraft-style products, and digital lines of credit where borrowers repeatedly draw and repay would be affected
- NBFCs must redesign existing products or discontinue them
- Applies to all NBFCs across all SBR layers (BL/ML/UL)
- The draft also deletes the existing "Demand/Call Loans" provision and paragraph 5(5) from Chapter II

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:**

This is directly relevant. MFL needs to:

1. Map every credit product against the new term loan vs. revolving credit definitions
2. Identify any products where borrowers repay and re-borrow within a sanctioned limit
3. Gold loan products structured as fixed-term with predetermined repayment schedules should qualify as term loans, but any flexibility in re-borrowing needs scrutiny
4. Prepare a feedback response before the August 28 deadline
5. If the draft is finalized, assess system and product redesign costs

**Effective date:** Immediately upon finalization (if draft is finalized as-is)

### [CIRCULAR] RBI Issues NBFC Miscellaneous Supervisory Directions, 2026

**What:** New comprehensive supervisory directions covering fair practices on interest charging, Prompt Corrective Action framework, nomination facility for deposit-taking NBFCs, and Core Financial Services Solution requirements.

**Issuer:** RBI, Department of Supervision
**Date:** 31 July 2026 (effective immediately)
**Reference:** RBI/DoS/2026-27/467

**Key provisions affecting MFL:**

- **Fair Practices on Interest Charging (Chapter III):** NBFCs must not charge interest from the date of loan sanction rather than disbursement, must not charge EMIs on sanctioned amount rather than disbursed amount without consent, and must communicate amortization schedule changes to borrowers. Applies to all NBFCs including gold loan operations.

- **PCA Framework (Chapter V):** Defines risk thresholds and corrective actions for NBFCs-D and NBFCs-ND across layers.

- **CFSS Implementation (Chapter II):** NBFCs in UL and ML with 10 or more fixed point service delivery units must implement Core Financial Services Solution. MFL, as an ML entity, falls under this requirement if it has 10 or more service delivery units.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:**

The interest charging practices provisions are immediately effective and directly relevant to gold loan disbursements. Ensure loan agreements and systems reflect interest computation from date of actual disbursement, not sanction date. The CFSS requirement is significant for ML-layer NBFCs with large branch networks — assess compliance timeline and costs.

## Teaching Session: Revolving Credit vs. Term Loans

### What is Revolving Credit?

Revolving credit is a credit facility where the borrower can draw funds, repay them, and draw again up to a sanctioned limit, repeatedly. Think of it like a credit card or an overdraft. You have a limit, you use some of it, you pay it back, and the limit replenishes so you can use it again.

### What is a Term Loan?

A term loan is a fixed amount of money borrowed for a specific purpose, disbursed in one or more instalments, and repaid according to a predetermined schedule. Once you repay it, the facility is closed. You can't re-borrow without a fresh sanction.

### Why is the RBI Making This Distinction?

The RBI's concern is that revolving credit from NBFCs is functionally similar to bank credit cards or overdrafts but without the same regulatory safeguards. When an NBFC offers a flexi loan or credit line, it's creating money that circulates without clear classification boundaries. If the borrower defaults, it's harder to determine when the loan became non-performing because the outstanding amount fluctuates.

For IRAC (Income Recognition and Asset Classification) purposes, term loans have clear due dates and overdue thresholds. Revolving credit muddies the water because the "due date" is ambiguous when the facility is open-ended.

### What Changes Under the Draft?

The draft amendment directions introduce two key definitions:

**Term Loan** (new definition): A fund-based credit facility with all of the following features:
- Fixed principal amount
- Disbursed in one or more instalments
- Repayable on a predetermined amortization schedule (periodic instalments or bullet)
- Once disbursed, the sanctioned limit cannot be restored or replenished upon repayment

**Revolving Credit** (new definition): Any fund-based credit facility that does not meet the term loan definition above.

The operative provision: "NBFC shall only offer credit products which are in nature of term loans and shall not offer any revolving credit products."

Exception: NBFCs authorized to issue credit cards.

### Impact on Gold Loan Products

Gold loan products are generally structured as term loans — fixed amount, fixed tenure, bullet repayment at the end. This should satisfy the new definition. However, some operational practices could raise questions:

- If a borrower repays a gold loan and immediately takes a new loan against the same gold collateral, is that "replenishment"?
- If the loan agreement allows the borrower to draw additional amounts against incremental gold pledged, does that create a revolving facility?

The answer depends on the specific product structure. A clean gold loan with a fixed amount, fixed tenure, and no replenishment clause should be fine. Any product that allows drawdown-and-repay cycles within a single sanctioned limit needs restructuring.
