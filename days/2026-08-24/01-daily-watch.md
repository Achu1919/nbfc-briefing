**3. Microfinance lifeline running out of time: ₹20,000-cr CGSMFI-2.0 only ~17% utilised, deadline August 31.** Sector trackers put disbursements at only ~₹3,300 cr of the ₹20,000-cr guarantee corpus (sanctions were just ₹770 cr as of June 10). Banks remain unwilling to lend to smaller, below-investment-grade MFIs even with 70–80% sovereign guarantee cover, while large MFIs find the EBLR/MCLR+2% rate caps unattractive versus market borrowings. CareEdge separately warns microfinance NPAs may edge up in FY27 as write-off practices normalise. Small MFIs — the most funding-constrained — are the ones left exposed. ## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Microfinance & SHG Lending (Monday)

**The sector is healing — but the funding window is closing.** Today's focus, per the Monday rotation, is microfinance and SHG lending, and there are three threads running this week:

**1. The credit guarantee scheme is the defining event.** CGSMFI-2.0 (launched March 20, 2026, ₹20,000 cr corpus, 80/75/70% guarantee cover for small/medium/large MFIs, rate caps at EBLR/MCLR+2%, on-lending capped 1% below six-month average rate) closes **August 31**. Utilisation stands at roughly **17% (~₹3,300 cr)**. Banks demand investment-grade ratings even against sovereign guarantees; rate caps make the scheme uneconomic for large MFIs with capital-market access. The July 31 NCGTC tweak (15% of corpus earmarked for small/medium MFIs) and the May cap raise (₹300→₹1,000 cr for large MFIs) helped only marginally. MFIN wants the large-MFI cap at ₹1,500 cr and the per-borrower lending limit up from 20% of AUM. **Why it matters:** small and mid NBFC-MFIs rely on bank lines; without scheme pick-up, funding stress persists through H2 FY27, and rate-cap friction reduces on-lending economics for everyone.

**2. Fundamentals are turning, but NPAs will normalise higher.** Q1 FY27 was the turnaround quarter — Spandana Sphoorty broke a five-quarter loss streak (PAT ₹11.88 cr, NCE 95.9%), Satin Creditcare grew PAT 182%. The FY25 stress (Sa-Dhan's Bharat Microfinance Report showed client base -13% and outstanding loans -14% in FY25 as lenders cleaned books) is behind the sector — but CareEdge warns FY27 NPAs will edge up as write-off practices normalise, i.e., the reported GNPA picture will look slightly worse even as collections stay steady.

**3. Competition from every side.** Diversified NBFCs — Bajaj Finance, Shriram Finance, gold lenders, banks via co-lending — are entering microfinance and SHG segments, ratcheting competition (SBI Securities notes "diversified NBFCs foraying into non-core segments such as MFI and gold loans... ratcheting up competition, leading to margin pressure"). The MFI response is efficiency (Spandana's cost/collection metrics), diversification (business loans, education, green loans) and consolidation (e.g., Criss Financial's proposed merger into Spandana — ICRA Watch-Positive earlier this summer).

**Monday watch-lines:** (a) whether the government extends/tweaks CGSMFI-2.0 before Aug 31; (b) August disbursement data from HDFC Bank/co-lending partners into MFIs; (c) any RBI communication on microfinance pricing/interest-rate directions.
