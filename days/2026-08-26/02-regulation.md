## 🔔 Regulatory Updates & Developments

### 1. IDF-NBFC Large Exposure Limits Tightened — [AMENDMENT]

**What:** RBI applies NBFC-IFC large exposure limits to IDF-NBFCs classified in the Upper Layer.

**Issuer:** RBI  
**Date:** August 25, 2026 (effective immediately)  
**Reference:** RBI — Non-Banking Financial Companies – Concentration Risk Management Fourth Amendment Directions, 2026

**In Plain English:** The RBI has amended its concentration risk rules so that Infrastructure Debt Fund NBFCs (IDF-NBFCs) in the Upper Layer now face the same single-borrower and group exposure limits as Infrastructure Finance Companies (NBFC-IFCs). Previously, IDF-NBFCs had different (generally more lenient) exposure caps. This amendment brings them into alignment.

**Impact on NBFCs:**
- Upper Layer IDF-NBFCs must now comply with stricter per-entity exposure limits
- Limits how much any single borrower/group can owe to an IDF-NBFC
- Part of RBI's broader effort to align risk management across NBFC categories

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** This does **not directly affect** MFL. MFL is not an IDF-NBFC and is not in the Upper Layer under the revised ₹1 lakh crore threshold. However, this signals RBI's intent to progressively tighten concentration-risk norms across the NBFC ecosystem — something ML-layer NBFCs like MFL should monitor.

**Issuer:** RBI (draft stage) / Industry  
**Date:** August 25, 2026 (ongoing)  
**Reference:** Draft RBI (NBFC-Credit Facilities) Amendment Directions, 2026 [EFFECTIVE:2026-08-06]

**In Plain English:** The industry body FISME has formally objected to RBI's proposed blanket ban on revolving credit by NBFCs. Their key argument: working capital finance for productive enterprises is fundamentally different from consumer revolving credit. Separately, major NBFCs including Bajaj Finance, Tata Capital, and Shriram Finance are seeking a direct meeting with RBI to urge reconsideration. The feedback deadline remains **August 28, 2026**.

- Approximately ₹2 lakh crore in revolving credit products at stake
- Affects MSME working capital, credit on UPI, flexi loans, loans against shares
- Piramal Finance MD noted that many products "function like OD but are structured as term loans" — a literal reading could disrupt these
- RBI DG Murmu clarified (Aug 19): "clarity, not prohibition" — intent is to bring discipline, not eliminate products

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** Gold loan bullet repayment products are **unaffected** — they are term loans by nature. MFL's MSME lending products should be reviewed for any revolving features. Low-to-moderate impact on core gold loan business.

**What:** RBI consolidated 628 circulars into 64 Master Directions across 11 entity types. Three key NBFC directions issued.

**Issuer:** RBI  
**Date:** July 31, 2026 (effective immediately)  
**Reference:** 
- NBFC — Miscellaneous Supervisory Directions, 2026 (RBI/DoS/2026-27/467)
- NBFC — Supervisory Returns Directions, 2026 (RBI/DoS/2026-27/466)
- NBFC — IRAC Third Amendment Directions, 2026 (RBI/2026-27/197)

**In Plain English:**

**A. Miscellaneous Supervisory Directions (Chapter highlights):**
- **CFSS mandatory** for ML/UL NBFCs with 10+ service delivery units (akin to core banking for banks)
- **Fair Practices Code tightened** — Cannot charge interest from sanction date; must charge from actual disbursement date; EMIs on disbursed amount only; changes to amortisation schedule must be communicated
- **Nomination facility** mandatory for all NBFC-D deposit accounts
- **Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework** introduced with risk thresholds (capital, asset quality, leverage, liquidity) and graduated corrective measures

**B. Supervisory Returns Directions:**
- Board/senior management responsible for data quality in returns
- All returns filed online via CIMS portal
- Strict timelines with penalties for late/incorrect filings
- Data aggregation and reporting capabilities required

**C. IRAC Third Amendment (effective Oct 1, 2026):**
- Interest on Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFA): accrued interest from extinguished exposure not recognised as income upon acquisition
- Income from SNFA treated as non-interest/other income
- Existing SNFA income reversal deadline: September 30, 2027

## 📚 Teaching Session — Day 8: NPA Classification for NBFCs

### What is it?

NPA (Non-Performing Asset) classification is the system RBI uses to categorise loans that have stopped generating income for the lender. When a borrower stops paying, the loan moves through progressively worse categories — and the lender must set aside more money (provisioning) as a buffer against potential losses.

Think of it like a medical triage: a "normal" patient (Standard asset) gets routine care, but once symptoms appear (overdue payments), the patient moves to increasingly critical categories requiring more intensive treatment (higher provisioning).

### The Technical Details

**The 90-Day Rule (Income Recognition)**
[Source: RBI Master Direction — IRAC Directions, updated October 2024]

For NBFC-ML and NBFC-UL (aligned with banks from Oct 2024):
- An account becomes NPA if interest and/or principal remains overdue for **90 days or more**
- "Overdue" means not paid by the due date
- Interest must be recognised only when realised (cash basis for NPAs)
- If interest is overdue >90 days but principal is current, the account is still NPA

**Classification Hierarchy:**

| Category | When it applies | Meaning |
|----------|----------------|---------|
| **Standard** | < 90 days overdue | Borrower is current or recently late but within limits |
| **Sub-Standard** | ≥ 90 days overdue | Borrower has defaulted; lender may not recover full amount |
| **Doubtful (D1)** | ≥ 12 months in sub-standard | Recovery is uncertain |
| **Doubtful (D2)** | ≥ 24 months in sub-standard | Recovery is more uncertain |
| **Doubtful (D3)** | ≥ 36 months in sub-standard | Recovery is highly uncertain |
| **Loss** | Unrecoverable | RBI/examiner/investigation determines the loan is uncollectible |

**Key Differences from Banks (pre-Oct 2024):**
Prior to October 2024, NBFCs used a **360-day** overdue threshold for NPA. RBI aligned NBFCs with the **90-day** bank standard from October 2024 onwards.

**Special Rules for Gold Loans:**
Gold loan NPAs are typically very low because:
1. Gold collateral is valued at a conservative LTV (max 75%)
2. Gold prices generally appreciate
3. Auction process exists for recovery

However, even gold loans become NPA if:
- Interest is not paid for 90 days
- Principal is not repaid at maturity (bullet loan maturity = due date)

### Why It Matters

1. **Provisioning cost**: NPA classification forces the NBFC to set aside money from profits
2. **Capital erosion**: High NPAs reduce Net Worth (NOF) which can breach capital adequacy ratios
3. **Credit rating**: Rating agencies weight NPAs heavily; high NPAs = lower rating
4. **RBI scrutiny**: High NPAs attract supervisory attention and potential PCA
5. **Profitability**: Interest on NPAs must stop being recognised (hit to P&L)

### Key Numbers/Thresholds

| Metric | NBFC-ML/UL (post Oct 2024) | Old NBFC Norm |
|--------|---------------------------|---------------|
| NPA trigger | 90 days overdue | 360 days overdue |
| Sub-standard start | 90 days | 360 days |
| Doubtful start | 12 months in sub-standard | 15 months in sub-standard |
| Loss determination | By audit/examiner | By audit/examiner |
| Interest recognition on NPA | Cash basis only | Cash basis only |

### Provisioning Rates (for reference)

| Asset Classification | Secured | Unsecured |
|---------------------|---------|-----------|
| Standard | 0.40% | 1.00% |
| Sub-Standard | 15% | 25% |
| Doubtful D1 | 25% | 40% |
| Doubtful D2 | 40% | 70% |
| Doubtful D3 | 100% | 100% |
| Loss | 100% | 100% |

### Recent Changes

1. **Oct 2024 — 90-Day NPA Norm**: The biggest change — NBFC-ML/UL moved from 360-day to 90-day overdue for NPA classification, bringing them in line with banks. This was part of the broader regulatory convergence under SBR.

2. **Jul 2026 — IRAC Third Amendment**: New rules on income recognition for Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFA). If an NBFC acquires a non-financial asset (e.g., property from a restructured loan), accrued interest from the extinguished exposure cannot be recognised as income. Effective October 1, 2026.

### Muthoot Fincorp Context

For MFL's gold loan business:

1. **Gold loan NPAs are inherently low** — LTV cushion and gold price appreciation provide strong protection. The 2024 gold price rally (₹70,000+ per 10g) has only widened this cushion.

2. **Bullet repayment risk**: Gold loans are often bullet-repayment (repay principal + interest at maturity). If a borrower doesn't repay at maturity, the account becomes overdue. 90 days after that = NPA. MFL needs robust maturity tracking.

3. **FP&A impact**: The shift from 360-day to 90-day NPA recognition (Oct 2024) would have front-loaded NPA recognition for MFL's portfolio. Any accounts that were "standard" under the 360-day norm but overdue 90-360 days would have been reclassified as NPA.

4. **Provisioning impact**: With 0.40% on standard secured gold loans, provisioning is manageable. But if NPAs spike, provisioning jumps to 15% (sub-standard) — a 37x increase per account.

5. **New Directions**: The Jul 2026 interest charging rules mean MFL must ensure gold loan interest is calculated from the actual disbursement date, not the sanction date. This needs a systems check.

### Quiz Yourself

**Q1:** A gold loan borrower took a ₹5 lakh loan on July 1, 2026 with 6-month tenure. They haven't paid anything. On what date does the loan become NPA?

**Answer:** The loan matures on December 31, 2026. From January 1, 2027, it's overdue. It becomes NPA on **March 31, 2027** (90 days after overdue). If the borrower also hasn't paid interest for 90+ days, it could become NPA earlier (interest overdue triggers NPA before principal maturity in some cases).

**Q2:** What happens to interest income when a loan becomes NPA?

**Answer:** Interest income recognition switches from accrual basis to **cash basis**. The NBFC can only book interest income when it is actually received in cash, not when it is "earned" on paper. Any accrued but unrealised interest must be reversed from P&L.

**Q3:** What is the provisioning rate for a gold loan classified as Sub-Standard?

**Answer:** **15%** of the outstanding balance (assuming it's secured by gold collateral). This is a massive jump from the 0.40% for standard assets — illustrating why NPA prevention is so important for profitability.
