### ⚠️ URGENT: Revolving Credit Ban — Comment Deadline TOMORROW (Aug 28)

- **What:** RBI proposes banning ALL NBFCs from offering revolving credit facilities (flexi loans, credit lines, etc.). Only term loans would be permitted. Credit card issuers are exempt.
- **Issuer:** RBI (Department of Regulation)
- **Date:** Draft issued August 6, 2026. **Comment deadline: August 28, 2026 (tomorrow!)**
- **Reference:** Draft Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies — Credit Facilities) Amendment Directions, 2026
- **In Plain English:** If this draft becomes final, NBFCs like Bajaj Finance, Muthoot Fincorp, and every other NBFC that offers "flexi loans" or "revolving credit lines" where borrowers can draw down, repay, and re-draw the same credit limit — would have to convert ALL such products to fixed term loans. The sanctioned limit cannot be "restored/replenished upon repayment of either the whole or a part of the principal amount."
- **Impact on NBFCs:** This is **potentially the single biggest product-level regulation for NBFCs in years.** Products like:
  - Flexi personal loans / salary overdrafts
  - Business credit lines (working capital)
  - Supply chain financing (revolving structure)
  - Consumer durable finance with revolving features
  - Share margin funding (demand/revolving)
  
  All would need to be restructured as term loans. This affects fee income (revolving facilities generate ongoing interest), product design, customer proposition, and treasury management.
- **Muthoot Fincorp Note:** MFL's core gold loan business uses bullet repayment (term loans), so the gold loan book is **largely unaffected**. However, if MFL has any MSME lending, personal loan overdraft facilities, or microfinance revolving products, those would need review. The key risk is operational — converting existing revolving products to term loans requires system changes, re-documentation, and customer communication.
- **Action Required:** If MFL has any revolving credit products, the compliance and product teams should review the draft and consider submitting comments via Connect 2 Regulate by tomorrow.

### 📋 Draft Interest Rates on Loans & Advances Directions 2026 (Comment by Sep 11)

- **What:** RBI proposes a single harmonised interest rate framework for ALL regulated entities — banks, NBFCs, HFCs, AIFIs, cooperative banks. Currently, interest rate rules exist only for banks; NBFCs were governed only by conduct norms.
- **Issuer:** RBI (Department of Regulation)
- **Date:** Draft issued August 12, 2026. Comment deadline: September 11, 2026. Proposed effective date: April 1, 2027.
- **Reference:** Draft Reserve Bank of India (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026 [EFFECTIVE:2027-04-01 if finalised]
- **In Plain English:** For the first time, RBI is bringing NBFCs under a structured interest rate determination framework. Key provisions:
  1. **Board-approved interest rate policy** mandatory for all REs
  2. All loans must be linked to an **internal or external benchmark + risk-based spread** (even fixed-rate loans)
  3. Loans cannot be priced **below the benchmark** (anti-undercutting rule)
  4. **APR ceiling** mandatory on microfinance and small-value loans (≤₹50,000)
  5. Interest on floating-rate loans must reset **at least every 3 months** (with exceptions for BL NBFCs, Tier 1-2 UCBs)
  6. **External benchmark linkage mandatory** for floating-rate personal and MSME loans for banks — but **NOT mandatory for NBFCs**
  7. Existing benchmark-linked loans must migrate by April 1, 2029
- **Impact on NBFCs:** This is a paradigm shift. NBFCs currently have wide discretion on interest rate pricing. Under the new framework:
  - Must document benchmark methodology, spread components, and pricing delegation
  - Gold loan NBFCs (like MFL) will need to articulate their internal benchmark (which may be cost of funds or external benchmark) and spread methodology
  - MFI subsidiaries must put APR ceilings on microfinance loans
  - Positive: External benchmark linkage is NOT mandatory for NBFCs — more flexibility than banks
- **Muthoot Fincorp Note:** Gold loan interest rates are typically set based on cost of funds + margin. MFL would need to:
  1. Adopt a Board-approved interest rate policy document
  2. Define its internal benchmark (likely MCLR-style or cost-of-funds based)
  3. Document spread components (credit risk, operating cost, gold price risk, margin)
  4. Ensure loan agreements specify benchmark, reset frequency, and spread separately
  5. If MFL has any floating-rate products, specify reset periodicity (max 3 months)
  
  **FP&A Impact:** This will formalise what most NBFCs already do informally, but the documentation and governance overhead is new. The migration deadline of April 2029 gives reasonable time.

### 📋 Recovery Agent Conduct — Comprehensive Framework (Effective Jan 1, 2027)

- **What:** RBI issues a sweeping overhaul of recovery agent conduct rules, applying a uniform standard across ALL regulated entities — banks, NBFCs, HFCs — for the first time.
- **Issuer:** RBI (Department of Regulation)
- **Date:** Issued August 6, 2026. **Effective: January 1, 2027**
- **Reference:** RBI/2026-27/230 — Reserve Bank of India (NBFCs — Responsible Business Conduct) Third Amendment Directions, 2026 [EFFECTIVE:2027-01-01]
- **In Plain English:** RBI has replaced scattered, entity-specific recovery agent instructions with a single comprehensive framework. Nine parallel circulars were issued on Aug 6 covering banks, SFBs, LABs, RRBs, UCBs, RCBs, AIFIs, NBFCs, and HFCs.
- **Key Changes:**
  1. **"Recovery agency" definition widened** — covers any entity regardless of contractual label (cannot hide behind nomenclature)
  2. **IIBF certification mandatory** for all recovery agents (existing agents must certify within 1 year)
  3. **Board-approved recovery policy** mandatory, covering: triggers, escalation matrix, code of conduct, borrower compensation for losses, demise-of-borrower procedures
  4. **Recovery agency list on website** — updated within 7 days of any change
  5. **Contact hours: 08:00–19:00 only** (unless borrower expressly agrees otherwise)
  6. **Prohibited practices** — abusive language, contacting relatives/friends, violence, misleading borrowers about debt
  7. **Device locking heavily restricted** — prohibited by default except for financed mobile devices, with narrow exceptions and mandatory one-hour reversal after repayment
  8. **Grievance redressal officer** mandatory for recovery complaints
  9. **Call recording** mandatory with 6-month retention
  10. **Compensation to borrowers** for losses from non-compliant recovery actions
- **Impact on NBFCs:** This is a 5-month implementation project. NBFCs must:
  - Draft and get Board approval for recovery policy
  - Renegotiate agency contracts to include code of conduct undertakings
  - Build website disclosure infrastructure for agency lists
  - Implement call recording and retention systems
  - Verify IIBF certification across agent base
  - Review incentive structures (bonuses tied to recovery rates could induce harsh practices)
  - If using device-locking technology (e.g., in device finance), rebuild against narrow exceptions
- **Muthoot Fincorp Note:** As a major gold loan NBFC, MFL's recovery operations (which are largely through gold auction rather than agent-based collection of personal loans) may be less affected than consumer finance NBFCs. However:
  - Any third-party collection agents used for overdue interest collection must be brought into compliance
  - The IIBF certification timeline is tight (by Jan 2028)
  - If MFL has any microfinance or personal loan portfolio with agent-based recovery, full policy framework needed
  - Gold auction process (under existing RBI directions) remains separate — but the "possession of security" provisions apply

### 📋 Concentration Risk — Fourth Amendment (Aug 25, 2026)

- **What:** RBI extends NBFC-IFC large exposure limits to IDF-NBFCs in the Upper Layer.
- **Issuer:** RBI (Department of Regulation)
- **Date:** August 25, 2026. Effective immediately.
- **Reference:** RBI/2026-27/248 — Reserve Bank of India (NBFCs — Concentration Risk Management) Fourth Amendment Directions, 2026 [EFFECTIVE:2026-08-25]
- **In Plain English:** Infrastructure Debt Fund NBFCs (IDF-NBFCs) classified in the Upper Layer under SBR are now subject to the same large exposure limits as NBFC-Infrastructure Finance Companies. This restricts how much they can lend to a single borrower/group.
- **Impact on NBFCs:** Narrow scope — only IDF-NBFCs in UL. General NBFCs including MFL are unaffected.
- **Muthoot Fincorp Note:** No direct impact. MFL is not an IDF-NBFC.

## 📚 Teaching Session: RBI Draft Interest Rate Directions 2026 — A Deep Dive

### What Is It?

The **Draft Reserve Bank of India (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026** is a proposed single harmonised framework governing how ALL regulated entities (REs) — banks, NBFCs, HFCs, AIFIs, cooperative banks — determine and charge interest rates on their loan portfolios.

This is historically significant because, until now, RBI had detailed interest rate regulations only for **commercial banks** (MCLR framework, external benchmark linkage). NBFCs were governed mainly by **conduct-related** norms (fair practices, transparency) but had no prescriptive interest rate determination framework.

### Why Is RBI Doing This?

Three reasons stated by Governor Malhotra:

1. **Uniformity:** Different entity types had different rules, creating an uneven playing field
2. **Transparency:** Borrowers couldn't easily compare rates across bank vs NBFC vs HFC loans
3. **Consumer Protection:** Ensure no entity charges usurious rates, especially on microfinance and small-value loans

### The Technical Details

#### Applicability
| Entity Type | Covered? | Mandatory External Benchmark? |
|-------------|----------|-------------------------------|
| Commercial Banks | ✅ | Yes (personal + MSME floating-rate loans) |
| SFBs / LABs | ✅ | Same as banks |
| RRBs | ✅ | No (optional) |
| UCBs (Tier 3 & 4) | ✅ | No (optional) |
| RCBs (>₹1000cr deposits) | ✅ | No (optional) |
| AIFIs (NABARD, SIDBI, etc.) | ✅ | No (optional) |
| **NBFCs (all layers)** | ✅ | **No (optional)** |
| HFCs | ✅ | No (optional) |

#### Key Provisions for NBFCs

**1. Board-Approved Interest Rate Policy (Para 5)**
- Must document: benchmark methodology, spread components, loan categories, pricing delegation
- Reviewed at least annually

**2. Interest Rate Framework (Para 6)**
- Fixed or floating rates permitted
- All rates must reference an **internal or external benchmark + risk-based spread**
- Loans cannot be priced **below the applicable benchmark** (anti-undercutting)
- Interest charged at **monthly rests** (except agricultural loans)
- Computed on **daily reducing balance** basis
- **Actual/Actual** day count convention

**3. Benchmark Requirements**
- **Internal benchmark:** Must be based on cost of funds. For banks, this is MCLR. For NBFCs, can be any internally determined cost-of-funds benchmark
- **External benchmark:** Repo rate, T-bill yields, SORR, or any FBIL-published rate
- **Reset frequency:** Maximum once every 3 months for floating-rate loans
  - **Exception:** BL NBFCs, Tier 1-2 UCBs, and RCBs with deposits ≤₹1,000cr are exempt from mandatory 3-month reset

**4. Spread Rules (Para 21)**
- Spread = credit risk premium + other components (operating cost, margin, tenor premium, etc.)
- Spread components and ranges must be defined in policy
- **Once a loan is sanctioned, the spread cannot be changed** unless there's a change in credit risk

**5. APR Ceiling (Para 6.6)**
- **Mandatory APR ceiling** on:
  - Microfinance loans
  - Small-value personal loans (≤₹50,000)
- Must ensure rates are **not usurious**

**6. Migration Timeline (Para 30)**
- All existing benchmark-linked loans must migrate to the new framework by **April 1, 2029**
- Migration must not disadvantage borrowers or charge migration fees

### Why It Matters for NBFCs

| Aspect | Before | After (Proposed) |
|--------|--------|------------------|
| Interest rate regulation | Conduct norms only | Full pricing framework |
| Benchmark requirement | None | Internal/external benchmark mandatory |

| Fixed-rate loan rules | Virtually none | Must link to benchmark + spread |
| External benchmark | Optional | Optional for NBFCs (good news!) |
| Migration deadline | N/A | April 1, 2029 |

### Recent Changes / Context

This draft follows the **August 5, 2026 monetary policy** where Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu announced RBI's intention to harmonise interest rate regulations across all REs. The draft consolidates **six separate circulars** (previously applicable only to banks) into one unified framework.

Key consultation points:
- How should NBFCs define their internal benchmark?
- Should NBFCs be required to adopt external benchmark for any category?
- What should the APR ceiling be for MFI and small-value loans?
- How to handle the transition for existing gold loan interest rate structures?

### Muthoot Fincorp Context

**Gold loan interest rates** are typically fixed-rate, set as a flat rate per month (e.g., ₹12 per ₹1,000 per month). Under the new framework:

1. **Benchmark linkage required:** Even fixed-rate gold loans would need to reference an internal benchmark + spread. MFL would need to document: "Our benchmark is X (cost of funds), our spread is Y (gold price risk + operating cost + margin)"

2. **No mandatory external benchmark:** Good news — gold loan rates don't need to be linked to repo rate

3. **APR disclosure:** Gold loan APRs would need to be transparent (current practice of quoting per-₹1,000 monthly rate would need APR translation)

4. **Policy documentation:** Board needs to formally approve the interest rate policy with all components

5. **System changes:** Loan management systems would need to separately track benchmark and spread components for reporting and audit

**FP&A Impact:** 
- Interest income recognition unlikely to change (accrual-based on fixed rate continues)
- BUT documentation and governance overhead increases
- Treasury team needs to formalise what they already do informally
- Budget for system upgrades to separate benchmark/spread in loan records

### Quiz Yourself

**Q1:** Under the draft directions, can an NBFC price a floating-rate loan below its internal benchmark? Why or why not?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
No. Paragraph 10 states: "A RE shall not price a loan below the applicable benchmark for that loan." This is an anti-undercutting provision to ensure benchmark integrity and prevent predatory pricing.
</details>

**Q2:** Are NBFCs required to link their floating-rate personal loans to an external benchmark like banks are? What's the exception?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
No. Paragraph 20 explicitly states that the mandatory external benchmark linkage for floating-rate personal and MSME loans applies ONLY to commercial banks. NBFCs, RRBs, UCBs, RCBs, and AIFIs may choose to offer external benchmark loans at their discretion. This is a significant flexibility advantage for NBFCs.
</details>

**Q3:** If MFL's current gold loan interest rate is quoted as ₹12 per ₹1,000 per month, how would this need to change under the new framework?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
MFL would need to: (a) identify an internal benchmark (e.g., cost of funds), (b) calculate the spread (gold price risk premium + operating cost + margin), (c) document both in the Board-approved interest rate policy, (d) disclose the benchmark and spread separately in loan agreements, and (e) ensure the total rate doesn't go below the benchmark. The flat-rate quoting convention would continue but must be justified as benchmark + spread.
</details>
