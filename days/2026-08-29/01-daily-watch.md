# 📊 NBFC Daily Watch — Friday, August 28, 2026 (published Sat, Aug 29)

## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

1. **FIDC formally asks RBI to drop the revolving-credit ban — comments window closed Aug 28.** The Finance Industry Development Council (FIDC), the NBFC industry body, submitted its objections to RBI's draft *NBFC Credit Facilities Amendment Directions, 2026*, which would force NBFCs to offer only term loans and bar all revolving credit products unless the NBFC is an authorised credit-card issuer. FIDC warned the blanket restriction would hurt working-capital access for MSMEs and hit borrowers who use flexi/overdraft-style facilities (a roughly **$26 billion** product market). The draft landed Aug 7 and immediately knocked Bajaj Finance / Bajaj Finserv / Tata Capital shares down 5–6%. The comment deadline was **August 28, 2026** — the ball is now in RBI's court on finalisation. This is the single most consequential NBFC regulatory story of the week. *(Deep regulatory analysis is in NBFC Guru's 7 AM report.)*
2. **Piramal Finance closes ₹2,100 crore QIP at the top of the band — near-10x demand.** The QIP (opened Aug 25, closed Aug 28) was priced at **₹2,110/share**, the upper end of the ₹2,000–2,110 band, drawing bids of nearly **₹21,000 crore (~10x)**. Global names: BlackRock, Goldman Sachs AM, Eastspring Investments; domestic MFs incl. ICICI Pru, Nippon India, Kotak, Quant, Axis, ABSL. This is Piramal Finance's first QIP since its November 2025 listing. Capital adequacy (18.9% pre-raise) gets a ~2pp lift; combined with the ₹1,750 crore promoter warrant issue (Nithyam Realty, EGM Sept 19), total equity infusion ≈ **₹3,850 crore**. Stock has doubled from its ₹1,124 IPO price — closed Aug 28 at ₹2,229.50 (mcap ₹50,538 crore).
3. **NBFCs enter an earnings upgrade cycle.** Brokerages raised FY27 NBFC earnings estimates by ~4% and FY28 by ~1% over the last three months, led by vehicle finance upgrades, as Q1FY27 validated the cyclical recovery — collections are better, slippages lower, credit-cost normalisation faster (ET BFSI, this morning). Business Standard (yesterday): FY27 outlook robust on strong AUM growth despite margin pressure from higher borrowing costs and competitive intensity.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive (Saturday) — Infrastructure & Project Finance

**Regulatory tailwind, weak demand data — infra lending is the slowest-growing NBFC segment right now.**

- **RBI's new risk-weight framework (finalised Jan 2026, effective Apr 1, 2026)** is genuinely favourable for NBFCs financing infrastructure: loans to **"high-quality" operational projects** (≥1 year post-COD, standard asset, concession-backed by govt/PSU contracts with escrow/TRA, pari-passu charge and step-in rights for lenders) attract **75% risk weight once the borrower has repaid ≥2% of sanctioned debt, and 50% once ≥5% is repaid** — thresholds were relaxed from 5%/10% in the draft. That's real capital relief for lenders concentrating on operational roads, power, renewables and ports, and it differentiates greenfield risk from operational assets for the first time. NBFC-IFCs and large players (PFC, REC, IREDA, Tata Capital, L&T Finance legacy infra books) are the natural beneficiaries.
- **But the RBI data shows why: as of May 2026, infrastructure credit (the largest slice of NBFC industry lending, ₹19.98 lakh crore) grew only 5.8% YoY** — well below overall NBFC credit growth of 14.2% (₹58.61 lakh crore). Power-sector lending also +5.8%. Compare commercial real estate +40.2% and gold loans +69.9% — capital is chasing retail, not infra.
- **Tightening oversight:** effective Aug 25, 2026, Upper-Layer **IDF-NBFCs** must follow NBFC-IFC large-exposure caps (Concentration Risk 4th Amendment) — a compliance squeeze for the largest infra lenders even as risk weights ease.
- **Power financiers are in a soft patch:** REC Q1 disbursements -9% YoY (AUM +2%), PFC similar, hurt by utility repayments (RBPF schemes) and slower power capex — but asset quality "exceptionally strong" and credit costs firmly low (Emkay). **PFC–REC merger** remains in progress. REC was among the biggest drags on the Fin Services index on Friday.
- **Green infra is where the growth is:** dedicated NBFCs — Ecofy (raised ₹380 cr led by BII/Finnfund in March; ₹1,400+ cr AUM, solar + e-2W/3W), Aerem/NetZero Finance (800 MW solar financed), Revfin (85,000+ EVs financed) — are scaling with development-finance backing; Budget 2026-27 continues to push new infrastructure financing instruments.
- **Read-through for Muthoot Fincorp's world:** the divergence is stark — secured retail (gold) growing ~70% vs infra at ~6%. Expect NBFC credit to keep tilting toward collateralised retail until project/utility capex revives; watch H2FY27 for the RBI risk-weight relief to start moving the needle on infra disbursements.
