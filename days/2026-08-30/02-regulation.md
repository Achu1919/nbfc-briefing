**In Plain English:** RBI inspected these companies with reference to their March 31, 2025 financial position. The penalties are relatively small (₹2–8 lakh each), but the **pattern** matters: RBI is systematically walking through NBFC inspections and issuing penalties for even minor compliance gaps. The Muthoot MCred penalty is particularly notable — it's a Muthoot Group entity penalised for incorrect NPA upgrades.

**Impact on NBFCs:**
- Every NBFC must verify: are we upgrading NPAs only after **all** arrears across **all** facilities are cleared? (Not just the specific facility)
- KYC risk categorisation review must happen at least every 6 months — automated system required
- Board changes exceeding 30% require **prior written RBI permission** — this is strictly enforced
- Internal Ombudsman must auto-escalate all rejected complaints — manual intervention is not enough
- Financial statements must contain **correct and complete** complaint data

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** ⚠️ **Directly relevant.** Muthoot MCred (formerly Muthoottu Mini Financiers) is a Muthoot Group entity. MFL must ensure its own NPA upgrade processes are airtight — the ₹3.10 lakh penalty is trivial, but the reputational risk and the signal to supervisors is not. Specifically:
- Verify that no loan account is upgraded from NPA to Standard unless **entire** arrears of interest and principal pertaining to **all credit facilities** to that borrower have been fully repaid

### 2. 📝 [INDUSTRY RESPONSE] FIDC Urges RBI to Rethink Revolving Credit Ban — Comment Period Closed Aug 28

**What:** The Finance Industry Development Council (FIDC), representing NBFCs, submitted detailed feedback to RBI on August 27 urging modification of the proposed blanket ban on revolving credit products.

**Key FIDC Arguments:**
1. **Broad product impact** — Supply chain finance, working capital demand loans, MSME loans, vehicle dealer loans, loans against securities, and fintech products all face disruption
2. **Redraw ≠ Evergreening** — FIDC distinguishes between restoring repaid principal within a sanctioned facility (should be allowed) vs. perpetual credit lines (should be banned)
3. **MSME working capital** — Businesses with fluctuating cash flows need redraw facilities; forcing term loans raises costs and pushes borrowers to informal lenders
4. **TReDS impact** — NBFC factoring on Trade Receivables Discounting System could be disrupted
5. **Loan against securities** — Should be permitted with LTV, margin, and mark-to-market safeguards
6. **Regulatory arbitrage** — Banks continue offering identical working-capital facilities; blanket NBFC ban creates competitive imbalance

**FIDC's Proposed Modification:**
Permit restoration/replenishment of principal repaid **ahead of schedule**, subject to:
- Cannot increase original sanctioned amount
- Cannot extend final maturity
- Cannot operate when any amount is overdue

**What Happens Next:** Comment period closed August 28. RBI will now review all submissions. Based on the strength of industry pushback (Bajaj Finance, Tata Capital, Shriram Finance all lobbied directly), expect either:
- A modified final direction with carve-outs for supply chain finance and loan against securities, or
- A significantly delayed implementation with a longer transition period

**Impact on NBFCs:** If implemented as drafted, approximately ₹2 lakh crore of NBFC credit products are affected. If modified per FIDC's suggestions, the impact narrows to pure evergreening-style products (flexi-loans, automatic credit lines).

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** Gold loan renewals are the key concern. When a borrower repays and re-pledges gold, is that a "restoration" of the sanctioned limit? FIDC's proposed carve-out (allowing redraw of prepaid amounts within a declining ceiling) would protect standard gold loan renewal practices. But if RBI sticks to the absolute ban, MFL needs to restructure its renewal process as a fresh loan sanction each time.

**Source:** Reuters, Aug 28, 2026; CNBC-TV18, Aug 28, 2026; FIDC letter to RBI dated Aug 27, 2026

## 📚 Teaching Session — Day 2 of 30: RBI's Regulatory Framework for NBFCs

### What is the RBI's regulatory framework for NBFCs?

The RBI regulates NBFCs through a layered system of legislation, directions, circulars, and master directions. Unlike banks (which are governed primarily by the Banking Regulation Act, 1949), NBFCs are governed by a patchwork of:

1. **The RBI Act, 1934** — Chapter V-B (Sections 45-IA to 45-QU) provides the foundational powers
2. **Companies Act, 2013** — Registration and corporate governance
3. **RBI Directions** — Master Directions, circulars, and notifications that operationalise the law
4. **Self-Regulatory Organisations** — Industry bodies like FIDC that influence policy (as we saw this week)

### The Legal Architecture

| Layer | What It Does | Key Sections |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **Registration** | Every NBFC must register with RBI | Section 45-IA |
| **NOF** | Minimum net owned fund requirement | Section 45-IB |
| **Deposits** | Rules for deposit-taking NBFCs | Section 45-IC, 45-ID |
| **Reserve Fund** | Transfer 20% of net profit to reserve | Section 45-IC |
| **Audit** | Statutory audit + RBI inspection | Section 45-J |
| **Power to Issue Directions** | RBI can issue directions to any NBFC | Section 45-JA, 45-L, 45-M |
| **Penalty** | Monetary penalty for non-compliance | Section 58-G, 58-B |
| **Power to Issue Directions** | RBI can issue directions to any NBFC | Section 45-JA, 45-L, 45-M |

### How RBI Regulates NBFCs vs Banks

| Aspect | Banks | NBFCs |
|--------|-------|-------|
| **Registration** | Banking license from RBI | Certificate of Registration from RBI |
| **Deposit insurance** | Yes (DICGC, up to ₹5 lakh) | **No** — deposit-taking NBFCs have no deposit insurance |
| **Priority sector lending** | Mandatory (40% of ANBC) | Mandatory for NBFC-MFIs; not for others |
| **CRR/SLR** | Mandatory | Not applicable |
| **Branch expansion** | Prior RBI permission beyond 4 branches in FY | Limited branch authorisation rules |
| **CRAR** | 9% minimum (Basel III) | 15% for ML/UL layer; varies by type |
| **LCR** | Mandatory | Mandatory for ML and UL layer NBFCs |
| **Wilful defaulters** | Can declare and report | Cannot declare (no legal backing) |
| **SARFAESI** | Yes — can enforce security | Only for NBFC-MFIs and NBFC-Factors |
| **IBC threshold** | ₹1 crore | ₹1 crore (for operational creditors) |

### RBI's Supervisory Toolkit

RBI has four main tools to regulate and enforce compliance:

1. **On-site Inspection** — RBI inspectors examine books, systems, and compliance at the NBFC's premises. This is how Muthoot MCred's NPA upgrade violation was caught (inspection reference: March 31, 2025 financial position).

2. **Off-site Surveillance** — Quarterly returns (NBS-7, NBS-8, NBS-9 for different NBFC types) submitted to RBI allow continuous monitoring without a physical visit.

3. **Prompt Corrective Action (PCA)** — Introduced July 31, 2026 for NBFCs. If an NBFC breaches certain thresholds (CRAR, NPA, leverage), RBI can impose restrictions on dividends, branch expansion, and new lending.

4. **Monetary Penalty** — Section 58-G penalties. The five NBFCs penalised this week demonstrate RBI's willingness to act on even small violations.

### The Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) Framework

Since October 2022, RBI classifies NBFCs into four layers based on size, activity, and risk:

| Layer | Asset Size (as of March 31) | Key Requirements |
|-------|----------------------------|------------------|
| **Base Layer (BL)** | < ₹1,000 crore | NOF ₹2 crore; leverage ratio ≤ 7x |
| **Middle Layer (ML)** | ₹1,000–10,000 crore | NOF ₹10 crore; CRAR 15%; Tier I 10%; full governance requirements |
| **Upper Layer (UL)** | ≥ ₹10,000 crore (or designated) | NOF ₹30 crore; CRAR 15%; Tier I 10%; NBFC-UL Master Direction; enhanced disclosures |
| **Top Layer** | Based on RBI's risk assessment | Enhanced regulatory requirements determined case-by-case |

**Gold-loan NBFCs** have a special requirement: **Tier I capital must be at least 12%** (not the standard 10%).

### Recent Changes to the Framework

1. **July 31, 2026** — Five Master Directions issued consolidating all NBFC supervision, audit, compliance, and cybersecurity rules
2. **August 6, 2026** — Annual UL list published (17 NBFCs including Muthoot Finance, Bajaj Finance, Tata Capital)
3. **August 6, 2026** — Recovery Agent Conduct directions finalised (effective January 2027)
4. **August 6, 2026** — Draft revolving credit ban released (comments closed August 28)
5. **August 12, 2026** — Draft Interest Rate Directions released (comments due September 11)

### Why It Matters for NBFCs

- **Compliance burden is increasing.** The number of Master Directions has grown from 3 to 8+ since SBR was introduced.
- **Enforcement is real.** Five penalties in one week shows RBI is not just writing rules — it's policing them.
- **Regulatory arbitrage is narrowing.** The Interest Rate Directions draft aims to harmonise rules across banks and NBFCs. The revolving credit ban creates a new asymmetry, but FIDC is pushing to close it.
- **Governance failures attract the biggest penalties.** Shri Ram Finance's ₹8.10 lakh penalty (for board appointment without prior permission) shows that governance shortcuts are expensive.

### Quiz Yourself

**Q1:** An NBFC's CRAR falls to 13.5%. The minimum requirement is 15% for its layer. What immediate action must the board take?

**Answer:** The board must acknowledge the breach, prepare a remediation plan, and submit it to RBI. Under the PCA framework (effective July 2026), breaching CRAR thresholds can trigger restrictions on dividends, branch expansion, and new lending. The board should also assess whether the breach affects their ability to accept deposits (if applicable) and whether any covenant breaches have been triggered in their borrowing agreements.

**Q2:** RBI inspects an NBFC and finds it upgraded 3 NPA accounts to Standard without full repayment of arrears. What is the likely consequence?

**Answer:** Based on the Muthoot MCred precedent (August 14, 2026), RBI will issue a show-cause notice, conduct a personal hearing, and impose a monetary penalty. The violation is classified under "Asset Classification" — the rule requires that all arrears of interest and principal pertaining to **all credit facilities** to the borrower must be fully repaid before upgrading from NPA to Standard. The penalty is typically ₹1–5 lakh, but the reputational cost and the supervisory flag are far more significant.

**Q3:** An NBFC wants to appoint a new director who will be the 6th director on a 9-member board (replacing one who resigned). Does it need RBI permission?

**Answer:** It depends on whether the total board change exceeds 30% of the existing board. If 3 or more directors have changed (excluding independent directors) within a period, prior written RBI permission is required. The Shri Ram Finance penalty (₹8.10 lakh) was specifically for this violation — the company appointed a director without obtaining prior RBI permission when more than 30% of its directors had changed. MFL should maintain a board-change tracker and proactively seek RBI permission whenever approaching the 30% threshold.
