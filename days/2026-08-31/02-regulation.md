## 🔔 Regulatory Updates — Sunday, 31 August 2026

### 1. 📝 [DEVELOPING] Revolving Credit Consultation Closes — RBI Now Reviewing Industry Feedback

**What:** The RBI's draft NBFC Credit Facilities Amendment Directions, 2026, which proposed a blanket ban on revolving credit products for NBFCs, had its stakeholder feedback window close on August 28, 2026. The regulator is now processing submissions from industry bodies, individual NBFCs, and MSME federations.

**Key Submissions Received:**
- **FIDC (Aug 27):** Urged RBI to distinguish between redraw within a sanctioned facility and evergreening. Suggested limited redraw be permitted where the facility has a fixed ceiling, predetermined amortisation schedule, and fixed maturity. Sought carve-outs for supply chain finance, TReDS factoring, and loan against securities.
- **FISME:** Warned blanket ban would choke MSME working-capital access, pushing smaller borrowers to informal lenders. Asked RBI to distinguish consumer convenience credit from productive working-capital finance.
- **Individual NBFCs (Bajaj Finance, Tata Capital, Shriram Finance):** Met on Aug 14 to coordinate a joint representation through FIDC, estimating the ban could disrupt credit products worth over two lakh crore rupees.

**What Happens Next:** RBI typically takes four to eight weeks to issue final directions after consultation closes. Final directions expected by mid-October 2026, though RBI could extend the consultation if feedback warrants it. The amendment will come into force immediately upon final issuance.

**Impact on NBFCs:**
- Any NBFC offering revolving credit, flexible loans, working capital demand loans, supply chain finance, loans against securities, or fintech credit products must prepare for restructuring
- Product mapping against the draft definition is essential even before final directions land
- If RBI maintains the blanket ban, NBFCs face competitive disadvantage versus banks offering identical products

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** Gold loans (bullet repayment) are not classified as revolving credit, so the core product is not directly affected. However, any MSME lending, working capital facilities, or loans against securities offered through subsidiaries or new product lines would fall under the draft definition. The FP&A team should map group-wide exposure to revolving credit products now.

**Source:** RBI Draft Notification, Aug 6, 2026; FIDC letter to RBI, Aug 27, 2026 (reported by Reuters, CNBCTV18, Business Standard)

### 2. 📊 [DATA] Gold Loan GNPAs at Record Low — NBFC Sector Shows Strengthening Collateral Buffers

**What:** A Lok Sabha starred question (August 10, 2026) revealed that gold loan Gross NPA ratios for NBFCs (Middle and Upper Layer, excluding CICs, PDs, HFCs) declined from 2.32% in March 2023 to 0.81% in March 2026 (provisional). Scheduled Commercial Banks saw a similar decline from 0.19% to 0.12% over the same period.

**Key Data Points:**
| Period | SCBs GNPA (Gold Loans) | NBFCs GNPA (Gold Loans) |
|--------|------------------------|------------------------|
| March 2023 | 0.19% | 2.32% |
| March 2024 | 0.19% | 2.35% |
| March 2025 | 0.22% | 2.13% |
| March 2026 (prov.) | 0.12% | 0.81% |

The RBI's Financial Stability Report (June 2026) confirmed that LTV ratios have declined across banks and NBFCs even as gold lending grew, meaning collateral buffers are strengthening as gold prices rise. Number of gold loan defaults has declined over the last five years.

**Impact on NBFCs:** Strong gold loan asset quality validates the sector's collateral-first model. However, when asset quality looks uniformly good across a sector, the regulator's focus tends to shift from prudential tightening to conduct-related norms — particularly around valuation transparency, auction procedures, and borrower protection.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** Directly relevant. MFL's core gold loan book benefits from this trend. The 0.81% GNPA for NBFCs is a significant improvement and reflects well on MFL's peer group. The convergence between bank and NBFC gold loan NPA ratios suggests the June 2025 Gold and Silver Collateral Directions (harmonised norms, 75% LTV cap, 12-month bullet repayment cap, stricter auction procedures) are working as intended. Full compliance effective April 1, 2026.

**Source:** Lok Sabha USQ No. 3648(E), August 10, 2026; RBI FSR June 2026

### 3. ⏰ [UPCOMING] Interest Rate Directions — Comments Close September 11

**What:** The Draft RBI (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026, issued August 12, would replace six separate RE-specific circulars with a single harmonised rulebook covering fixed and floating rate loans for banks, NBFCs, AIFIs, and cooperative banks. Effective date proposed: April 1, 2027. Comments due by September 11, 2026 (10 days from today).

**Key Provisions for NBFCs:**
- Standardised pricing methodology: benchmark plus spread for all floating rate loans
- Board-approved Annual Percentage Rate (APR) ceiling for microfinance and personal loans up to fifty thousand rupees (no specific numerical cap proposed yet)
- Migration timeline: two years from effective date (by April 2029)
- Applies to all regulated entities including NBFCs, HFCs, and cooperative banks

**Impact on NBFCs:** This is a fundamental change to how NBFCs price and disclose loan costs. The APR ceiling for small-ticket MFI/personal loans could directly constrain pricing flexibility. The harmonised framework eliminates regulatory arbitrage between different RE types.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** High impact. MFL's gold loan pricing, microfinance products, and any personal loan offerings would all fall under this framework. The APR ceiling for loans up to fifty thousand rupees needs immediate analysis from a pricing and margin perspective. If MFL has not yet submitted comments, the September 11 deadline is imminent.

**Source:** RBI Draft Notification, Aug 12, 2026; CorpLawUpdates.in; Vinod Kothari analysis

### 4. ✅ [IN EFFECT] NBFC Miscellaneous Supervisory Directions 2026 — Active Since July 31

**What:** RBI's Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies — Miscellaneous) Supervisory Directions, 2026, issued July 31 with immediate effect. Key requirements now in force:

- **CFSS Implementation:** NBFCs in Upper and Middle Layers with 10 or more fixed point service delivery units must implement Core Financial Services Solution (akin to CBS for banks). Quarterly progress reports required to RBI's Senior Supervisory Manager.
- **Fair Practices — Interest Charging:** NBFCs must review and correct practices of charging interest from sanction date (not disbursement date), EMIs on sanctioned amount rather than disbursed amount, changes to amortisation schedule not communicated to borrowers, and collecting installments in advance while charging interest on full amount.
- **Nomination Facility:** NBFCs-D must obtain nominations from all existing and new eligible deposit customers. Board/CSC must review coverage periodically. Progress reported to SSM through DAKSH portal quarterly.
- **PCA Framework:** New Prompt Corrective Action framework with risk thresholds for NBFCs-D and NBFCs-ND (excluding CICs). Exit from PCA requires four continuous quarterly statements showing no breaches.

**Impact on NBFCs:** CFSS is a significant IT and operational investment for ML/UL NBFCs. The fair practices on interest charging could require system-level changes to loan management systems. PCA framework gives RBI a structured intervention tool.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** As an ML-layer NBFC, MFL falls under the CFSS requirement if it has 10 or more fixed point service delivery units (branches with customer-facing operations). The fair practices on interest charging should be reviewed against MFL's current gold loan and microfinance disbursal practices. The PCA framework is a reminder that capital adequacy and asset quality thresholds now have formal consequences.

**Source:** RBI/DoS/2026-27/467, July 31, 2026; Master Direction available at rbi.org.in

### 5. 🔍 [CLARIFICATION] SEBI: Stock Brokers Cannot Undertake NBFC Activities

**What:** SEBI's Nodal Coordination Cell, in an interpretive letter dated August 10, 2026, clarified that a SEBI-registered stock broker may not undertake activities as an NBFC under the current regulatory framework. The clarification came in response to Relitrade Stock Broking Private Limited's request to hold both registrations simultaneously.

**Key Points:**
- Regulation 12(1) of the Stock Brokers Regulations, 2026 permits stock brokers to carry out activities under another financial sector regulator's framework "in the manner specified by the Board"
- Currently, only NDS-OM government securities activities (under RBI) and IFSCA-regulated activities in GIFT City are permitted
- SEBI has not issued any framework permitting NBFC activities by stock brokers
- Rule 8(1)(f) and 8(3)(f) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 also prohibit brokers from engaging in business other than securities

**Impact on NBFCs:** Limited direct impact on most NBFCs. Relevant for financial conglomerates considering stock broking + NBFC under the same entity. Clarifies the regulatory wall between SEBI and RBI regulated activities.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** Low direct relevance unless MFL or group entities are considering entering stock broking or vice versa.

**Source:** SEBI Informal Guidance, Issue No. 1/18462/2026, August 10, 2026

## 📚 Teaching Session: NBFC Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework

The July 31 NBFC Miscellaneous Supervisory Directions introduced a formal PCA framework for NBFCs. Here's what you need to know.

### What is PCA?

PCA is RBI's early intervention mechanism. When an NBFC breaches certain financial thresholds, RBI places it under PCA and imposes restrictions. Think of it as a yellow card before the red card. The NBFC gets a chance to fix its problems, but with constraints on what it can do.

### Who Does It Apply To?

NBFCs-D (deposit-taking) and NBFCs-ND in Middle, Upper, and Top Layers. Excludes Core Investment Companies. Does not apply to Base Layer NBFCs or those not accepting public funds.

### Risk Thresholds

PCA is triggered by breaches in three parameters:

**Capital Adequacy:**
- Tier I capital ratio below 6% (or CRAR below 10%) triggers restrictions
- Tier I below 4.5% (or CRAR below 7.5%) triggers stronger restrictions

**Asset Quality:**
- Net NPA ratio above certain thresholds triggers restrictions
- The exact thresholds are set in the Master Direction

**Leverage:**
- Total debt to tangible net worth ratio above thresholds

### What Happens Under PCA?

Depending on severity, RBI may:
- Restrict dividend payments
- Restrict branch expansion
- Restrict new business lines
- Require special Board approvals for certain decisions
- In extreme cases, restrict further lending

### Exit from PCA

An NBFC can exit PCA only when:
- No breaches in risk thresholds for four continuous quarterly financial statements
- One of those quarters must be the most recent quarter

### Why It Matters for MFL

PCA is a formalised version of what RBI has always done informally through supervisory dialogue. The difference now is that the triggers are transparent and the consequences are codified. For MFL's capital planning, the key takeaway is that falling below Tier I of 6% or CRAR of 10% doesn't just trigger a conversation with RBI — it triggers formal restrictions on business operations.
