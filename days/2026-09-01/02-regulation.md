#### 1. Draft Interest Rate Directions — Comments Due Sep 11 ⚠️

**What:** RBI issued draft "Interest Rates on Loans and Advances Directions, 2026" on Aug 12 proposing a harmonised pricing framework for ALL regulated entities (banks, NBFCs, HFCs). This is the most sweeping change to NBFC interest rate regulation in years.

**Key proposals:**
- All REs must have **Board-approved pricing policy** reviewed annually
- Interest rate = **Internal/External Benchmark + Risk-Based Spread**
- **APR ceiling mandatory** for microfinance loans (≤₹50k) and small-value loans
- No lending **below the benchmark** — eliminates below-benchmark pricing
- External benchmark adoption is **discretionary** (not mandatory) for NBFCs
- **Floating-rate resets** max every 3 months (BL NBFCs exempt from this)
- Existing loans must migrate to new framework by **April 1, 2029**
- New loans from **April 1, 2027**

**MFL Impact:** 🔴 HIGH — MFL must prepare Board-level pricing policy documentation, implement benchmark+spread methodology across gold loans and MSME lending, and plan migration of existing portfolio. Group-company lending needs genuine credit appraisal backing.

**Source:** [Economic Times — RBI floats harmonised interest rate determination draft rules](https://legal.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/regulators/rbi-floats-harmonised-interest-rate-determination-draft-rules-for-banks-nbfcs/133187703) | [Vinod Kothari FAQ](https://vinodkothari.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FAQ-on-Draft-Directions-for-interest-loans-and-advances.pdf)

#### 2. Recovery Agent Conduct Directions — Final (Aug 6)

**What:** Comprehensive overhaul of recovery agent engagement rules. Replaces scattered provisions with a single framework.

**Key requirements:**
- **IIBF certification** mandatory for all recovery agents (1-year transition for existing agents)
- **Recovery hours:** 8 AM to 7 PM only
- **Call recording** mandatory, retained for 6 months
- Board-approved **recovery policy** covering due diligence, code of conduct, compensation for borrower losses
- **Gradual device restrictions** — full restrictions only after 60 days past due; incoming calls/SMS/emergency SOS never restricted
- Policy must address **financial distress situations** with documented procedures

**MFL Impact:** 🔴 HIGH — MFL must update recovery agency agreements, ensure all agents get IIBF certified by Jan 2028, implement call recording infrastructure, and draft Board-level recovery policy.

**Source:** [RBI Circular Index — Third Amendment Directions, 2026](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/bs_circularindexdisplay.aspx/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=13672) | Ref: RBI/2026-27/230 DOR.MCS.REC.No.199/01-01-039/2026-27

#### 3. IRAC 3rd Amendment — SNFA Income Recognition (Jul 16, eff Oct 1)

**What:** Interest accrued on Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFA) acquired by NBFCs cannot be recognised as income. Any income already recognised on SNFAs outstanding as of Sep 30, 2026 must be reversed through P&L by Sep 30, 2027.

**MFL Impact:** 🟡 MEDIUM — Review any SNFA acquisitions in the portfolio. Ensure interest reversal on extinguished exposures is properly booked.

## 📚 Teaching Session: Interest Rate Framework — What the Draft Directions Mean for NBFCs

Since the **Sep 11 deadline** for comments on the Interest Rate Directions draft is 10 days away, today's teaching session covers this critical topic in depth.

### What Is the Draft Interest Rate Directions, 2026?

The RBI wants to replace the patchwork of interest rate rules — which currently differ between banks, NBFCs, and cooperative banks — with **one unified, principles-based framework** applicable to every regulated lender in India.

Until now, NBFCs were governed mainly by **conduct-related** aspects (fair practices, transparency) rather than pricing regulations. Banks had benchmark-linked pricing rules. The draft Directions bring everyone under the same roof.

### The Technical Details

**Source:** Draft RBI (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026 | Issued August 12, 2026 | Comments due September 11, 2026

**Who it applies to:**
- Commercial Banks, RRBs, UCBs, RCBs, AIFIs
- All NBFCs including HFCs (all layers)

**What REs must do:**

1. **Board-approved pricing policy** — reviewed at least annually, covering:
   - Methodology for determining interest rates
   - Internal benchmark definition
   - Spread components
   - Loan categories
   - Delegation of pricing powers

2. **Benchmark + Spread model for all loans:**
   - Fixed-rate loans: Internal/External Benchmark + Risk-Based Spread
   - Floating-rate loans: Internal/External Benchmark + Risk-Based Spread
   - **Cannot price below the benchmark** for any loan

3. **APR ceiling mandatory** for:
   - Microfinance loans
   - Small-value loans
   - Must ensure rates are not usurious

4. **Floating-rate reset:** Every RE must reset the benchmark at periodicity not exceeding 3 months — **except BL NBFCs, Tier 1/2 UCBs, and small RCBs**

5. **External benchmark is optional** for NBFCs (unlike banks where it's mandatory for floating retail loans)

6. **Migration deadline:** All existing benchmark-linked loans must be migrated to the new framework by **April 1, 2029**

### Why It Matters

| Aspect | Current State | Under Draft Directions |
|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| Pricing regulation | NBFCs: conduct-based only; Banks: benchmark-linked | Unified for all REs |
| Benchmark type | NBFCs: internal only; Banks: external for retail floating | Both allowed for all |
| Below-benchmark pricing | Permitted by NBFCs | **Banned** |
| MFI pricing | Rate caps exist but inconsistent | **APR ceiling mandatory** |
| Documentation | Informal | Board-approved, annual review |
| Migration | Not required | All existing loans by Apr 2029 |

### What This Means for Muthoot Fincorp

1. **Gold Loan Pricing:** MFL currently uses internal benchmarking for gold loans. The draft requires formal benchmark + spread documentation. Gold loans are generally fixed-rate products, so the migration burden is lighter — but the pricing policy and KFS requirements will need to be rigorous.

2. **MSME / Microfinance:** Any microfinance or sub-₹50k loans will face an explicit APR ceiling. MFL must calculate all-inclusive APR (interest + all fees/charges) and ensure compliance.

3. **Group Lending:** Risk-based pricing for lending to group/promoter entities must have genuine credit appraisal backing. RBI is watching for "financing vehicles for promoter/group entities" disguised as genuine lending.

4. **FP&A Impact:** 
   - Treasury and ALM teams need to formalise benchmark identification
   - All loan products need a documented spread calculation methodology
   - MIS systems must capture and report benchmark + spread separately
   - Migration planning for 2+ years of existing loans

5. **Timeline pressure:** Comment window closes Sep 11. Even before final issuance, MFL should begin internal assessment of:
   - Current pricing methodology vs. proposed framework
   - Products affected (gold, MSME, personal, group lending)
   - System changes needed
   - Gap analysis for Board presentation

### Recent Changes

This draft follows Governor Malhotra's August 5, 2026 announcement of intent to "rationalise the regulatory framework on interest rates for all REs on a principle-based basis." The draft was released on August 12. Final Directions will be issued separately for each category of RE after considering public feedback.

### Quiz Yourself

1. **Q:** Under the draft, can an NBFC price a floating-rate gold loan below its internal benchmark if the borrower has excellent collateral?
   **A:** No. The draft explicitly states REs **cannot price a loan below the applicable benchmark** for that loan category, regardless of risk profile.

2. **Q:** MFL has 2,000+ branches. What is the deadline for migrating all existing benchmark-linked loans to the new framework?
   **A:** April 1, 2029 — a one-time mapping exercise. This gives approximately 2.5 years from the effective date.

3. **Q:** Is MFL required to adopt an external benchmark (like repo rate) for its floating-rate loans?
   **A:** No — unlike banks, external benchmark adoption is **discretionary** for NBFCs. MFL can continue with internal benchmarks, but the pricing methodology must be formally documented and Board-approved.
