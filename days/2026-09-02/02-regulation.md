#### 1. ⚠️ Draft Interest Rate Directions — Comment Deadline Sep 11 (9 DAYS LEFT)

**What:** RBI issued draft "Interest Rates on Loans and Advances Directions, 2026" on Aug 12 proposing a harmonised pricing framework for ALL regulated entities (banks, NBFCs, HFCs, co-op banks, AIFIs).

**Key proposals:**
- All REs must have **Board-approved pricing policy** reviewed annually
- Interest rate = **Internal/External Benchmark + Risk-Based Spread**
- **APR ceiling mandatory** for microfinance loans (≤₹50k) and small-value loans
- No lending **below the benchmark** — eliminates below-benchmark pricing
- Floating-rate reset frequency ≤ 3 months (except BL NBFCs)
- **Migration deadline:** All existing benchmark-linked loans must migrate by **April 1, 2029**

**Key dates:**
| Parameter | Date |
|-----------|------|
| Comment deadline | **September 11, 2026** |
| Effective date (new loans) | April 1, 2027 |
| Migration deadline (existing) | April 1, 2029 |

**MFL Impact:** 🔴 HIGH — MFL's gold loan pricing, MSME lending rates, and any microfinance products all need formal benchmark + spread documentation. Internal benchmark adoption is allowed (unlike banks where external is mandatory for retail floating), but the pricing methodology must be rigorously documented and Board-approved.

**FP&A Action:** This is the most impactful pending regulation for MFL. With the comment deadline 9 days away:
- Internal review of current pricing methodology vs proposed framework should be completed this week
- Product-by-product mapping (gold, MSME, personal loans) against draft requirements
- If MFL wishes to submit comments to RBI through industry bodies (FIDC, etc.), the window closes Sep 11
- Even without submitting comments, begin gap analysis and system impact assessment

**Source:** RBI Draft Notification, Aug 12, 2026 | [Corplaw Updates Analysis](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/rbi-draft-interest-rates-loans-advances-directions-2026)

#### 2. NBFC Miscellaneous Supervisory Directions, 2026 (Effective Immediately)

**What:** RBI issued comprehensive NBFC Miscellaneous Supervisory Directions on Jul 31, 2026 covering CFSS implementation, Fair Practices Code (interest charging), Nomination Facility, and the Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework.

**Key requirements effective NOW:**

**A. Core Financial Services Solution (CFSS)**
- NBFCs in Upper and Middle Layers with **≥10 fixed point service delivery units** must implement CFSS (akin to Core Banking Solution)
- Quarterly progress reports to RBI's Senior Supervisory Manager
- CFSS must provide: seamless digital customer interface, anywhere/anytime facility, integrated functions, centralised database, and suitable MIS

**B. Fair Practices Code — Interest Charging**
RBI flagged 5 specific unfair practices that must be corrected:
1. Charging interest from sanction date, not disbursement date
2. Charging EMIs on sanctioned amount vs actual disbursed amount
3. Changing amortisation schedule without communicating to borrowers
4. Charging interest for entire month when loan is disbursed/repaid mid-month
5. Collecting instalments in advance but charging interest on full loan amount

**C. Nomination Facility (NBFC-D only)**
- NBFCs accepting deposits must obtain nominations from all eligible customers
- Board/CSC must review nomination coverage periodically
- Quarterly reporting to RBI via DAKSH portal

**D. Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework**
- Applies to NBFCs-D and NBFCs-ND (ML/UL/TL) excluding CICs
- Risk thresholds based on CRAR, NPA ratios, leverage ratio
- Triggers restrict dividends, branch expansion, management compensation

**MFL Impact:** 🔴 HIGH
- **CFSS:** MFL, as an ML-layer NBFC with 2,000+ branches, must implement CFSS. This is a major IT/systems investment. Quarterly progress reporting to RBI is mandatory.
- **Fair Practices:** Interest charging practices across all gold loan, MSME, and personal loan products must be audited against the 5 flagged practices. Any deviation needs immediate correction.
- **PCA Framework:** MFL must monitor its CRAR, NPA, and leverage metrics against PCA thresholds to avoid regulatory restrictions.

**Source:** RBI/DoS/2026-27/467, Jul 31, 2026 | [RBI Notification](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13586&Mode=0)

#### 4. IRAC 3rd Amendment — SNFA Income Recognition (Effective Oct 1, 2026)

**What:** RBI amended the IRAC Directions for NBFCs on Jul 16, introducing new rules for income recognition on Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFA) acquired by NBFCs.

**Key changes effective Oct 1, 2026:**
- Accrued but unrealised interest/charges from periods **prior to SNFA acquisition** cannot be recognised as income
- Any such income already recognised in books as of Sep 30, 2026 must be reversed through P&L **by Sep 30, 2027**
- Income from SNFA must be recognised as "non-interest/other income" in the year realised
- Upkeep expenses for SNFA must be expensed in the year incurred

**MFL Impact:** 🟡 MEDIUM — Relevant if MFL or any group entity has acquired or plans to acquire non-financial assets (real estate, equipment, etc.) as part of debt recovery or restructuring. The FP&A team should check whether any SNFA income has been recognised in prior periods.

**Source:** RBI/2026-27/197, Jul 16, 2026 | [RBI Notification](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13575&Mode=0)

## 📚 Teaching Session: Corporate Governance for NBFCs

**What is it?**

Corporate governance is the system of rules, practices, and processes by which an NBFC is directed and controlled. For NBFCs, RBI prescribes specific governance norms that go beyond the Companies Act 2013 requirements — these are designed to ensure that NBFCs, which handle public deposits and systemic risk, are managed by competent, independent, and accountable people.

Think of it as the "operating system" of the company — who makes decisions, who watches the decision-makers, and what checks and balances exist to prevent misuse of power.

---

**The Technical Details**

RBI's governance requirements for NBFCs are primarily contained in:
- **Chapter V of the NBFC-ML Master Direction** (RBI/DoR/2023-24/106, Oct 19, 2023)
- **RBI (NBFC- Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025**
- **RBI (NBFC — Miscellaneous) Supervisory Directions, 2026** (Jul 31)
- **Companies Act, 2013** (Sections 149, 150, 177, etc.)

**A. Board Composition Requirements**

| Requirement | NBFC-ML | NBFC-UL |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| Minimum directors | 6 | 6 |
| Independent directors (% of board) | ≥50% | ≥50% |
| Woman director | ≥1 (if applicable under Companies Act) | ≥1 |
| CEO/MD age limit | 70 years | 70 years |
| CEO/MD maximum tenure | As per Companies Act | 15 years (can be extended by special resolution) |
| Fit & proper certification | Mandatory | Mandatory |

**B. Fit & Proper Criteria**

Every director and key managerial person (KMP) must be "fit and proper" as per RBI's Fit & Proper criteria for NBFCs. Disqualifications include:
- Conviction by a court for any offence involving moral turpitude
- Being declared a wilful defaulter by any bank/FI
- Being an undischarged insolvent
- Has been removed from service of any company/financial institution
- Default in repayment to NBFCs/Banks
- Connected with a company that has been wound up
- Disqualified as a director under the Companies Act

For NBFC-UL directors, additional requirements:
- **Net worth of ₹5 crore** (personal net worth)
- Must provide a declaration of personal net worth annually
- No person who is a director in more than 10 NBFCs simultaneously

**C. Mandatory Board Committees**

| Committee | Composition | Key Responsibilities |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Audit Committee** | Min 3 independent directors;Chair must be independent | Financial reporting, internal controls, statutory audit, related party transactions |
| **Nomination & Remuneration Committee** | Min 3 directors; majority independent | Director appointments, CEO succession, remuneration policy |
| **Stakeholders Relationship Committee** | As per Companies Act | Investor complaints, share transfers, dividend payment |
| **Risk Management Committee** | Min 3 directors; CEO/MD cannot chair if also chair of Audit Committee | Risk identification, assessment, mitigation, risk appetite |
| **ALCO** | Board-level; CEO/MD typically chairs | Asset-liability management, interest rate risk, liquidity risk |
| **IT Strategy Committee** | At least one IT-expert director | IT policy, cybersecurity, digital transformation |
| **CSR Committee** | As per Companies Act (if applicable) | CSR spending, CSR policy |
| **NCLT Committee** (for Related Party Transactions) | As per SEBI LODR (listed NBFCs) | RPT approval, arm's length compliance |

**D. Related Party Transactions (RPT)**

This is one of the most scrutinised areas of NBFC governance:

1. **Definition:** A transaction with a "related party" — includes directors, their relatives, promoter group entities, subsidiaries, associates, and any entity where a director has material interest
2. **Board approval:** All RPTs must be approved by the Audit Committee
3. **Material RPTs (listed NBFCs):** Require shareholder approval via special resolution; cannot be passed through postal ballot
4. **Arm's length:** All RPTs must be at arm's length — meaning the terms must be as favorable as what would be available from an unrelated party
5. **Disclosures:** All RPTs must be disclosed in the financial statements — both individually and in aggregate
6. **Thresholds:** Individual transaction value or outstanding balance ≥₹1 crore OR ≥1% of total assets, whichever is lower

**E. Whistle-blower Policy (Vigil Mechanism)**

- All NBFC-ML and above must have a whistle-blower policy
- Directors and employees can report concerns about accounting, internal controls, or audit matters
- Audit Committee oversees the mechanism
- Protection against victimisation for whistle-blowers
- Must be posted on the company website

**F. CEO/MD Separation & Age Limits**

- **NBFC-UL:** CEO/MD must not exceed 70 years of age. If CEO completes 15 years, renewal requires special resolution
- **NBFC-ML:** No specific age cap, but fit & proper criteria apply
- The role of Chairman and MD should preferably be separated (not mandatory but recommended by RBI)
- Non-executive Chairman is preferred for NBFC-UL

**G. Board Meetings**

- Minimum **4 board meetings per year** (quarterly)
- Maximum gap between two consecutive meetings: **120 days**
- At least one meeting every quarter with independent directors present
- Board meetings must cover: financial results, risk review, compliance review, governance matters

---

**Why It Matters**

Weak governance has been at the centre of India's biggest NBFC crises:
- **IL&FS (2018):** Board failed to flag massive hidden leverage; collapsed, triggering a liquidity crisis for the entire NBFC sector
- **DHFL (2019):** Related party transactions with promoter entities drained funds; eventually went to IBC
- **Yes Bank (2020):** Governance failures led to disproportionate exposure to risky borrowers

RBI's governance norms are designed to prevent these scenarios. For NBFCs that handle public deposits or have systemic importance, the consequences of governance failure extend beyond the company — they affect depositors, lenders, and the broader financial system.

**For listed NBFCs specifically:** SEBI's LODR adds another layer — quarterly compliance certification, related party transaction norms, board evaluation requirements, and continuous disclosure obligations.

**Quiz Yourself:**

1. **Q:** MFL wants to lease office space from a company where one of its independent directors is also a director. What approval process is required?

   **A:** This is a Related Party Transaction. It must be approved by the Audit Committee (not the full board). If the transaction value exceeds the materiality threshold (₹1 crore or 1% of total assets, whichever is lower), it also requires shareholder approval. The transaction must be at arm's length — MFL should obtain an independent valuation of the lease terms to demonstrate fairness.

2. **Q:** MFL's board has 8 directors, including 3 independent directors. Is this compliant with RBI governance norms for an NBFC-ML?

   **A:** No. RBI requires at least 50% of the board to be independent directors. With 8 directors, MFL needs at least 4 independent directors, but currently has only 3 (37.5%). MFL must appoint at least one additional independent director to achieve 50% (4 out of 8).

3. **Q:** Under the new PCA framework (Jul 31, 2026), what happens if MFL's CRAR falls below 9%?

   **A:** If CRAR falls below 9% (one of the PCA risk thresholds for NBFCs-ND), RBI can impose restrictions including: prohibition on dividend payments, restriction on branch expansion, restriction on management compensation, requirement to submit a remediation plan. The severity depends on which specific threshold is breached — 9% CRAR, NPA >6%, or leverage ratio >26x.
