## 📚 Teaching Session: Interest Rate Directions, 2026 — What Every NBFC FP&A Team Needs to Know

*Why today's topic? The draft "Reserve Bank of India (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026" has a comment deadline of **September 11, 2026** — just 8 days away. RBI DG Murmu has signalled this will go through. MFL needs to understand the implications NOW.*

### What Is It?

RBI has proposed a **single, harmonised framework** for interest rate determination on loans and advances across ALL regulated entities (REs) — banks, NBFCs, HFCs, cooperative banks, RRBs, and AIFIs. Until now, detailed lending-rate rules mainly targeted banks (MCLR, external benchmark mandates), while NBFCs had lighter conduct-related norms. This draft changes that fundamentally.

### The Technical Details

#### A. Core Principles (Applies to ALL REs)

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Benchmark-based pricing** | Every loan must reference an internal benchmark OR an external benchmark, plus a risk-based spread |
| **Floor on pricing** | Loan cannot be priced below the applicable benchmark for that loan |
| **Daily reducing balance** | Interest must be computed on daily reducing balance basis |
| **Actual/Actual day count** | No more 360-day year conventions |
| **Monthly rests** | Interest charged at monthly rests (annual for long-duration agri; crop-cycle for short-duration agri) |
| **Board-approved policy** | RE must have a Board-approved interest rate policy, reviewed at least annually |

#### B. Fixed Rate Loans — NEW Ground

- Must be priced off a benchmark + spread (not ad hoc)
- Cannot be priced below the benchmark
- When switching between fixed ↔ floating, the respective provisions apply to each period

#### C. Floating Rate Loans

- **Benchmark, reset periodicity, and reset date** must be specified in the loan agreement
- Resets generally **not exceeding 3 months** (BL NBFCs exempt from this specific requirement)
- Spread components must be defined: **Credit Risk Premium (CRP) + Operating Cost + Term Premium + Business Strategy Premium**

| Spread Component | Change Rule |
|---|---|
| CRP | Can only change when borrower's credit profile changes (after comprehensive review) |
| Other components | Cannot be revised before 3 years for floating-rate loans |
| Exception | Can reduce earlier for customer retention on non-discriminatory grounds |
| BL NBFC exemption | Spread revision restrictions not mandatory for Base Layer NBFCs |

#### D. External Benchmark — NOT Mandatory for NBFCs

This is the **key difference** from banks:
- **Banks:** Floating-rate retail loans and MSME loans MUST link to external benchmark (repo rate, T-Bill yields, SORR, etc.)
- **NBFCs, AIFIs, RRBs, cooperative banks:** MAY, at their discretion, choose external benchmark — **not mandatory**
- This is a significant win for NBFC pricing flexibility

#### E. APR Ceiling — High Impact for MFL

| Loan Type | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Microfinance loans** | RE must **explicitly put a ceiling on APR** inclusive of all charges/fees |
| **Small-value personal loans** (principal ≤ ₹50,000) | Same APR ceiling requirement |
| **All rates** | Must not be usurious |
| **Short-term agri loans** (≤1 year) to small/marginal farmers | Total interest + charges cannot exceed the principal amount |

**Key definition:** "Small-value loan" = personal loan to an individual where principal ≤ ₹50,000.

#### F. Migration of Existing Loans

- All existing loans must be migrated to the new framework via a **one-time mapping exercise**
- **Migration deadline:** April 1, 2029
- **Borrower consent required** for migration
- **Migration cannot increase the interest rate** applicable immediately before transition
- **No charges** may be levied for migration

### What It Means for NBFCs

1. **Pricing discipline:** No more ad hoc rate-setting. Every loan product needs a documented benchmark methodology. This is a significant compliance uplift for smaller NBFCs.

2. **Spread transparency:** The four-component spread structure (CRP, operating cost, term premium, business strategy premium) forces NBFCs to decompose their pricing. This data will likely feed into supervisory analysis.

3. **3-year lock on spread components (except CRP):** For floating-rate loans, you cannot adjust operating cost, term premium, or business strategy premium for 3 years. This constrains pricing agility — if your cost of funds rises, you can't immediately pass it through via spread changes (unless it's credit risk related).

4. **External benchmark is optional:** RBI has explicitly kept external benchmark adoption voluntary for NBFCs. This is recognition that NBFC funding structures are more complex (market-linked, NCD-based) and a single benchmark may not fit all products.

5. **APR ceiling is a pricing cap:** For MFI loans and small-ticket personal loans, the APR ceiling is effectively a regulatory price cap. This could compress margins if the ceiling is set aggressively.

### Quiz Yourself

**Q1:** Under the draft Interest Rate Directions, can MFL offer a gold loan priced below its internal benchmark?
- **Answer:** No. Para 7/9 states "A RE shall not price a loan below the applicable benchmark for that loan." The floor is the benchmark itself.

**Q2:** If MFL's cost of funds increases by 50 bps, can it immediately increase the spread component on existing floating-rate microfinance loans?
- **Answer:** Only if the increase is attributable to credit risk premium (CRP) and tied to a change in the borrower's credit profile. Operating cost, term premium, and business strategy premium cannot be revised for 3 years on floating-rate loans (unless MFL is in Base Layer, which gets an exemption).

**Q3:** Is MFL required to link its floating-rate personal loans to the RBI repo rate?
- **Answer:** No. External benchmark linkage is mandatory only for banks (for floating-rate retail and MSME loans). For NBFCs, it is discretionary. MFL can use its own internal benchmark.
