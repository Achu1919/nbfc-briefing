# 🎧 NBFC Daily Watch — Podcast Script — Thursday, 3 Sep 2026

Good morning Achu! This is your NBFC Daily Watch for Thursday, September 3rd — your morning briefing on everything that moved in India's lending world yesterday.

We open with three big ones.

First — a genuinely historic day on the ratings front. Japan's JCR upgraded India's sovereign rating to A-minus from BBB-plus, the first "A" category rating for India in over 35 years, with a stable outlook. And here's what matters for our world: JCR explicitly cited the improvement in asset quality and capital adequacy of the non-banking financial sector, along with banks, as a key reason. This strengthens India's external funding optics at exactly the time NBFCs are tapping global and local bond markets aggressively. The upgrade came on the same day a market rout hit financial stocks, so the headline reaction was muted — but the funding-cost story is quietly very positive.

Second — markets had their third straight losing session, but institutions bought the dip. The Sensex closed down 374 points at 76,570, and the Nifty fell 141 points to 23,914, slipping below the psychological 24,000 mark after touching an intraday low of 23,787. The trigger: fresh US-Iran strikes, crude oil near 95 dollars a barrel, and a global bond sell-off that pushed India's ten-year yield towards 7 percent. Nifty Financial Services fell 0.84 percent. But here's the twist: FIIs net bought 6,688 crore and DIIs bought another 2,813 crore on the day — combined institutional buying of nearly 9,500 crore. The sell-off is sentiment and oil, not institutional exit. Worth remembering next time the tape feels scary.

Third — Capri Global Capital closed its debut 300-million-dollar foreign bond, a 7.55 percent senior secured note due 2029, oversubscribed 2.3 times with an order book over 700 million dollars. Managed by Barclays, Citi, Deutsche Bank, Emirates NBD and UBS, with ratings of BB-minus and Ba3. What this tells us: mid-size NBFCs can still raise dollars at workable spreads — a strong signal for the whole sector's funding math.

Now the quick-fire round.

L&T Finance went all-in on gold: it plans 500 new gold-loan branches a year, taking its network from 343 today to 2,500 to 3,000 branches over five years, after its gold book grew 182 percent to 3,829 crore. Direct competition for every gold lender, including Muthoot.

Jefferies named its four NBFC picks to outperform: Bajaj Finance, top pick, target 1,280; Aditya Birla Capital at 475; Cholamandalam at 1,860; and Shriram Finance at 1,210 — with 23 to 28 percent EPS growth expectations over three years, driven by earnings upgrades, not multiple expansion.

Ujjivan Small Finance Bank's CEO Sanjeev Nautiyal stepped down citing health, with Carol Furtado approved by the RBI as interim CEO — the bank's one-lakh-crore loan book plan for FY30 now hinges on the successor.

Microfinance turned a corner: the MFIN Micrometer report showed portfolio growth of 1.1 percent quarter-on-quarter, disbursements up 43 percent, and asset quality sharply improved — the headline portfolio contraction is mostly banks reclassifying micro-loans as retail credit.

Shadow lenders are lining up at least 10,000 crore rupees of rupee bonds, starting as early as next week, with PFC leading — everyone racing to lock in rates before they rise.

And gold loans officially overtook personal loans as India's second-largest retail asset class, with Motilal Oswal projecting the market to cross 30 lakh crore rupees by FY28 — though it expects banks and diversified NBFCs to gain share while pure-play gold lenders face margin pressure.

Today's deep dive is MSME and commercial lending. The picture is a fascinating divergence: the manufacturing PMI slid to a five-year low of 52.8 in August, with factory employment falling for the first time in 30 months — yet bank credit to industry jumped 20 percent in July. NBFCs remain the fastest-growing MSME lenders, and SIDBI's latest MSME Pulse shows credit shifting from companies to individual entrepreneurs. The question for October results: does that demand convert into NPAs, or into earnings upgrades? Jefferies is betting on the latter.

That's your morning briefing. Full details are in your written report. Have a great day!
