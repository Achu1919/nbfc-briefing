### 1. Revolving Credit: DG Murmu Meets FIDC; RBI May Soften Stance
**Classification:** [AMENDMENT / CLARIFICATION] — Still in draft stage, but evolving fast

**What:** RBI Deputy Governor S.C. Murmu met FIDC and NBFC CEOs on 3 September 2026 to discuss the Draft Revolving Credit Ban (Aug 6, 2026). RBI received 350+ industry responses and is now leaning toward a **partial restriction** rather than the originally proposed blanket ban.

**Key developments from the Sep 3 meeting:**
- RBI initially assumed the ban would affect only 3–4 NBFCs. Industry feedback showed **"a significantly larger section"** would be impacted.
- RBI is now open to considering exemptions or a more targeted prohibition.
- Supply chain financing (SCF) has emerged as a key area where NBFCs seek a carve-out — loans are monitored via GST data and disbursed directly to suppliers.
- Industry estimates suggest products representing **"substantial credit outstanding"** may be affected.
- All industry suggestions will be consolidated through CII and sent to RBI.
- **Final guidelines expected in ~2 months** (approximately November 2026).

**Source:** ETBFSI, Business Standard, Livemint (Sep 3, 2026); RBI Draft Circular RBI/2026-27/__ DOR.STR.REC.__/21-07-001/2026-27 (Aug 6, 2026)

**In Plain English:** RBI wanted to ban all revolving credit from NBFCs (flexi-loans, credit lines, working capital where you can re-draw after repaying). After 350+ industry responses saying "this is way broader than you think," RBI is now considering a softer approach — maybe only restricting the most problematic revolving products, not everything.

**Impact on NBFCs:**
- The blanket ban is now less likely — good news for NBFCs offering flexi-loans, supply chain finance, and invoice discounting.
- However, some restrictions are still coming — NBFCs should prepare for tighter rules on revolving products.
- Products with limited redraw (like certain working capital facilities) may get exemptions.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** MFL's gold loan products are bullet-repayment term loans — **not** revolving credit. **Low direct impact.** However, if MFL has any MSME or microfinance working capital products with re-draw features, those may be affected. Worth monitoring the final definition.

- Lender **returns may reduce** and **borrower costs may rise**.

### 3. ⏰ DEADLINE IN 6 DAYS: Draft Interest Rate Directions — Comments Due Sep 11
**Classification:** [NEW_REG] — Draft stage, comment deadline imminent

**What:** RBI released the Draft "Reserve Bank of India (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026" on August 12, 2026. The **public comment deadline is September 11, 2026** — just 6 days away.

**Key provisions of the draft:**
- **Harmonised framework** for ALL Regulated Entities (banks, NBFCs, HFCs, AIFIs, cooperatives).
- Replaces 6 separate RE-specific circulars with one unified framework.
- **Effective date:** April 1, 2027.
- **Interest at monthly rests** mandated for all REs (except agricultural).
- **Daily reducing balance** basis for interest computation.
- **APR ceiling** required on microfinance loans and small-value personal loans (≤ ₹50,000).
- Interest not to exceed principal for short-term agricultural loans to small/marginal farmers.
- **Fixed-rate loans:** Must reference internal or external benchmark + risk spread. Cannot price below benchmark.
- **Floating-rate loans:** Must link to external or internal benchmark. Spread can be reset only once every 3 years (at minimum).
- **Board-approved policy** on interest rates, reviewed annually.
- **No usurious pricing** — supervisory scrutiny applies.

**Source:** RBI Press Release 2026-27/355 (Aug 12, 2026); Draft Directions RBI/2026-27/__ DOR.CRE.REC.__/13.03.000/2026-27

**In Plain English:** RBI wants to standardise how ALL lenders set interest rates. Right now, banks follow one set of rules and NBFCs follow different (looser) rules. The draft says: everyone follows the same principles. For NBFCs, the big change is they may need to link loan rates to benchmarks and lock spreads for 3 years — something banks already do.

**Impact on NBFCs:**
- **HIGH IMPACT** — This would be the most significant change to NBFC interest rate pricing in years.
- NBFCs currently have wide discretion in pricing. The draft proposes benchmark-linked pricing with spread lock-in.
- Spread reset only once every 3 years could squeeze margins in a rising rate environment.
- APR ceiling on microfinance and small-value personal loans could cap revenue.
- Gold loan rates would need to be benchmarked — currently most gold loan NBFCs price based on cost of funds + margin.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** **HIGH RELEVANCE.** MFL should:
1. Submit comments before Sep 11 if not already done.
2. Model the impact of benchmark-linked pricing on gold loan and microfinance yields.
3. Assess the 3-year spread lock-in impact on NIM under different rate scenarios.
4. Prepare for APR ceiling impact on small-ticket personal loans.

**Classification:** [NEW_REG] — Already issued, compliance clock ticking

**What:** RBI issued comprehensive Recovery Agent Conduct Directions on August 6, 2026, effective January 1, 2027. These replace the earlier, lighter-touch recovery agent guidelines.

**Key requirements:**
- **IIBF certification mandatory** for all recovery agents (existing agents have 1 year to comply).
- **Board-approved recovery policy** covering escalation matrix, code of conduct, compensation to borrowers for wrongful recovery.
- **Recovery hours restricted** to 08:00–19:00.
- **Call recording** for at least 6 months.
- **Advance notice** to borrower before any recovery agent contact.
- **List of recovery agencies** must be published on NBFC's website.
- **Technology-based recovery** (e.g., device locking) must follow graduated restrictions with advance notice.

**Source:** RBI/2026-27/230 DOR.MCS.REC.No.199/01-01-039/2026-27 (Aug 6, 2026)

## 📚 Teaching Session: Interest Rate Regulation for NBFCs — The New Harmonised Framework

### What is it?

RBI is proposing to **unify interest rate rules across all lenders** — banks, NBFCs, cooperatives, and financial institutions — through the Draft Interest Rates on Loans and Advances Directions, 2026. This is a **paradigm shift** for NBFCs, which historically had wide pricing freedom.

### Why Now?

**The background:**
- Banks have been on External Benchmark Linked Lending (EBLR) since October 2019 — repo rate-linked.
- NBFCs have no benchmark-linking mandate — they price based on cost of funds + margin.
- RBI's August 5, 2025 Monetary Policy announced the intent to "rationalise the regulatory framework on interest rates for all REs."
- The draft was released on August 12, 2026.

**The logic:** When banks and NBFCs lend to the same borrower, the pricing rules should be comparable. Different rules create regulatory arbitrage.

### The Technical Details

| Aspect | Current (NBFCs) | Proposed (Draft) |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| Benchmark | No mandate | Must use internal or external benchmark |
| Spread | Discretionary | Risk-based, documented in policy |
| Spread Reset | No restriction | Minimum 3-year lock-in for floating loans |
| Interest Rests | Monthly (practice) | Monthly (mandated) |
| Day Count | Varies | Actual/Actual (mandated) |
| Reducing Balance | Varies | Daily reducing balance (mandated) |
| APR Disclosure | KFS for microfinance | APR ceiling on microfinance + small loans (≤₹50K) |
| Board Policy | Not required for pricing | Mandatory annual board-approved policy |
| Floating Rate | No specific rules | Must link to benchmark; spread reset only every 3 years |
| Fixed Rate | No specific rules | Must reference benchmark; cannot price below benchmark |

### Why It Matters

1. **For NBFCs:** This is the biggest pricing regulation change in decades. NBFCs that currently charge, say, 18% based on pure discretion would need to justify the rate through a benchmark + documented spread.

2. **For Gold Loan NBFCs:** Gold loan rates are typically priced at cost of funds + margin. Under the new framework, the benchmark could be a T-bill yield or repo rate, with a fixed spread. This could **compress margins** if the spread is locked for 3 years and funding costs change.

3. **For MFI Lending:** The APR ceiling on microfinance loans means there's a hard cap on total charges — interest + processing + insurance + any other fees. This needs careful modelling.

### Key Numbers & Thresholds

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Effective Date | April 1, 2027 |
| Comment Deadline | September 11, 2026 |
| Spread Lock-in (Floating) | Minimum 3 years |
| APR Ceiling | On microfinance loans and personal loans ≤ ₹50,000 |
| Interest Rests | Monthly (except agricultural) |
| Day Count Convention | Actual/Actual |
| Reducing Balance | Daily |
| Board Policy Review | At least annually |

### The Gold Loan Angle

For gold loan NBFCs specifically:
- Gold loans are typically **short-tenor** (bullet repayment, 6–12 months).
- Most are **fixed-rate** — borrower pays a fixed interest and gets gold back on maturity.
- Under the draft, fixed-rate loans must reference an **internal or external benchmark + risk spread**.
- The **spread cannot be below the benchmark** — this prevents predatory pricing below cost.
- For floating-rate gold loans (if any), the spread can only be reset every 3 years.
- **APR ceiling** applies to gold loans where the principal ≤ ₹50,000 (small-ticket gold loans).

**Practical question:** If MFL's gold loan rate is, say, 12% p.a., and the benchmark (say, 1-year T-bill) is 6.5%, the spread would be 5.5%. If rates rise and T-bill goes to 8%, MFL can't change the spread for 3 years — but the total rate would go to 13.5%. Good for MFL in a rising rate scenario. Bad if rates fall and T-bill drops to 4% — rate goes to 9.5% but MFL can't reduce the spread for 3 years.

### Quiz Yourself

**Q1:** Under the draft Interest Rate Directions, an NBFC can reset the spread on a floating-rate loan:
- (a) Anytime, at its discretion
- (b) Only once every 3 years
- (c) Only once every 5 years
- (d) Only when RBI changes the repo rate

**Answer:** (b) Only once every 3 years. The draft mandates a minimum 3-year lock-in on spread resets for floating-rate loans.

**Q2:** The draft proposes an APR ceiling on:
- (a) All personal loans
- (b) Microfinance loans and personal loans ≤ ₹50,000
- (c) Gold loans only
- (d) All MSME loans

**Answer:** (b) Microfinance loans and personal loans where principal ≤ ₹50,000 (small-value loans).

**Q3:** An NBFC currently prices its gold loan portfolio at a flat 14% without any benchmark reference. Under the draft directions, this would:
- (a) Be perfectly compliant — NBFCs have pricing freedom
- (b) Need to be restructured to reference a benchmark + documented spread
- (c) Only be affected if MFL is an NBFC-UL
- (d) Only apply from April 2028

**Answer:** (b) All REs, including NBFCs, would need to price loans with reference to an internal or external benchmark plus a documented risk-based spread.
