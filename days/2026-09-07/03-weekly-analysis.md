## 🏛️ Regulatory & Policy Watch

**RBI Deputy Governor's 5-point roadmap (CII NBFC & HFC Summit, Sep 3).** S.C. Murmu set out five supervisory priorities — governance and culture; liquidity management; asset quality and credit risk; conduct and customer protection; digital transformation and cyber resilience — while explicitly blessing sector growth: "NBFCs and HFCs are no longer on the sidelines." He noted NBFC credit has risen to 16.7% of GDP from 15.9% a year earlier and is ~27% of bank credit (from 26%). Under the umbrella: phased LCR requirements, enhanced risk-management and internal-audit requirements, and fraud-risk management directions.

**What it means:** (1) Regulation is enabling, not constricting — expect more consolidation-friendly treatment of scale players; (2) compliance costs rise as a % of opex, especially cyber (NBFCs were brought into RBI's consolidated cybersecurity framework in mid-2026 with board-level accountability — boards must now own technology risk); (3) liquidity norms (LCR) will keep pushing NBFCs toward longer-duration, diversified liabilities.

**SEBI market-structure moves.** The Closing Auction Session (CAS) was implemented on weekly expiry day (Sep 3) — markets wobbled on it (Sensex -417 pts Thursday) — and SEBI indicated it may review derivative settlement-price methodology. Expect continued near-term volatility on expiry days.

**Revolving credit draft norms (in feedback stage).** RBI is consulting NBFCs on draft guidelines for revolving credit facilities; CRISIL's view (August): disruption manageable, NBFC loan growth should hold in FY27. Watch the final draft for unsecured/BNPL implications.

**Microfinance conduct tightening (context).** MFIN's revised norms (June 2026) — ₹2 lakh borrower-debt cap, max 3 MFI lenders per borrower, tighter NPA rules — are now the operating framework as the sector recovers.

**Muthoot Fincorp-specific:** Your company's ₹3,000 crore fresh-issue IPO (DRHP filed mid-August, ≥35% retail) is with SEBI; the Muthoot Finance–Muthoot Money merger consolidates the #1 player's cost base right as you price your equity — competitive positioning, yield discipline and gold-price volatility will be the key valuation watch-items. Debt track record remains strong (public NCDs rated Crisil AA-/Positive, BWR AA/Stable earlier this year).

## 💡 Analyst's Take

**The signal vs the noise.** The noise this week was gold and the global bond selloff — both are real but both are *levels*, not *direction*. The signal is structural: RBI's summit messaging effectively declared NBFCs systemically central (16.7% of GDP and rising, 27% of bank credit), while the regulator's five pillars — governance, liquidity, asset quality, conduct, cyber — map exactly onto the balance-sheet upgrades scale players have been making. The Muthoot–Muthoot Money merger and the ferocious retail demand for NBFC NCDs (Indel 704%, Kosamattam closed early, Paisalo pre-closed) are the same story from two angles: the market is rewarding size, funding depth and clean asset books. Expect more mergers, more IPOs (Muthoot Fincorp's ₹3,000 cr DRHP is in the queue) and more liability-franchise building over the next 12 months. The trend accelerating is **consolidation into funded scale**; what's decelerating is volume-at-any-yield lending — the Manappuram-at-33%-cheaper pricing strategy is the exception that eventually forces the whole gold segment to re-price risk.

**What Muthoot Fincorp leadership should watch.** (1) **Gold's Friday crash is a gift and a warning**: it tests the <60% LTV discipline in real time and will shape SEBI's and investors' read of the IPO; a calm, well-communicated LTV/auction framework will be a differentiator. (2) **Lock in funding now**: FCNR(B)-fuelled liquidity is suppressing spreads while global yields scream otherwise — the India–US spread at 216 bps is a multi-year low, and the window of cheap wholesale money is narrower than it feels; extend tenors before the Fed acts. (3) **The pricing war is the swing variable for Q2 margins** — Muthoot Finance's 15% FY27 guidance revision post-Q2 will set the tone for the whole gold complex. (4) **Cyber and governance are now board-level deliverables**, not IT projects — budget accordingly, because the RBI roadmap made it explicit this week.

**Contrarian observation.** The market is treating the gold-loan boom as priced-in momentum — but the substitution effect is underrated: with unsecured/microfinance credit still constrained (banks retreating, MFIN caps at ₹2 lakh per borrower), gold-secured lending is absorbing demand that has nowhere else to go. That makes gold-loan growth *more durable* than the price-chart gyrations suggest — and it means a gold correction hits volumes less and yields more. The second contrarian point: everyone is scared of the Fed hike, but India's domestic liquidity cushion (4.4-year surplus, $127 bn FCNR) and RBI's demonstrated willingness to smooth rates mean NBFC funding costs may decouple from global yields for a quarter or two. Borrowers who front-load liabilities win; borrowers who wait for "certainty" pay for it.
