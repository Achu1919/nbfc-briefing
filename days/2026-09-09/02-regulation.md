# 📚 Teaching Session: Interest Rate Harmonisation Framework — The Draft That Changes Everything

## What Is It?

RBI has proposed a single, harmonised framework for how all regulated entities (REs) — commercial banks, NBFCs, HFCs, co-operative banks, AIFIs — determine interest rates on loans. This replaces a patchwork of entity-specific directions with one unified rulebook.

**Official name:** Draft Reserve Bank of India (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026

**Status:** Draft issued August 12, 2026. Comments open until September 11, 2026. Final directions to follow separately for each RE category.

**Reference:** RBI Press Release No. 63355 (Aug 12, 2026)

---

## Why Is RBI Doing This?

Currently, banks use external benchmarks (repo-linked), MCLR, Base Rate, or BPLR. NBFCs set their own benchmarks or use internal ones. There's no common language for comparing loan costs across lenders. The new framework creates:

1. **Transparency** — a common pricing language for borrowers
2. **Fairness** — caps on opaque charges for small-ticket loans
3. **Level playing field** — same rules for banks and NBFCs
4. **Faster rate transmission** — benchmark resets every 3 months maximum

---

## The Technical Details

### Core Architecture: Benchmark + Spread

Every loan must be priced as:

**Loan Rate = Applicable Benchmark + Spread**

| Component | What It Is | Rules |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| **Benchmark** | The base reference rate | Published monthly; banks use external benchmarks, NBFCs build their own |
| **Spread** | Risk premium + operating costs + margin | Must be justified by Board-approved policy |
| **Credit Risk Premium** | Part of spread — can change | Only if borrower's credit profile materially changes, after review |
| **Non-Credit-Risk Spread** | Operating costs, margin, other costs | **Locked for minimum 3 years** once fixed |

### The 3-Year Spread Lock (Paragraph 10 of draft)

This is the most debated provision. Once an NBFC sets the non-credit-risk component of its spread:

- It **cannot be changed** for 3 years from the date of sanction
- Exception: if the benchmark itself changes (monthly resets), the total rate moves — but the spread portion stays fixed
- This prevents REs from gradually widening spreads after loan origination

### Benchmark Requirements

| RE Type | Benchmark Requirement |
|---------|----------------------|
| Banks (large) | Must publish MCLR replacement benchmark monthly |
| Banks (small SFB/LAB) | Same as above |
| **NBFCs** | Must build benchmark from scratch; publish methodology on digital interfaces or at branches |
| **HFCs** | Same as NBFCs |
| Co-op banks | Same as NBFCs |
| AIFIs | Same as NBFCs |

**NBFC-specific note:** NBFCs are NOT required to link to an external benchmark (unlike banks for personal/MSME floating loans). They build their own internal benchmark following the prescribed methodology.

### Floating Rate Reset

- Maximum reset frequency: **3 months** for floating-rate loans
- This ensures faster transmission of rate changes to borrowers
- Base Layer NBFCs are exempt from this requirement

### APR Ceiling on Small Loans

For **personal loans and microfinance loans up to ₹50,000:**

- REs must set a **Board-approved ceiling on the Annual Percentage Rate (APR)**
- APR includes interest + all fees and charges
- The ceiling must not be "usurious" (RBI has not set a specific number)
- This replaces price-based risk management with underwriting-based risk management in this segment

**Why it matters:** In sub-₹50K lending, lenders currently use very high rates to offset risk across a broad group. The APR ceiling forces lenders to assess repayment ability more carefully rather than rely on high pricing.

### Daily Interest Calculation

- Interest must be calculated **daily** on unpaid principal
- Posted **monthly** at minimum
- Method: **Reducing balance, actual/actual day count**

### Migration Path

| Period | Obligation |
|--------|-----------|
| **Now to Mar 2027** | Design and build — benchmark computation, Board policy, systems, documentation |
| **Apr 1, 2027** | Go live — all new lending on new framework; dual-running begins |
| **Apr 2027 to Mar 2029** | Migration — existing benchmark-linked loans migrated with borrower consent |
| **Apr 1, 2029** | Full migration deadline |

Migration requirements:
- Requires **borrower consent** (cannot be unilateral)
- Cannot increase the borrower's rate
- No charge for migration

## Why It Matters for All NBFCs

### Pricing Transparency
Borrowers will finally be able to compare a ₹50,000 MFI loan from an NBFC with a bank's personal loan on an apples-to-apples basis. APR disclosure makes this possible.

### Spread Lock Impact on Profitability
The 3-year lock on non-credit-risk spreads removes a key tool for margin management. If operating costs rise (rent, salaries, compliance), the NBFC absorbs them for 3 years per loan. This requires more careful initial pricing.

### Sub-₹50K Segment Restructuring
The APR ceiling forces a fundamental rethink of small-ticket unsecured lending. Lenders with high-risk, high-return models in this segment (many fintech NBFCs) will need to either:
1. Tighten underwriting (reject more borrowers)
2. Accept lower margins
3. Exit sub-₹50K segment entirely

### Compliance Infrastructure
NBFCs must build monthly benchmark publication, maintain spread documentation, set up APR calculation systems, and manage dual-framework operations for 2 years.

## Muthoot Fincorp Context — HIGH IMPACT

### Gold Loans (Core Book)

Gold loans are typically fixed-rate with bullet or EMI repayment. Under the new framework:

1. **Benchmark requirement:** MFL must publish a benchmark and build a methodology — even for fixed-rate gold loans. The benchmark serves as the floor.
2. **APR disclosure:** All gold loan charges (processing, valuation, documentation) must be included in APR. This creates transparency on the true cost of a gold loan vs. a bank's secured loan.
3. **Sub-₹50K gold loans:** These are a significant portion of MFL's gold loan book. The APR ceiling applies to these — potentially capping the effective yield if MFL charges high processing fees on small loans.
4. **3-year spread lock:** For fixed-rate gold loans with 12-month tenure, this is less relevant (loan retires before lock expires). But for any revolving or renewal-based products, it matters.

### MFI Book (Microfinance)

**This is where the impact is highest:**

- The APR ceiling directly targets MFI lending up to ₹50,000
- Current MFI pricing (cost of funds + risk premium + margin) must be restructured to fit within a Board-approved ceiling
- If MFL's MFI loans are priced at 22-26% (industry average), the APR ceiling may force a reduction
- Combined with the Hinduja Leyland enforcement (Sep 4) for MFI pricing norms, RBI is clearly signalling that MFI pricing is under close scrutiny

### FP&A Implications

| Area | Impact |
|------|--------|
| **Revenue** | Potential yield compression on sub-₹50K MFI loans if APR ceiling binds |
| **Systems** | Investment in benchmark computation, APR calculation, spread tracking |
| **Disclosures** | New APR disclosures in KFS and sanction letters from Apr 2027 |
| **Dual-running costs** | Two pricing frameworks operational Apr 2027–Mar 2029 |
| **Capital** | Any yield compression may reduce profitability and internally-generated capital |

## What Other NBFCs Are Saying

Industry bodies (FIDC, UFF/DLAI, FACE) have raised concerns:

1. **APR ceiling may reduce credit access** — thin-file, new-to-credit borrowers may be denied if lenders can't price for risk
2. **3-year lock is too rigid** — NBFCs have shorter asset tenors than banks; 3 years may not match their operating model
3. **NBFC benchmark-building is complex** — unlike banks that have MCLR infrastructure, NBFCs must build from scratch
4. **Sub-₹50K segment hit hardest** — many fintech NBFCs specialise in this segment; the ceiling could force exits

## Quiz Yourself

1. **Q:** Under the draft, can an NBFC change the non-credit-risk component of its spread 18 months after sanction?
   **A:** No. The non-credit-risk spread is locked for 3 years. Only the credit-risk premium component can change if the borrower's credit profile materially changes.

2. **Q:** Is an NBFC required to link its loan pricing to the RBI repo rate (external benchmark)?
   **A:** No. Unlike banks (which must use external benchmarks for floating-rate personal and MSME loans), NBFCs build their own internal benchmark following the prescribed methodology.

3. **Q:** A ₹35,000 microfinance loan — does the APR ceiling apply?
   **A:** Yes. The APR ceiling applies to all personal loans and microfinance loans up to ₹50,000. The Board must set a ceiling that is not "usurious."
