# NBFC Regulatory Daily Report — 10 September 2026

## Live Items

### 1. Interest Rate Directions — Comment Deadline Tomorrow (Sep 11)

**What:** RBI's Draft Interest Rates on Loans and Advances Directions, 2026 (released Aug 12) closes for public comments tomorrow, September 11.

**Plain English:** RBI wants one rulebook for how every lending entity in India prices loans. Banks, NBFCs, HFCs, cooperatives — same benchmark-plus-spread framework.

**Key provisions for NBFCs:**
- All floating rate loans priced off a benchmark (internal or external) plus a spread
- Loan cannot be priced below the benchmark
- Spread components: credit risk premium (CRP), operating cost, term premium, business strategy premium
- CRP can change with borrower profile; all other spread components locked for 3 years on floating rate loans
- BL NBFCs exempt from 3-year lock and 3-month reset requirement — ML/UL NBFCs are not
- APR ceiling mandated for microfinance and small-value personal loans (under ₹50,000) — board-determined, no quantitative threshold specified
- External benchmark optional for NBFCs; mandatory for banks on personal and MSME loans
- Effective date: April 1, 2027

**Impact on MFL:** As ML-layer, the 3-year spread lock applies to MFL's floating rate book. Limits future repricing flexibility. APR ceiling for MFI loans requires board policy action. External benchmark remains optional.

**Source:** RBI Press Release PRID 63355, Aug 12, 2026; Draft Directions on RBI website

## Teaching Session

### Interest Rate Determination Under the Draft Directions

The draft directions introduce a unified pricing framework. Every loan rate = benchmark + spread. The benchmark can be external (repo rate, T-bill yields, SORR) or internal (for NBFCs, derived from marginal cost of funds).

The spread is the new control point. It has four components: credit risk premium, operating cost, term premium, and business strategy premium. The CRP is mandatory and must be positive — it represents the borrower-specific risk. It can only change when the borrower's credit profile changes.

The other three components, once set, are frozen for three years on floating rate loans for ML/UL entities. You can lower them for customer retention, but you can't raise them. This means an NBFC that sets a tight spread today can't widen it later even if funding costs rise or market conditions change.

BL NBFCs get a carve-out: no mandatory 3-month reset, no 3-year spread lock. This is because BL entities are smaller and the regulatory burden is lighter under SBR.

The APR ceiling for microfinance and small-value loans is the other major shift. RBI requires each RE to set a board-approved maximum APR. No number is prescribed, but the direction is clear: RBI wants a cap, and it wants it to be defensible at the board level.

**Quiz yourself:**
1. Under the draft, can an NBFC price a floating rate loan below its internal benchmark? (No — para 8/10 prohibit this)
2. Which spread component can change without waiting three years? (CRP — if the borrower's credit profile changes)
3. Are BL NBFCs exempt from the 3-year spread lock? (Yes — the draft exempts BL NBFCs from this requirement)
