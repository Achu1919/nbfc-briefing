### 1. [NEW_REG — BANKS; WATCH FOR NBFCs] Draft KYC Amendment — Money-Mule Debit-Hold SOP

- **What:** RBI has put out draft KYC Amendment Directions creating a time-bound SOP for temporary debit holds on suspected money-mule accounts and cyber-fraud proceeds.
- **Issuer:** RBI (Department of Regulation – AML)
- **Date:** Draft issued 11 September 2026. Comments due **2 October 2026**. SOP effective **1 April 2027** (or earlier at the bank’s option).
- **Reference:** Press Release 2026-2027/1109; Draft DOR.AML.REC.No.__/14-01-001/2026-27; content Id=5170. Triggered by Supreme Court order dated **4 August 2026**.
- **Source:** [RBI Press Release prid=63587](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63587) | [Draft Directions](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/bs_viewcontent.aspx?Id=5170)

**In Plain English:** The Supreme Court told RBI to give banks a standard playbook for freezing money that looks like it came from cyber fraud / mule accounts. The draft SOP says: if a bank’s monitoring system flags a transaction of **₹1,000 or more** as suspected mule proceeds, the bank must freeze the amount (or, only as a last resort, the whole account) **immediately**, tell the customer, give 20 days to explain, decide within 10 days of the explanation (or 30 days if none comes), and if still suspicious, report to police via NCRP-CFCFRMS. Maximum freeze without a law-enforcement order: **60 days**. Fail to file an STR on a confirmed mule account = deemed KYC non-compliance.

**Scope (important):** This draft is issued under **Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949**. It applies to Commercial Banks (including SFBs, Payments Banks, RRBs, LABs) and Urban Cooperative Banks. **It does not currently apply to NBFCs.** Final bank-wise directions will be issued after comments.

**Impact on NBFCs:** Indirect, but real.
- Gold-loan and digital-lending disbursements go to the **borrower’s bank account**. A debit hold on that account can stall disbursement confirmation, EMI collection, or refund of auction surplus.
- If MFL’s collection/disbursement bank flags a borrower as a mule, the loan cash-flow breaks even though MFL did nothing wrong.
- RBI often mirrors bank KYC conduct into the NBFC KYC Directions later. Treat this as a **preview**, not a current obligation.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** Low direct compliance load today. Ops/collections should still know the 60-day freeze clock exists at banks, because it will show up as “payment bounced / account frozen” on gold-loan and MFI accounts. Compliance can watch whether an NBFC-parallel draft appears after 2 October. No Board policy change required until RBI extends this to NBFCs.

**Comments:** Connect 2 Regulate, or email `amldorfeedback1@rbi.org.in` with subject “Feedback on Draft Reserve Bank of India (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026”, by **2 October 2026**.

## 🎓 Daily Teaching Session — Saturday: Gold-Loan LTV, Valuation & Conduct

### Gold Loan LTV, Bullet Repayment and Auction Conduct

**What is it?**

Gold loans look simple: customer pledges jewellery, you lend a percentage of its value, they repay, you return the gold. RBI treats them as a **special product** because (a) the collateral is liquid and price-volatile, (b) bullet loans can hide interest until maturity, and (c) auction of family jewellery is a conduct flashpoint. The rules sit in three places, not one circular:

1. **RBI (NBFC – Credit Facilities) Directions, 2025** — Chapter IV, Lending against Gold and Silver Collateral. Ref: RBI/DOR/2025-26/347, DOR.CRE.REC.No.266/07-01-008/2025-26, updated **15 July 2026**.
2. **RBI (NBFC – Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025** — Chapter III-G, conduct for gold/silver collateral (assaying, documents, auction, unclaimed gold). Ref: RBI/DOR/2025-26/362, updated **1 July 2026**.
3. **RBI (Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral) Directions, 2025** — the all-RE harmonised set issued **6 June 2025** (RBI/2025-26/47, DOR.CRE.REC.26/21.01.023/2025-26), to be complied with by **1 April 2026**.

Applies to **all layers** (BL / ML / UL) that do gold or silver collateral lending. MFL is ML.

---

**The Technical Details**

### 1. What you may lend against — and what you may not

Eligible collateral = jewellery, ornaments or coins of gold or silver. **Not** primary gold/silver (bars, biscuits, dore), **not** gold ETFs / gold MFs, **not** a loan whose purpose is to *buy* gold.

Board-approved credit policy must set:
- single-borrower and aggregate portfolio caps for gold/silver loans
- maximum LTV and **what happens if LTV is breached mid-tenure**
- valuation standards and purity standards
- PSL documentation if you claim priority-sector tagging

### 2. How you value the gold (this drives LTV)

Take the **lower of**:
- (a) average closing price of that purity over the **preceding 30 days**, or
- (b) closing price on the **preceding day**

Source: **IBJA** or a **SEBI-regulated commodity exchange**. You cannot pick yesterday’s spike if the 30-day average is lower.

Assaying (Responsible Business Conduct):
- same procedure in **every branch** — no “this branch uses a different XRF”
- borrower **present** at assaying
- certificate / e-certificate in duplicate: caratage, gross weight, net weight, stone/lac deductions, defects, image, value
- methodology for net weight and price **must be on the website**

### 3. LTV — the number FP&A actually lives with

From **1 April 2026** the old flat 75% cap was replaced by a **tiered consumption-loan LTV** (harmonised gold-collateral directions):

| Total consumption gold loan per borrower | Maximum LTV |
|---|---|
| ≤ ₹2.5 lakh | **85%** |
| > ₹2.5 lakh and ≤ ₹5 lakh | **80%** |
| > ₹5 lakh | **75%** |

Three traps that still catch gold-loan NBFCs:

1. **Continuous LTV.** LTV is not a “day-1” test. It must hold through the life of the loan. Gold price down 12% in a quarter can put an 85% loan over the cap without a single rupee of extra disbursement.
2. **Bullet loans use amount *repayable at maturity*.** LTV = (principal + interest that will have accrued by due date) / gold value. Accrued interest eats LTV headroom every day.
3. **Renewals / top-ups.** Only on **standard** accounts; for bullets, only **after accrued interest is paid**; **not permitted** if the loan would breach LTV on the maturity date. A gold-price downcycle therefore converts “evergreen renewals” into NPAs.

Risk weights (capital, not LTV):

| LTV | Indicative risk weight | Standard provision (ML) |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 50% | 35% | 0.40% |
| 50–75% | 50% | 0.40% |
| Gold loans with LTV > 75% | higher RW; **1% standard provision** (IRAC ML) | 1.00% |
| > 85% | **not permitted** | — |

NPA is still **90 days** overdue, same as every other NBFC loan. Gold does not buy you a longer DPD clock. Upgradation still needs **entire arrears** cleared on all facilities of that borrower (Muthoot MCred was fined ₹3.10 lakh in August for upgrading without full arrears — that lesson applies to gold too).

### 4. Bullet vs EMI

| Ticket | Structure |
|---|---|
| ≤ ₹2.5 lakh | Bullet permitted; typical max tenure **12 months**; interest still to be serviced at least quarterly under the gold-loan operating framework |
| > ₹2.5 lakh | **EMI only** — no bullet |

Foreclosure: 7-day cooling-off from sanction; after that, foreclosure charges only if disclosed (KFS / agreement). Microfinance (if any) cannot levy prepayment charges at all.

### 5. Auction and release (conduct — this is where RBI inspects branches)

Loan agreement **and** KFS must describe: collateral, value, when auction is triggered, notice period, how surplus is refunded, assaying/auction charges.

After full repayment, pledged gold is released on the timelines in the Responsible Business Conduct Directions. Surplus from auction goes back to the borrower. Loss, purity discrepancy, or missing packets: record it, tell the borrower/legal heir, pay compensation per Board SOP.

Unclaimed gold after auction/repayment has its own tracking — do not quietly absorb it into inventory.

---

**Why It Matters**

For a gold-loan ML NBFC, LTV is simultaneously:
- a **product** constraint (how much you can lend),
- a **capital** constraint (risk weights and the 12% gold-loan Tier I add-on),
- an **earnings** constraint (1% standard provision above 75% LTV; NPA if you cannot renew a breached bullet),
- a **conduct** constraint (wrong assaying / bad auction = penalty + reputational damage).

FP&A feels it in three lines of the P&L/balance sheet: interest income on bullets (accrual vs cash), standard-asset provisioning on the >75% LTV bucket, and GNPA if renewals freeze in a price correction.

**Recent Changes**

- **1 April 2026:** Harmonised gold/silver collateral directions went live — tiered LTV, continuous monitoring, bullet LTV on amount repayable at maturity, tighter renewal rules.
- **15 July 2026:** Credit Facilities Directions updated (still the live Chapter IV).
- **1 January 2027:** Recovery-agent SOP applies to telephonic gold-loan follow-up, even though the ultimate recovery tool is auction, not door-step recovery.
- **No new gold-loan circular in the last 24 hours.**

Previously (pre-Apr 2026): a flat **75% LTV at disbursal** was the working rule most gold NBFCs ran. Now: higher LTV on small tickets, but you **cannot look away after day 1**.

---

**Muthoot Fincorp Context**

MFL’s core book is gold. Practical FP&A checklist:

1. **Split the book** by LTV bucket (≤50 / 50–75 / 75–85) and by bullet vs EMI. The 75–85% bucket costs **1% standard provision** and more capital. A 200 bps mix shift into that bucket is a P&L event, not a footnote.
2. **Bullet LTV at maturity, not at disbursal.** Build a monthly “accrued-interest eat” report: how many bullets will breach LTV at due date if gold is unchanged / −5% / −10%.
3. **Renewal freeze = NPA factory.** If gold corrects, you cannot roll a breached bullet. Model GNPA under a 10% gold-price shock; do not assume historical rollover rates.
4. **Do not use AUM as the GNPA denominator.** MFL standalone: gross AUM → −ARC sold → −DA/co-lending off-book → +accrued interest → −fee amortisation = gross carrying amount. MHFL (subsidiary) currently uses on-book AUM and skips accrual + amortisation — flag for consolidation restatement.
5. **Auction surplus and unclaimed gold** are conduct + liability items, not “other income” opportunities. ACRE’s ₹27.30 lakh fee-income penalty is a reminder that RBI reads the income line.
6. **Muthoot Finance is UL and a different group.** Their gold-loan disclosures are a peer benchmark, not MFL’s rulebook. MFL stays **ML** until assets cross ₹1 lakh crore and RBI names it on the UL list.

**Quiz yourself**

1. A ₹2 lakh bullet gold loan is sanctioned at 84% LTV on day 1. Gold is unchanged. Accrued interest will add 12% of principal by maturity. Is the loan still LTV-compliant at due date? What do you do at renewal?
2. Why can two branches of the same NBFC not use different XRF haircuts for “stone weight”? Where must the valuation method be published?
3. MFL is ML. A gold loan sits at 78% LTV and is standard. What standard-asset provision rate applies, and which capital add-on (Tier I) is gold-loan specific?

*(Answers: (1) LTV at maturity ≈ 84% × 1.12 ≈ 94% — over 85%, non-compliant; cannot renew unless interest is paid **and** LTV is brought back under cap, typically by part-principal or top-up gold. (2) Assaying procedure must be uniform across branches; methodology on the website — RBC Directions Ch. G. (3) 1% standard provision because LTV > 75%; gold-loan NBFCs need 12% Tier I, not 10%.)*
