## Executive Summary
- **Credit growth stays red-hot but the market is giving it away cheap.** July NBFC credit rose **14.9% YoY to ₹59.89 lakh crore** (vs 10.6% a year ago), with retail lending surging **21.4%** — yet Nifty Financial Services trades at a P/E of just **15.56**, near the bottom of its 5-year range. - **Gold is the sector's engine room and it refuses to cool.** NBFC gold-loan portfolios grew **~68% YoY** on elevated gold prices; brokers (Antique, Jefferies) turned actively bullish on gold-loan names this week, even as they warn competition will intensify.

## 🏛️ Regulatory & Policy Watch

- **RBI operationalises a five-point supervisory roadmap.** At the 7th NBFC & HFC Summit (Sep 3), Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu set out five supervisory priorities for the years ahead: **governance, liquidity, asset quality, customer conduct, and cyber resilience**. No new binding rule was issued, but the guidance to **diversify funding and reduce dependence on short-term wholesale borrowing** is a clear directional signal. NBFC credit now stands at ~**16.7% of nominal GDP** (up from 15.9% a year ago).
  - *What it means:* Expect closer scrutiny of liquidity buffers and funding-mix, a theme that will land hardest on NBFCs that grew fast on wholesale short money.
- **SBR deregistration window.** Under RBI's 2026 Scale-Based Regulation amendment, a new "Unregistered Type I" category exempts certain NBFCs (no public funds, no customer interface) from mandatory registration. Existing registered NBFCs meeting the criteria can apply for deregistration via PRAVAAH **by Sept 30, 2026**.
  - *Implications:* A one-way drip of small, captive/group NBFCs out of the formal regulatory net is likely; Muthoot Fincorp's around-the-corner IPO means UR-I is irrelevant to it but relevant to group entities being rationalised.
- **RBI participation at Global Fintech Fest (Sep 9).** Deputy Governor Rohit Jain's keynote on "Emerging Technologies in Finance" reaffirmed intent on purpose/prudence/policy in AI-led finance — a watch-item for digital lending and LSP oversight.
- **Earlier-this-week echo that still bites:** The Aug 7 norms on **revolving credit facilities** (BNPL-style) had knocked Bajaj Finance and Tata Capital ~5%; the market is still digesting the compliance cost of that circular.
- **Direct Muthoot Fincorp relevance:** The **₹3,000-crore IPO** (DRHP filed Aug 13) is in SEBI review; fresh issue to shore up Tier-I capital, fund onward lending and expansion — capital adequacy for a gold-heavy, growth book is a governance/compliance positive under the new supervisory lens.

## 💳 Credit & Lending Trends

- **July Reserve Bank WSS data (released this week):** NBFC+HFC credit **+14.9% YoY to ₹59.89 lakh crore**, up from **10.6%** a year ago. Growth leaders: **retail +21.4%** (housing, gold jewellery, consumer durables, vehicles), **agri +18%** (vs 5.4% in July 2025). Momentum clearly broadened beyond gold into housing and agri.
- **Lending & deposit rate dynamics:** Policy repo is anchored at **5.25%** with a neutral stance, but **market borrowing costs are rising** — 10Y G-Sec back around/above **7%**. This squeezes the liability side just as retail credit grows at 21%, pressuring NIMs for lenders who priced assets in a lower-rate world.
- **Funding-cost spectrum visible in this week's raises:** diversified/high-rated NBFC-HFCs issued at **7.60–7.90%** (Aavas 7.60%, Shriram 7.80%/7.85% yield, Bajaj Housing 7.79%, Aditya Birla Capital undisclosed), while gold/MFI and retail-oriented issuers paid **8.65–10.00%** (Muthoot FinCorp retail NCDs 8.89–9.25%, Muthoot Microfin 9.25%, Edelweiss public NCDs to 10%). **The credit-quality vs. pricing spread is wide and widening — a feature, not a bug, for gold lenders.**
- **Liquidity backdrop:** banking-system surplus remains massive (~₹10.5–10.7 lakh cr), cushioning the selloff, even as the RBI works to drain it.

## ⚠️ Asset Quality Deep Dive

- **Microfinance stress is decisively easing (MFIN Micrometer, Q1 FY27):** Industry portfolio rose **+1.1% QoQ to ₹3,28,708 cr** — the turnaround after **seven consecutive quarters of contraction** (still −6.9% YoY). **PAR 31–180 days fell to 1.6%**, from 5.6% a year ago and 2.1% last quarter; NBFC-MFIs' own PAR is ~1.5%.
- **Drivers:** regulatory guardrails, a better-than-expected monsoon, shift to existing-to-credit (ETC) borrowers, higher tickets, and large write-offs/ARC sales. Structural rise in average loan per account (+27.8% YoY to ₹37,584) shows lenders pivoting to quality over volume.
- **Funding rebound:** Debt funding to member NBFC-MFIs **+91.4% YoY** in Q1; banks are the dominant fresh-lenders (80.4% of fresh funding) while their *direct* microfinance exposure fell 28.5% (partly reclassification to retail).
- **Watch-list:** 180+ DPD remains elevated (~17.1% in the SIDBI-Equifax series) — long-tail legacy stress persists even as forward flow stabilises. Bihar (16% of industry, new MFI Bill) and Karnataka remain monitorable.
- **For gold lenders:** Asset quality is the mirror image — secured, high-LTV collateral, and elevated gold prices provide a powerful floor; provisions remain a tailwind rather than a headwind this cycle.

## 🌍 Macro Context

- **Global shock, domestic support:** Brent surged toward **$110/bbl** (up sharply on Middle-East supply fears), US 10Y yields near 5%, and the India 10Y G-Sec breached 7% — a classic imported-cost shock. Soaring energy raises India's import bill and complicates the RBI's growth-vs-inflation trade-off.
- **Inflation:** July CPI **4.45%** (food 5.52%) — inside target but rising; RBI projects FY27 inflation ~5.0% with a potential **5.9% peak in Q3**. This argues against near-term rate cuts, keeping the policy repo at 5.25% for a 4th meeting.
- **Growth:** RBI forecasts **GDP +6.7% for FY26-27** (IMF saw FY25-26 at 7.3%), supported by consumption, investment, construction, capital goods and credit — a growth backdrop that keeps credit demand constructive even as rates stay higher-for-longer.
- **Foreign flows / funding costs:** A stronger-yield, weaker-rupee (~95/USD), crude-shock mix is negative for FPI flows into financials and keeps NBFC external funding costs firm (ECBs already ~7% of PNB Housing's borrowing). Domestic banking-system liquidity (~₹10.5 lakh cr surplus) remains the counter-vailing support.

## 💡 Analyst's Take

The cleanest way to read this week is as a **divergence between fundamentals and price**. On the fundamental side, everything is accelerating — NBFC credit at 14.9% with retail at 21.4%, gold growing ~68% on record metals prices, microfinance finally turning after seven quarters, and the debt market showing deep, bid-up appetite for NBFC paper across the rating curve. On the price side, Nifty Financial Services sits near the bottom of its five-year valuation range (P/E ~15.6) and ~10% off highs, with banks and NBFCs sold off hard on a crude-and-yield shock. When a sector grows this fast and trades this cheap, the market is pricing in a *persistence risk* — that the same tailwinds (gold, cheap funding, benign asset quality) don't last. The contrarian read is that the sell-off is indiscriminate macro de-risking, not sector-specific deterioration, and high-quality retail/gold lenders are the accidental beneficiaries of that mispricing.

The funding spread this week is the quiet signal worth watching. High-rated diversified lenders printed at 7.6–7.9%, while gold and microfinance issuers paid 8.9–9.3% or more. That gap is the market's honest statement on near-term credit risk — and it *favours* secured gold lenders, whose effective net spreads remain wide even as their cost of funds moves up. For Muthoot Fincorp leadership, three things matter most: **(1)** the ₹3,000-cr IPO is the single biggest strategic lever — it de-risks the balance sheet, funds growth and lowers the cost of capital ahead of a possibly higher-rate window; **(2)** the retail NCD Tranche V (8.89–9.25%) is a live barometer of retail confidence in the franchise and should be watched for subscription velocity through Sept 22; and **(3)** the **competition signal** — Jefferies' Buy-on-Manappuram/Hold-on-Muthoot and Antique's double-bullish initiation both flag that gold competition (banks, new entrants) will intensify exactly as the pie keeps growing. Asset quality, given 85% LTV and record gold, is the sector's fortress — but 180+ DPD stress in the microfinance book is a reminder that "recovery" in one pocket can coexist with lingering legacy pain in another. The market appears to be missing how strongly secured and diversified NBFCs are the *defensive* expression of Indian credit right now.
