## 🎓 Daily Teaching Session — Wednesday refresher: Capital Adequacy (CRAR, Tier I/II, NOF)

Curriculum is in post-Day-30 refresher mode. Yesterday was SNFA because it goes live in 15 days. Today is the core FP&A number: **how much capital MFL must hold, in what form, against what risk**.

### Capital Adequacy — CRAR, Tier I / Tier II, NOF

**What is it?**

Capital adequacy is the buffer that must sit between MFL’s assets and a loss. RBI does not let an NBFC lever the book until the first rupee of loss wipes out equity. It requires a **minimum ratio of own funds to risk-weighted assets (RWA)** — the Capital to Risk-weighted Assets Ratio, or **CRAR**.

Think of it as three nested questions:

1. **How much?** — CRAR and the Tier I floor (layer-wise).
2. **Of what quality?** — Tier I (loss-absorbing while you are a going concern) vs Tier II (loss-absorbing in gone-concern / subordinated form).
3. **Is the franchise even allowed to exist?** — Net Owned Fund (NOF) is the **entry ticket**, not the running ratio.

Banks have Basel III (CET1, CCB, leverage ratio, G-SIB buffers). NBFCs have a **simpler, stricter-looking CRAR of 15%** for Middle and Upper Layer, with **no capital conservation buffer**. Do not quote bank 11.5% or the urban-legend **30% CRAR**. That 30% figure is wrong.

---

**The Technical Details**

**Source:** Scale-Based Regulation framework, originally *Master Direction – NBFC – Scale Based Regulation Directions, 2023* (RBI/DoR/2023-24/106, DoR.FIN.REC.No.45/03.10.119/2023-24) [EFFECTIVE:2023-10-19], folded into the 28 November 2025 NBFC Master Direction set. Layer thresholds and CRAR/NOF floors are unchanged in the 2026-27 UL list exercise.

**Applies to:** BL / ML / UL differently. MFL is **ML**. Muthoot Finance (separate group) is **UL**.

### The formula

**CRAR = (Tier I + Tier II) / Risk-Weighted Assets**

Must be maintained **at all times**, not just on 31 March. A mid-quarter gold-price spike that lifts AUM, or a rating downgrade that lifts risk weights, can breach the floor even if year-end looked comfortable.

### Layer-wise floors

| Parameter | Base Layer (BL) | Middle Layer (ML) — **MFL** | Upper Layer (UL) |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------------------|------------------|
| Size (simplified) | < ₹1,000 cr | ₹1,000–10,000 cr | ≥ ₹10,000 cr / identified list |
| **Minimum CRAR** | None (leverage cap) | **15%** | **15%** |
| **of which Tier I** | — | **10%** | **10%** ( **12%** if gold loans ≥ 50% of AUM ) |
| **Minimum NOF** | ₹2 cr | **₹10 cr** | ₹30 cr |
| Leverage | Max **7×** owned funds | Not the binding constraint; CRAR is | Same as ML + tighter governance |
| Capital conservation buffer | No | No | No (do **not** apply bank CCB) |

**Gold-loan overlay (MFL-relevant):** If gold-loan AUM is **≥ 50% of total AUM**, Tier I must be **12%**, not 10%. That is a going-concern overlay because gold books can gap on price and auction realisation. MFL is a gold-loan franchise — **treat 12% Tier I as the working floor** unless compliance confirms gold is below 50% on a continuing basis (unlikely).

### What counts as Tier I (going-concern)

- Paid-up **equity** (and perpetual non-redeemable preference, if any)
- **Free reserves** (not revaluation reserves)
- **Statutory reserve** under **s.45-IC** of the RBI Act, 1934 (the 20% of net profit that must be transferred every year before dividend)
- Specified innovative perpetual debt, **capped** (typically up to 1.5% of RWA for ML/UL)

Deductions bite: investments in shares of other NBFCs, certain related-party advances, and deferred-tax / intangibles under the applicable MD. Do not “gross up” CRAR with items that NOF already knocks out.

### What counts as Tier II

- **Subordinated debt** with original maturity **≥ 5 years** (amortised in the last 5 years for capital credit)
- **Revaluation reserves**, typically at a **55% haircut** (only 45% credited — confirm the current MD paragraph before a board note; older texts used 50%)
- **General provision** on standard assets, **capped at 1.25% of RWA**
- Tier II **cannot exceed 100% of Tier I** — you cannot stack cheap sub-debt on a thin equity base

### NOF — the licence number, not the ratio

**NOF ≈ owned funds − investments in shares of other NBFCs − certain related-party / group exposures** (exact deductions are in the SBR / Registration MD).

- Drop below the layer floor and you are in **licence / layer trouble**, not just a ratio miss.
- ML floor is ₹10 cr — MFL is orders of magnitude above this. NOF is not MFL’s binding constraint; **CRAR and the gold-loan 12% Tier I** are.

### Risk weights (why gold is capital-light)

RWA is not AUM. A ₹100 gold loan at 50% risk weight consumes ₹7.5 of CRAR capital (15% × 50). A ₹100 unsecured personal loan at 125% consumes ₹18.75.

| Asset | Typical risk weight (NBFC MD) |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| Cash / G-Secs | 0% |
| Bank claims (short / highly rated) | 20% (tenor/rating dependent) |
| **Gold loans, LTV ≤ 50%** | **35%** |
| **Gold loans, LTV 50–75%** | **50%** |
| Claims on other NBFCs (ML/UL) | 100% |
| Commercial real estate | 100–150% |
| Consumer / retail | 75–125% |
| Unsecured | 125% |

**FP&A implication:** mix shift from gold toward unsecured / MSME / housing (MHFL) **raises RWA density** even if AUM is flat. A 1-ppt mix shift into 125% RW assets is a capital event, not just a yield event.

LTV on gold remains a **hard 75% cap** under gold-loan directions. Pushing origination toward the 75% LTV bucket moves the book from 35% RW to 50% RW — a **43% increase in capital consumed per rupee of gold AUM**.

### ICAAP — the board conversation above the floor

The 15% / 12% numbers are **regulatory minima**. Upper Layer NBFCs must run an Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP) — stress gold price, auction haircut, operational risk, concentration, liquidity. ML entities are expected to have a **board-approved capital plan** even where a full bank-style ICAAP is not copied 1:1. For MFL FP&A: the useful ICAAP questions are (a) gold-price −20% / −30%, (b) auction realisation vs LTV, (c) refinance freeze, (d) mix shift to non-gold.

PCA (Prompt Corrective Action) for NBFCs uses CRAR (and NNPA, and leverage) as a trigger. Breaching the CRAR floor is not a “we’ll top up next quarter” event — it is a supervisory event.

---

**Why It Matters**

- **Growth capacity.** AUM growth is capped by (current capital × CRAR headroom) / RWA density. If MFL runs at 18% CRAR with gold at 50% RW, the headroom is 3 ppts; the same headroom on unsecured at 125% RW funds far less AUM.
- **Dividend / s.45-IC.** 20% of net profit is locked into statutory reserve **before** dividend. That helps Tier I over time, but it is not free — it is retained earnings you cannot distribute.
- **Pricing.** Capital charge is a real cost of funds. Gold at 35–50% RW can clear a lower spread than MSME at 100%+ and still hit the same RoE.
- **Group vs peer.** Muthoot Finance (UL, different group) has the same 15% CRAR but **12% Tier I as a gold UL**, plus UL governance. Do not copy their disclosure pack into an MFL board note without stripping UL-only items.
- **MHFL (HFC subsidiary).** HFCs are **automatically ML** regardless of size. Housing risk weights and LTV bands differ from gold. Consolidation of RWA is where gold-light capital meets housing-heavy capital.

**Muthoot Fincorp Context**

MFL is **ML, gold-heavy**. Working capital rules of thumb for FP&A:

1. **Plan to 12% Tier I, not 10%.** If gold is ≥ 50% of AUM, the overlay is in force.
2. **Track RWA density, not just AUM.** A mix shift into MSME / unsecured / MHFL housing is a CRAR event. Show “capital consumed per ₹100 of new AUM” by product in the monthly pack.
3. **LTV mix is a capital lever.** Origination clustered at 70–75% LTV pays 50% RW; a larger share of ≤50% LTV gold is 35% RW. That is a 15-ppt RW delta — material at MFL scale.
4. **Do not manage to the 15.0% print.** Supervisory comfort and PCA distance want a buffer. A 15.2% CRAR with gold-price risk is a stress-test fail, not a pass.
5. **s.45-IC + dividend.** Model the 20% statutory transfer as a **Tier I build**, not as a residual after dividend. Dividend capacity is what is left after transfer **and** after the CRAR/Tier I buffer the board wants.
6. **Never paste Muthoot Finance (UL) capital slides into an MFL note.** Different company, different layer, different governance overlay.

**Quiz yourself:**

1. MFL’s gold book is 70% of AUM, LTV 65%. What is the **Tier I** floor — 10% or 12%? Why?
2. Two new loans of ₹100 each: (A) gold at 45% LTV, (B) unsecured personal at 125% RW. How much **CRAR capital** does each consume at a 15% CRAR? Which one needs more capital, and by what multiple?
3. True or false: an NBFC-UL must hold **30% CRAR**. (If you said true, re-read the pitfall — CRAR is **15%** for ML and UL. 30% is a myth.)

**Answers:** (1) **12%** — gold ≥ 50% of AUM triggers the gold-loan Tier I overlay. (2) A: RWA = ₹35, capital = 15% × 35 = **₹5.25**. B: RWA = ₹125, capital = 15% × 125 = **₹18.75**. Unsecured consumes **3.6×** the capital of low-LTV gold. (3) **False.**
