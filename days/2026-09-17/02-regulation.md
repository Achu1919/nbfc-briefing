### 1. [ENFORCEMENT / JUDICIAL] Supreme Court directs RBI to actually enforce vehicle-repossession / recovery guidelines — Cholamandalam ordered to compensate

- **What:** Supreme Court held that financiers cannot seize a hypothecated vehicle by force, stealth or at odd hours, even if the loan agreement has a self-help repossession clause. It directed **RBI to take effective steps to secure genuine compliance** by NBFCs and scheduled commercial banks with the recovery / repossession guidelines it has already issued. Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company Ltd was ordered to close the borrower’s loan accounts, refund the ₹4.5 lakh sale proceeds with 6% interest, pay **₹10 lakh** compensation, and ₹50,000 costs.
- **Issuer:** Supreme Court of India (bench of Justices P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe). Registry directed to send a copy of the judgment to RBI.
- **Date:** Judgment delivered **Wednesday, 16 September 2026**. Press (PTI / ET BFSI) **16 Sep, 19:49 IST**. [EFFECTIVE:2026-09-16] for the parties; RBI follow-up circular **not yet issued**.
- **Reference:** Civil appeal arising from the Allahabad High Court order in the matter of a Tata SFC 407 truck financed by Cholamandalam (borrower reported in press as Hari Dutta Sharma). Loan ~₹10.40 lakh (₹9.36 lakh disbursed + ₹1.04 lakh supplementary); vehicle taken at ~**1:00 a.m. on 9 April 2023** without the contractual **7-day notice**; sold 31 August 2023 for ₹4.5 lakh. Official SCI case number not on the RBI site as of this morning — treat PTI as the public source until the judgment PDF is on sci.gov.in.
- **Source:** [ET BFSI / PTI, 16 Sep 2026](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/banking/sc-asks-rbi-to-secure-compliance-of-norms-on-seizure-of-financed-vehicles-by-nbfcs-banks/134290251)

**In Plain English:** The Court did **not** ban repossession. It said a contractual right to take the asset is commercially necessary for vehicle finance to people without other collateral — *but* that right is not a licence to break a steering lock at 1 a.m. Existing RBI guidelines already say: no force, no stealth, no harassment, notice before possession, a last chance to pay before sale. The Court’s finding is that those guidelines have “existed only on paper.” RBI is now under a judicial direction to make them real. The compensation (₹10 lakh for mental agony / loss of livelihood, plus refund of sale proceeds) is the price of getting the process wrong.

**Previous position:** RBI’s Fair Practices / Responsible Business Conduct directions already required lawful possession. The **6 August 2026** *NBFC – Responsible Business Conduct Third Amendment Directions* (RBI/2026-27/230, DOR.MCS.REC.No.199/01-01-039/2026-27) go further — Board policy, IIBF-certified agents, 08:00–19:00 contact window, notice before first visit, possession clause contents, call recording for 6 months — but they only **go live on 1 January 2027**. The Supreme Court has just told RBI that waiting for that date is not an excuse for overnight seizures happening *now*.

**Impact on NBFCs:**
- Vehicle / CE / hypothecated-asset books (Chola, Shriram, Mahindra Finance, captive auto NBFCs) are in the blast radius. Overnight “self-help” repossession without the contractual notice is now a Supreme Court-level liability, not just an RBI inspection finding.
- Expect RBI to respond with a **compliance circular or supervisory letter** to NBFCs and banks. Watch the Press Release page and DoR/DoS mailers — do not wait for 1 Jan 2027 to fix possession SOPs.
- Compensation clauses in the 6 Aug directions (para 100F — Board policy must provide compensation for loss from non-compliant recovery) just got a judicial illustration of quantum.
- Ombudsman / RBI-ICS complaints on recovery will be easier for borrowers to win.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** MFL is **not** a vehicle-finance NBFC. Direct book impact is **low**. Indirect / conduct impact is **medium**, for three reasons:

1. **Gold-loan auction / pledge enforcement** is the analogue of vehicle repossession. Auction without the contractual notice, or collection visits outside 08:00–19:00, or “taking” ornaments other than through the documented pledge-and-auction path, is the same fact-pattern the Court just punished. Gold is the borrower’s livelihood store of value in the same way the truck was.
2. **MHFL (housing subsidiary)** and any MSME / hypothecated-security recovery must already follow SARFAESI / contractual notice. The Court’s language on “stealth / dead of night / no recourse” applies to field staff and outsourced agencies **today**, not from 1 Jan 2027.
3. **Recovery Agent Conduct** (taught below) goes live in **106 days**. This judgment is the reason the Board should not treat IIBF / hours / recording as a January paperwork exercise.

**FP&A action:** No P&L provision required at MFL from this one Chola order. Ask collections / legal for (a) confirmation that gold-auction notice timelines are in the loan agreement and actually followed, (b) a one-page gap vs para 100R possession-clause contents, (c) status of IIBF enrolment for outsourced recovery. Flag any open ombudsman recovery complaints in the next ALCO / Risk pack.

Overnight press (ET 17 Sep, 01:04 IST; TOI) presents it as RBI “clarifying” the 50:50 principal-business test, the CIC tests, and the definition of **public funds (including indirect receipt through group entities)** after rejecting Tata Sons’ CoR-surrender.
- **Issuer:** RBI (Department of Regulation / DNBS FAQ page — not a Master Direction).
- **Date:** FAQ stamp **15 September 2026**. Press **16–17 September**. The underlying tests are **old** (50:50 test = Press Release 1998-99/1269 dated 8 April 1999).
- **Reference:** [RBI FAQ Id=1167](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/FAQs.aspx?Id=1167)
- **Source:** [ET, 17 Sep 2026, 01:04 IST](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/banking/finance/banking/rbi-clarifies-principal-business-rules-for-nbfcs-cic-definition-after-tata-sons-cor-rejection/articleshow/134296053.cms)

**In Plain English:** Nothing in this FAQ creates a new obligation for a lending NBFC that already holds a CoR. It restates:

| Test | Rule (unchanged) |
|------|------------------|
| **50:50 principal business** | Financial assets > 50% of total assets (net of intangibles) **and** income from financial assets > 50% of gross income → must register as NBFC |
| **CIC** | ≥90% of net assets in group shares/debt/loans; ≥60% in group equity (and InvIT sponsor units); asset size ≥ ₹100 crore; **accepts public funds**; no other financial activity except group funding / deposits / G-Secs |
| **Public funds ≠ public deposits** | Includes ICDs, bank finance, CPs, debentures, and **indirect** receipt “through associates and group entities which have access to public funds” (FAQ Q.12) |

Tata Sons’ argument — “we repaid standalone debt, so we no longer accept public funds and can surrender the CoR” — is the argument this FAQ definition is being used to shut down. That is a **CIC / UL listing** story, not an MFL story.

**Impact on NBFCs:** Only material if you are (a) a holding-company CIC trying to exit the SBR perimeter, or (b) a Type I / unregistered entity arguing you have no public funds. The Type I deregistration window still closes **31 December 2026**. Indirect public funds via the group is the tripwire.

**Muthoot Fincorp Note:** **None for the lending book.** MFL is a registered ML ICC with customer interface and public funds (bank lines, NCDs). Do not confuse Muthoot Fincorp with Tata Sons, and do not confuse it with Muthoot Finance (UL, separate group). Group entities that are Type I / CICs should still hit the 31 Dec window if they qualify — the FAQ does not extend that window.

## 🎓 Daily Teaching Session — Thursday refresher: Recovery conduct, possession of security, and why yesterday’s Supreme Court order matters

Curriculum is in post-Day-30 refresher mode. Gold-loan LTV was taught on 12 Sep. Capital was yesterday. Today is the topic the Court just put on RBI’s desk: **how an NBFC is allowed to collect, and how it is allowed to take the security**.

### Recovery of loan dues & engagement of recovery agencies

**What is it?**

Recovery is the moment the loan stops being an asset-quality ratio and becomes a human interaction. RBI does not ban collection. It bans **harsh collection**. The rule of thumb:

> You may take the security. You may not take it at 1 a.m. You may call the borrower. You may not call her relatives to humiliate her. You may outsource. You cannot outsource the **liability**.

Two layers now sit on top of each other:

1. **Existing Fair Practices / Responsible Business Conduct** — already in force. This is what the Supreme Court said has been “only on paper.”
2. **RBC Third Amendment Directions, 6 August 2026** — a full rewrite of Section J, **effective 1 January 2027**. Board policy, IIBF certificate, 08:00–19:00 window, 6-month call recording, possession-clause contents, compensation for wrongful recovery.

Yesterday’s judgment is RBI being told: do not wait until January to make layer 1 real.

---

**The Technical Details**

**Source (current, in force):** *Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025*, Chapter III (Responsible Lending Conduct), as amended.

**Source (final, not yet live):** *Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Responsible Business Conduct) Third Amendment Directions, 2026*, RBI/2026-27/230, DOR.MCS.REC.No.199/01-01-039/2026-27, **6 August 2026**, [EFFECTIVE:2027-01-01].

**Applies to:** All NBFCs **except** Mortgage Guarantee Companies, CICs, NBFC-Account Aggregators, Standalone Primary Dealers, NOFHCs, and NBFCs with no customer interface. **MFL is in.** MHFL is in (HFC via NHB Act overlay; treat the conduct standard as the floor). Muthoot Finance (UL, separate group) is in too — not our problem.

**Layer:** Conduct rules are **activity-based**, not SBR-layer-based. BL, ML and UL all follow the same recovery hours and the same ban on harsh methods. UL has extra governance (more independent directors, Chief Compliance Officer) which makes a recovery failure more embarrassing, not a different collection clock.

### The two definitions that close the outsourcing loophole (para 6(11A)/(11B))

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Recovery agency** | Any entity **or individual other than the NBFC’s own employees** engaged — whatever you call the contract — to help recover dues **including taking possession of a security** |
| **Recovery agent** | The person at the customer interface working for that agency |

If you hire a person directly on an outsourcing contract to do recovery, **both** sets of instructions apply to that individual. Relabelling them “business correspondent,” “field executive,” or “valuer-cum-collection” does not help.

Own employees who do recovery are **also** covered (para 100C). You cannot hide behind “we don’t use agencies.”

### What the Board policy must contain by 1 Jan 2027 (paras 100D–100G)

1. Triggers for starting recovery, and a **graded escalation matrix**.
2. Code of conduct for employees **and** agents.
3. What happens on **death of the borrower**.
4. A documented pre-escalation path for financial-distress cases (resolution options **before** the heavy hand).
5. Due diligence, performance standards, inspection/audit of agencies, and **penal action** when they break the rules.
6. **Compensation to borrower/guarantor** for loss from non-compliant recovery (para 100F). Yesterday’s ₹10 lakh is a judicial data point for how boards will now think about this clause.
7. If you ever lock a financed mobile device, the gradual-restriction timeline belongs in the same policy (paras 100S–100U). Gold-loan MFL: mostly N/A, but digital-lending / device-finance in the group is not.

### IIBF certificate (para 100I)

Every recovery agent must hold the **IIBF Debt Recovery Agents** certificate (or an IIBF tie-up institute). Agents already on the books on 1 Jan 2027 get **one year** to obtain it (i.e. by **1 Jan 2028**). New engagements from 1 Jan 2027: certificate first, then the beat.

This is the item that slips in gold-loan networks because “the auctioneer / the jewellery appraiser also does follow-up.” If they interface with the defaulted borrower, they are a recovery agent.

### Disclosure, notice, recording (paras 100K–100P)

| Obligation | Clock |
|------------|-------|
| Empanelled-agency list on the **website** (name, type, address, period, purpose: recovery vs possession) | Update within **7 calendar days** of any change; **immediately** on termination |
| Intimation to borrower/guarantor of the agency **before first in-person visit** | **At least 1 day prior** |
| Change of agency mid-process | **Immediately** notify borrower |
| Termination of agency | **Immediately** notify assigned borrowers so they stop dealing with that agency |
| Call content recorded (employee **and** agent, both directions) | Preserve **6 months**, or until disposal if sub judice. Borrower must be told the call is recorded |
| Recovery targets / incentives | Must **not** induce the harsh practices in para 100Z |

### Possession of security — this is the paragraph the Supreme Court just illustrated (para 100R)

If the loan agreement has a possession clause and you rely on it, the clause must be **legally valid** and **brought to the borrower’s notice at execution**. The contract must specify:

1. Notice period before taking possession.
2. Circumstances in which notice can be waived.
3. Procedure for taking possession.
4. **Final chance to repay before sale/auction.**
5. Procedure for giving the security back.
6. Procedure for sale/auction.

Chola’s clause, as described by the Court, allowed the financier to skip notice, enter premises, and set the sale process at its own discretion. That is exactly the list above, inverted. For **gold loans**, map this onto the pledge-and-auction path: notice before auction, published auction, surplus back to the borrower. “We had the ornaments in the strong-room already” is not a waiver of auction notice.

### The clock and the manners (para 100Y)

- Contact / visit **only 08:00–19:00**, unless the borrower **expressly** asked otherwise.
- Honour a request to avoid a particular time, in normal circumstances.
- Place of contact = borrower’s choice; only after **two successive no-shows** may you go to residence or workplace.
- Avoid bereavement, medical emergency, marriage.
- **Microfinance:** collection at a mutually decided designated place; residence/work only after two successive no-shows.
- Identity card on every visit. Agents also carry an authorisation letter **and** a copy of the 100L notice. Both must show the agency phone number and the NBFC grievance-redressal officer.
- Written comms from an agent need **prior NBFC approval** and the sender’s name/contact.
- Receipt on every collection.

### What is “harsh” — a closed list plus a general ban (para 100Z)

Deemed harsh, without limiting the general ban:

1. Minatory or abusive language.
2. Posting the borrower’s video/audio or personal details on social media.
3. Inappropriate messages (mobile or social).
4. Excessive calling/messaging, or calling **outside prescribed hours**.
5. Threatening or anonymous calls.
6. Intimidating/harassing the borrower **or** relatives, referees, friends, co-workers — including public humiliation or privacy intrusion.
7. Violence or threat of violence against person, family, assets, reputation.
8. False or misleading representations about the debt or the consequences of non-payment.

Calling the gold-loan customer’s employer, or WhatsApping a photo of the pledged ornament to the family WhatsApp group, is 100Z(6) and 100Z(2). Do not do it. Do not let an agency do it.

### Grievance redress (para 100AA)

Dedicated recovery-grievance channel. Details in the **loan agreement** and in the pre-visit intimation. Every recovery communication must carry name, email, phone and address of the GRO.

**Why It Matters**

Conduct failures have become an **enforcement theme** in 2026 (recovery-agent directions in August; DG Murmu at the CII NBFC & HFC Summit on 3 Sep: “conduct regulation, grievance redressal and responsible lending remain top priorities”). Yesterday the Supreme Court converted that theme into a **direction to the regulator** and a **₹10 lakh compensation** data point against a listed UL NBFC.

For FP&A this is not a CRAR number. It is:

- **Contingent liability** on wrongful-possession / ombudsman awards (para 100F compensation is now a live policy item).
- **Opex** — IIBF fees, call-recording storage, website list, agency due diligence.
- **Franchise risk** — gold-loan NBFCs live on repeat customers walking into a branch with jewellery. One viral 1 a.m. story does more damage than 50 bps of NIM.

**Recent Changes**

- **Previously (pre-6 Aug 2026):** Recovery-agent instructions were scattered through Chapter III (old paras 26–27, 89–100). Lighter on IIBF, recording, website lists, and possession-clause contents.
- **Now (from 1 Jan 2027):** A single Section J, 100A–100AB. Broader definition (possession of security is recovery). Mandatory Board policy including **borrower compensation**. IIBF. 08:00–19:00. 6-month recording.
- **16 Sep 2026:** Supreme Court — existing guidelines are not optional; RBI must enforce them against NBFCs **and** banks. Self-help repossession clauses survive only if they are not unconscionable and are actually followed.

**Muthoot Fincorp Context**

MFL’s core book is **gold**. The security is already in the branch strong-room. That is a structural advantage versus vehicle finance — you do not need a night squad. It is also a structural **temptation**: because you already hold the gold, skipping auction notice or leaning on the family looks “operationally easy.” The Court has just said that kind of ease is how you write a ₹10 lakh cheque.

Practical map for MFL:

| MFL process | RBC / SC overlay |
|-------------|------------------|
| Branch follow-up on overdue gold loan | 08:00–19:00; identity card; no family/employer calls |
| Outsourced collection (if any, incl. MFI) | IIBF by 1 Jan 2027 (new) / 1 Jan 2028 (existing); website list; 1-day pre-visit notice |
| Gold auction | Para 100R items 1–6 must be in the pledge agreement **and** followed. Surplus to borrower. |
| Digital / app reminders | 100Z(3)/(4) — no barrage, no off-hours, no social-media shaming |
| MHFL / MSME SARFAESI | Notice before possession; no stealth; Court language applies now |
