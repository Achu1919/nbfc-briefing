## 🎓 Daily Teaching Session — Friday refresher: How an NBFC dies (CoR cancellation, surrender, Type I)

Curriculum is in post-Day-30 refresher mode. Yesterday was recovery conduct (the Supreme Court overlay). Today’s two PRs are a live illustration of **Day 4 — Licensing & Registration**: how the CoR is taken away, and which exits are real.

### Certificate of Registration — cancellation, surrender, and the Type I window

**What is it?**

An NBFC is not a company that happens to lend. It is a company that **RBI has licensed** under **Section 45-IA** of the RBI Act, 1934. The licence is the **Certificate of Registration (CoR)**. Without it, you cannot commence or carry on the business of a non-banking financial institution.

There are only three honest ways the CoR ends:

1. **RBI cancels it** (today’s PR 1133) — you did not qualify, or you stopped complying.
2. **You surrender it and RBI agrees** (today’s PR 1132) — you exited the business, you became a Type I / unregistered CIC, or the company ceased to exist.
3. **The company is resolved / amalgamated** under IBC / NCLT / Companies Act, and RBI then cancels because there is no legal person left.

There is **no fourth way** called “we repaid our standalone debt so we are no longer an NBFC.” That was Tata Sons’ argument. RBI said no on 11 September.

---

**The Technical Details**

**Source (licence):** Section **45-IA** of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. No company shall commence or carry on NBFI business without a CoR.

**Source (cancellation power):** Section **45-IA(6)** — the section cited in both of today’s PRs. RBI may cancel a CoR if the company (among other grounds) ceases to carry on NBFI business, fails to comply with conditions, or fails to meet the principal-business / NOF tests.

**Source (who must register; Type I; CIC):** *Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Registration, Exemptions and Framework for Scale Based Regulation) Directions, 2025*, as amended in 2026. Type I / unregistered exemption **[EFFECTIVE:2026-07-01]**. Existing eligible registered entities: apply for deregistration by **31 December 2026**.

**Source (what “public funds” and “principal business” mean):** RBI FAQ *“All you wanted to know about NBFCs”*, Id=1167, restamped **15 September 2026**. The tests themselves are old (50:50 test = Press Release 1998-99/1269 dated 8 April 1999).

**Applies to:** Every company whose financial assets **and** financial income both exceed 50% (the 50:50 test). Activity-based, not SBR-layer-based. BL, ML and UL all need a CoR. MFL is in. MHFL is in (HFC via NHB Act overlay + RBI). Muthoot Finance (UL, separate group) is in too — not our problem.

**Layer:** Registration is **on/off**. SBR layer (BL / ML / UL) decides *how tightly* you are supervised once you are in. It does not decide *whether* you need a CoR. Type I is the only calibrated off-ramp, and it is deliberately narrow.

### The 50:50 test (still the front door)

| Leg | Rule |
|-----|------|
| **Assets** | Financial assets > 50% of total assets (net of intangibles) |
| **Income** | Income from financial assets > 50% of gross income |
| **Result** | Both true → you **must** have a CoR. One true, one false → not an NBFC (but RBI can still look at you). |

You cannot “average” your way out. A gold-loan company with 90% financial assets and 90% interest income is an NBFC even if the promoter also owns a jewellery shop next door.

### Public funds ≠ public deposits

This is the tripwire Tata Sons hit, and the sentence CDN Finance had to satisfy.

**Public funds** include, not just public deposits:

- bank finance
- inter-corporate deposits
- commercial paper
- debentures / NCDs
- **indirect** receipt “through associates and group entities which have access to public funds” (FAQ Q.12, restamped 15 Sep)

**Public deposits** are a subset. An ML gold-loan NBFC like MFL has public funds even if it takes **zero** public deposits — because it has bank lines and NCDs.

### Type I — the only clean exemption

| Condition | Must be true |
|-----------|----------------|
| Public funds | **None** — including no indirect group funding |
| Customer interface | **None** — no borrowers, no depositors, no distribution of financial products |
| Asset size | **≤ ₹1,000 crore** to stay unregistered. Above that, you register as Type I even with no customers / no public funds |
| Deadline (already registered, now eligible) | Apply via **PRAVAAH** by **31 December 2026** |

Today’s PR 1132, bucket (iii), is the textbook: *CDN Finance Pvt Ltd — “not availing public funds and not having any customer interface.”*

If either condition fails — a single bank OD, a single NCD, a single walk-in borrower, or a group company on-lending public money to you — you are **not** Type I.

### CIC — a different off-ramp, a different trap

A Core Investment Company is an NBFC whose business is holding group companies.

| CIC test | Rule |
|----------|------|
| Group investments | ≥ 90% of net assets in group shares / debt / loans |
| Group equity | ≥ 60% in group equity (and InvIT sponsor units) |
| Size | Asset size ≥ ₹100 crore |
| Public funds | **Accepts public funds** → must register as CIC |
| Activity | No other financial activity except group funding / deposits / G-Secs |

**Unregistered CIC** (today’s bucket (ii), Anagram Industries): meets the CIC asset tests **and** does **not** accept public funds (including indirect). Then you may not need a CoR.

**Registered CIC / UL** (Tata Sons): public funds, including indirect through listed group companies that hold your shares, keep you inside. Repaying standalone debt does not switch the lights off if the group still has public money.

### Four ways today’s eight names left — and which one MFL will never use

```
                    ┌─ (i)  Exit NBFI business          → stop lending; wind down book
   Surrender ───────┼─ (ii) Unregistered CIC             → group holding, no public funds
   (you ask)        ├─ (iii) Unregistered Type I         → no public funds, no customers
                    └─ (iv) Company ceases to exist      → merger / strike-off / dissolution

   Cancellation ───── RBI takes the CoR (NOF, 50:50, non-compliance, inactivity)
   (RBI acts)
```

MFL, as a customer-facing gold-loan ICC with bank lines and NCDs, sits in **none** of the surrender buckets. The only way MFL’s CoR ends is if RBI cancels it for cause, or if the company itself is merged / resolved — neither of which is a planning scenario. Group **investment** companies might sit in (ii) or (iii).

### Why the August order / September PR lag exists

RBI’s legal act is the **cancellation order**. The press release is a **market notice**: banks, debenture trustees, depositories, and the MCA should stop treating the name as a licensed NBFC. If you are a treasury counterparty, the order date is when they stopped being legal; the PR date is when you are expected to have noticed.

**Quiz yourself:**

1. **Q:** MFL prepays every bank line and every NCD, so it has no “public funds.” Can it surrender the CoR as Type I?
   **A:** **No.** Type I also requires **no customer interface**. A gold-loan borrower walking into a branch is customer interface. Public funds was only one of two gates. Even on public funds, *indirect* group funding would still count (FAQ Q.12).

2. **Q:** Today’s PR cancelled CDN Finance as Type I and refused Tata Sons last week. Same section of law. Why different outcomes?
   **A:** s.45-IA(6) is the *power*. The *facts* differ. CDN Finance: no public funds, no customers. Tata Sons: CIC with public funds, including **indirect** through listed group entities, and UL identification. The FAQ restamp of 15 Sep is the sentence that closes the Tata-style argument.

3. **Q:** A cancelled-CoR company (say Parsoli) still has a small ICD outstanding to an MFL group entity on 18 Sep. What is the issue?
   **A:** From the **order date** (18 Aug for Parsoli), they are not licensed to carry on NBFI business. You are transacting with an unlicensed counterparty. Credit, legal and reputational — not a pricing footnote. Close it.
