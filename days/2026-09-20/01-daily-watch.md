## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

1. **Tata Sons boardroom war escalates → ₹40,000 crore wiped off Tata group** — On Thursday (17 Sep) the Tata Sons board reappointed **N Chandrasekaran for a fresh five-year term** (his 3rd) despite opposition from Tata Trusts, and simultaneously resolved to **initiate compliance with RBI listing requirements** for an Upper-Layer NBFC. Tata Trusts (which hold ~66% voting rights) rejected the listing Thursday night, and on **Friday 18 Sep** listed Tata group companies sold off hard: **Tata Chemicals -8.35%** (intraday -10%, steepest since Mar 2024), **TCS -2.75% to -3.88%** (≈₹27,000 cr market cap lost), Tata Motors PV -3.4%, Tata Investment Corp -3.43%. Combined group market-value erosion ≈₹40,000 crore. The trigger: the **RBI last week rejected Tata Sons' application to surrender its NBFC (CIC) registration**, keeping it under the mandatory-listing rule. **Tata Capital was the lone gainer (~+1–2%, near ₹347)**, +~2% per some reports/1.05% intraday. A potential Tata Sons IPO could top **$5 billion, valuation >₹20 lakh crore** — among India's largest ever. *(Sources: CNBC-TV18, Mint, India Infoline, Fortune India, BS — 18 Sep 2026)*

2. **RBI forces Tata Sons toward listing; NCLT/EGM battle likely** — Proxy advisory **IiAS** says the Chandra reappointment "will not endure" because the decision requires a shareholder vote the Trusts will block. Trusts have paths to the NCLT (injunction) or an EGM. Tata Trusts chair **Noel Tata** warned a listing would "destroy the character" of the holding company; **Shapoorji Pallonji Group (18.4%)** backs listing and wants a long-awaited exit. IiAS floated an **offer-for-sale of Tata Sons' listed stakes (e.g. TCS)** to fund an SP buyout instead. **Watch for you at Muthoot Fincorp:** regulatory hardening of governance/listing norms for UL-NBFCs is a live theme. *(Sources: CNBC-TV18, India Infoline — 18 Sep 2026)*

3. **Gold firms to fresh highs — direct read-through to gold-loan NBFCs** — Gold rallied to **$4,385.62/oz (+1.02%)** on Friday and **MCX gold futures ₹1,54,138/10g (+0.76%)**, supported by a weaker dollar, safe-haven demand and US Treasury long-bond buybacks. NBFC gold-loan outstandings are already up **69.3% YoY to ₹3.41 lakh crore (June 2026)**. This is the single strongest growth engine in the sector and the key neighbour to Muthoot's business. *(Sources: 5paisa post-market, Capital Market, BusinessStandard, TOI)*

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — SUNDAY: Weekly Preview + Sector Health Check

**Where the sector stands (health check):**
- **Gold loans = the growth engine.** NBFC gold-loan book +69% YoY to ₹3.41 lakh cr (Jun-26); organised market ~₹15–18.6 lakh cr. Growth is increasingly **gold-price and repeat-borrowing driven** (82% of originations in 2025 came from existing customers; pledged tonnage flat at ~260–265 t across the big two) — a concentration of over-leverage risk to monitor. New entrants (Aditya Birla Capital, Tata Capital, Bajaj ramping) are intensifying yield competition.
- **Microfinance:** emerging from a FY24–26 stress/consolidation phase; lending ticked up Q1 QFY27 for the first time in 7 quarters; borrower-count still contracting. Government credit-guarantee scheme (up to ₹20,000 cr flow) is a tailwind.
- **Housing finance:** steady; affordable/mid housing demand in Tier-2/3; mid-tier HFCs (LIC HFL, GIC HFL, HomeFirst) comfortably rated.
- **Auto/vehicle finance:** strong but normalising (CV volumes post-pre-buying); **EV financing is the standout — Shriram Finance EV disbursements up 5x to ₹250 cr/month, targeting ₹500 cr/month next year**; expects +18–20% asset growth FY27.
- **Capital & funding:** NBFCs well capitalised (Shriram CRAR 34%, net worth ₹1.09 lakh cr post-MUFG); funding costs easing after RBI rate cuts; commercial-paper issuance strong.
