## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. Rate-hike cycle is now the dominant NBFC theme — Fed hiked, Indian NBFC stocks down up to 9% in a month**
The US Federal Reserve delivered its first rate hike since 2023 — 25 bps to a 3.75–4% range — with officials signalling one more increase later this year, and the US 10-year yield crossed the psychological 5% mark. Per a **JM Financial** note (Sept 21), Indian NBFC stocks fell up to 9% over the past month on anticipation of a domestic policy hike and rising yields. JM estimates a 50 bps repo hike would shave 25–45 bps off FY28 cost of funds across NBFCs — the quantum depending on funding mix, bank-borrowing share, bond maturity profile and repricing risk. **Why it matters at Muthoot Fincorp:** gold/MFI lenders are called out among the negative-NIM names, while housing finance is the biggest beneficiary.

**2. FII outflows top ₹23,000 crore in September; RBI under 'tightening pressure'**
Foreign institutional investors pulled more than ₹23,000 crore from domestic equities in September, keeping pressure on headline indices. The pressure for an RBI hike is building as global central banks tighten and domestic price pressures persist. The rupee settled flat at 95.95/USD on heavy central-bank intervention. Notably, the RBI mobilised a record ~US$133 billion in FCNR(B) deposits via its special concessional swap window to fortify forex reserves and defend the rupee.

**3. Gold loan boom keeps compounding (Tue sector focus) — NBFC gold book ~₹3.54 lakh cr, +68.5% YoY**
Latest RBI sectoral deployment data (July 2026): NBFC gold loan portfolio (incl. HFCs) grew **68.5% YoY to ~₹3.54 lakh crore**, accelerating from 43.9% a year earlier — the fastest-growing retail segment. Banks' gold loans grew 88.1% (from 136.4%), to ₹5.52 lakh crore. Total organised gold-loan market is ~₹18.6–19.4 lakh crore and ICRA projects **₹30 lakh crore by March 2028**. (Full deep-dive below.)

## 💻 Digital & Fintech

- **Digital gold demand exploded:** retail digital gold purchases surged **110% YoY to ~₹2,500 crore/month** (June–Aug 2026) ahead of the festive season — a soft-signal tailwind for gold-backed lending perception, though SEBI has flagged risks.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Gold Loan NBFCs (Tuesday focus)

Gold loans are decisively the best-faring retail segment in the NBFC space, and the data keeps getting better:

- **NBFC gold portfolio (incl. HFCs) grew 68.5% YoY to ~₹3.54 lakh crore in July 2026** (RBI), vs 69.9% in May (₹3.30 lakh cr). Total NBFC credit grew 14.9% to ₹59.89 lakh crore; retail grew 21.4%. Gold was the fastest-growing retail product.
- **Market size:** total organised gold-loan pool ~₹18.6 lakh crore (Mar-26; ~11% of retail credit) to ₹19.4 lakh cr (Experian basis). 34% CAGR FY21–FY26, ~50% YoY in FY26. **ICRA sees ₹30 lakh crore by March 2028** (30%+ CAGR FY27–FY28), with NBFC AUM CAGR 35% vs banks 30% — NBFC share rising to ~23% by FY28.
- **Why NBFCs are winning share:** NBFC gold-loan AUM already ~₹4 lakh crore (Mar-26; +54% CAGR FY26). Experian: gold-loan sourcing value +84% YoY in FY26; gold loans = 41% of retail-lending sourcing value (up from 30% in FY25, 20% in FY24); **NBFC sourcing-value share jumped to 44% from 33%** while PSBs fell from 45% to 37%. Average ticket size nearly doubled from ₹0.98 lakh (FY23) to ₹1.96 lakh (FY26).
- **Competition is intensifying:** the top-4 NBFCs' share of NBFC gold assets fell to ~70% (Mar-26) from ~90% (Mar-22). New entrants and diversified NBFCs are launching gold books organically (Cholamandalam, Piramal) or inorganically (Tata Capital via Yogloans; L&T Finance), with ~3,000 gold-loan branches slated for addition in FY27. Players in the pipeline include Aditya Birla Capital and Godrej Finance.
- **The catch — "gold rush" could cool:** HSIE cautions gold price gains may be peaking; a price correction/consolidation typically follows a surge year, extending break-even timelines for newer players and pressuring yields. NBFC yields remain ~200–250 bps below FY20–21 peaks. ICRA flags LTV/regular-repayment transition risk but expects limited credit losses given liquid collateral and strong auction recovery (92–106%).
- **Valuations:** HSIE initiated Muthoot with ADD (TP ₹3,020) and Manappuram with REDUCE (TP ₹310), reflecting Muthoot's operational moat (cost-to-income ~20% vs ~44% at MGFL; ROAA 6.7% vs 2.8%).
