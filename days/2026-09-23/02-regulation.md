### Lending against gold jewellery — LTV, bullets, and why a 12% Tier I exists

**What is it?**

A gold loan is a loan secured by pledged gold jewellery / ornaments. It looks simple (weigh, test, lend, keep the packet). RBI treats it as a **special-collateral product** because (a) the security is liquid and price-volatile, (b) the typical structure is a **bullet** (principal + interest at maturity), and (c) a gold-heavy NBFC can grow a very large book against a thin equity base if risk-weights and LTV are loose. So the rule-book is three stacked constraints: **how much you may lend against the gold (LTV)**, **how the loan must amortise (bullet vs EMI)**, and **how much capital you must hold against that book (CRAR / Tier I)**.

MFL is an **NBFC-ML** whose principal business is gold jewellery loans. Muthoot Finance is a **separate UL peer** — same product family, different company, different group, different layer.

**The Technical Details**

Home for the product rules is **RBI (NBFCs – Credit Facilities) Directions, 2025, Chapter IV — Lending against Gold and Silver Collateral** (updated through 2026). Capital sits in the **Prudential Norms on Capital Adequacy Directions, 2025** plus the gold-loan overlay in SBR. IRAC is the general 90-day machine (yesterday’s class) — gold is **not** carved out of NPA.

**1. LTV — tiered, continuous, and (for bullets) on the amount repayable at maturity** `[EFFECTIVE:2026-04-01]`

Previously: a **uniform 75%** cap, typically tested at disbursal.

Now (from **1 Apr 2026**):

| Total consumption gold-loan amount per borrower | Maximum LTV |
|-------------------------------------------------|-------------|
| ≤ ₹2.5 lakh | **85%** |
| > ₹2.5 lakh and ≤ ₹5 lakh | **80%** |
| > ₹5 lakh | **75%** |

Three operational teeth that trip FP&A models:

1. **Continuous LTV.** You do not get to test once at sanction and forget. If gold price falls and the ratio breaches the cap, you are offside *during the life of the loan*, not only at origination.
2. **Bullet LTV uses the amount repayable at maturity** (principal **+ accrued interest**), not the rupees disbursed. A 12-month bullet at 18% that looks like 80% LTV on day-1 can be >85% on the maturity amount. That is why small-ticket bullets need a **disbursal LTV buffer**, not a cap-hugging 85%.
3. **Valuation.** 30-day average price (IBJA or equivalent), physical verification, purity (XRF or similar). Hallucinated “today’s spot × 85%” is not the regulatory number.

Above **85% is not permitted**. Risk-weights on the standard book (unchanged in structure): **≤50% LTV → 35% RW**; **50–75% → 50% RW**; standard-asset provision **0.40%**.

**2. Bullet vs EMI**

| Loan amount | Repayment |
|-------------|-----------|
| ≤ ₹2.5 lakh | Bullet **permitted** (max tenure typically **12 months**; interest at least **quarterly**) |
| > ₹2.5 lakh | **EMI only** — bullet not allowed |

Renewals / top-ups (from 1 Apr 2026): only on **standard** accounts; for bullets, only **after accrued interest is repaid**; **no renewal** if LTV would be breached on the maturity amount. In a gold-price downcycle this is the NPA-manufacturing rule: you cannot evergreen a bullet that has eaten its LTV headroom.

Foreclosure: **7-day cooling-off** from sanction; charges only after that; disclose in KFS where digital-lending rules apply.

**3. Capital overlay for gold-heavy NBFCs**

| Parameter | Rule | Layer |
|-----------|------|-------|
| CRAR | **15%** minimum, ongoing | ML and UL |
| Tier I | **10%** standard ML | ML |
| Tier I if **≥50% of assets are gold jewellery loans** | **12%** | Activity overlay, including ML |
| UL extra | **CET1 9%** on top of 15% CRAR | UL only — **not MFL** |
| UL identification | Audited assets **₹1,00,000 crore+** `[EFFECTIVE:2026-06-24]` | Scoring repealed |

**Why 12% Tier I?** A gold book can be levered against liquid collateral that *looks* low-risk until a price shock + bullet bunching hit together. RBI’s answer is not “don’t do gold”; it is “hold more going-concern capital if gold *is* the business.” If MFL’s gold-jewellery assets are ≥50% of assets (they are, as a gold NBFC), **Tier I 12% is the binding floor, not 10%.**

**4. Auction / recovery — product rule + SC overlay**

Gold that is not redeemed is sold. That is a **recovery action**, so it sits under (a) the gold-loan auction process (notice, public auction, surplus back to the borrower) and (b) the **Responsible Business Conduct** recovery-agent rules (IIBF certification, 08:00–19:00, call recording — **[EFFECTIVE:2027-01-01]**).

The **16 Sep 2026 Supreme Court** Chola repossession judgment is **not a gold-loan circular**. It is a conduct overlay: recovery guidelines bind **now**, RBI was told to enforce them, and “we are waiting for 1 Jan 2027” is not a defence. For MFL that means auction **notice, hours, and agent conduct** on the gold book cannot wait for the RBC effective date.

IBBI’s 21 Sep PG circular is **irrelevant to the jewellery book** (individual, collateral-first). It matters for MSME / MHFL promoter PGs.

**Why It Matters**

- **NPA clock ≠ LTV clock.** 90-day IRAC still runs on a gold loan. A borrower can be standard on DPD and still be an LTV-breach problem — or the reverse. FP&A needs both meters.
- **NIM vs LTV buffer.** Hugging 85% on a bullet maximises origination yield and **minimises** the shock-absorber when gold sells off. That is a volume-vs-classification trade-off, not a free lunch.
- **Capital.** Gold risk-weights are friendly (35–50%) *inside* the LTV cap. Breach the cap and you have a permission problem, not just a RW problem. The 12% Tier I floor is the strategic constraint on leverage.
- **Jan 2027 + SC overlay.** Collections and auction vendors are a conduct risk *this festive season*, not a 1 Jan project.
