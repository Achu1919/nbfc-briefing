### 1. [ENFORCEMENT] KLM Axiva fined ₹2.70 lakh for not refunding gold-auction surplus

- **What:** RBI penalised a Kerala gold-loan NBFC for failing to pay auction surplus back to borrowers.
- **Issuer:** RBI (Department of Supervision / DoC)
- **Date:** Order **22 Sep 2026**; PR **24 Sep 2026**. Inspection as on **31 Mar 2025**.
- **Reference:** Press Release **2026-2027/1181**. Penalty under **s.58-G(1)(b) read with s.58-B(5)(aa)** of the RBI Act, 1934.
- **In Plain English:** When pledged gold is auctioned, anything realised **above** the loan outstanding (plus contracted charges) is the **borrower’s money**. KLM Axiva did not pay that surplus to certain borrowers. - **Impact on NBFCs:** Auction surplus is a **liability**, not other income. RBI inspects the cash trail: auction proceeds → dues adjusted → refund. “We could not find the borrower” is not a defence unless the public-notice path in para 60 has been followed and documented.
- **Muthoot Fincorp Note:** **High.** KLM Axiva is a Kerala gold-loan peer. Same product, same auction machine, same DoS inspection cycle. Para 66 of the RBC Directions gives a hard clock: surplus must go back within **seven working days** of receiving full auction proceeds. Today’s teaching is this rule.

### Transparency in auction procedure — why surplus is never “ours”

**What is it?**

A gold loan is a **pledge**. The ornament is the borrower’s asset, held as security. If the borrower does not repay, the NBFC may **sell** the pledge through a prescribed public auction and apply the proceeds to the loan. Anything left after dues is still the borrower’s. RBI wrote that as a **seven-working-day refund clock**, not as a courtesy. KLM Axiva’s ₹2.70 lakh penalty is DoS saying: we checked the cash, and some borrowers did not get their residual.

Two books, not one:

1. **Product / LTV** — Credit Facilities Directions, 2025, Chapter IV (when you may lend, how you value, LTV caps). RBI/DOR/2025-26/347, DOR.CRE.REC.No.266/07-01-008/2025-26, 28 Nov 2025, updated **15 Jul 2026**.
2. **Conduct at auction and after** — Responsible Business Conduct Directions, 2025, Chapter III **G** (paras 43–74). RBI/DOR/2025-26/362, DOR.MCS.REC.No.281/01-01-039/2025-26, 28 Nov 2025, updated **1 Jul 2026**. Applies to **all layers** of NBFC-D / ICC / Factor / MFI / IFC / IDF / **HFC** with customer interface. MFL is **in**.

Annex II of the RBC MD (“Lending against collateral of Gold Jewellery”) was **deleted from 1 Apr 2026**. The live text is Chapter III G, not the old annex.

**The Technical Details**

**G.1 Policy (para 43).** The Board gold-lending policy / SOP must specify, among other things: trigger event for auction; timeline for serving the auction notice; mode of auction; notice period for settlement before auction; empanelment of auctioneers; what happens if collateral is lost, deteriorated, or purity-discrepant; **fair compensation** and timelines.

**G.3 Documents (para 49).** The **loan agreement** (and KFS for charges) must describe: collateral, value, auction trigger, notice period, release timelines on full repayment, **refund of surplus**, assaying/auction charges. If surplus refund is not in the agreement, that is itself a gap — and it will not save you from para 66.

**G.5 Release after repayment (paras 58–59, 69).** Full repayment → return gold **same day, max seven working days**. Delay attributable to the NBFC: compensate **₹5,000 per day** beyond that. Unclaimed after reminders is a different bucket (G.8), not a reason to keep the packet quietly.

**G.6 Transparency in Auction Procedure (paras 60–66) — the KLM Axiva section**

| Para | Rule | Why inspectors care |
|------|------|---------------------|
| **60** | Adequate notice to borrower / legal heir to repay or settle **before** auction. Keep copy + acknowledgement. If untraceable after best efforts **and** a public notice, auction only after **one month** from that public notice. | “We had the ornaments in the strong-room already” is not a waiver of notice. Same logic as the 16 Sep SC Chola judgment, mapped onto pledge. |
| **61** | Public auction: advertisements in **at least two newspapers** — one regional language, one national daily. | No private / in-house “sale” to a jeweller friend. |
| **62** | Auction only through an **experienced/trained employee** or a **Board-empanelled auctioneer**. Employee-run auctions need surprise visits + internal audit. | Outsourcing MD still applies if you use an auctioneer. |
| **63** | Reserve price **≥ 90% of current value**. If auction fails **twice**, reserve may drop to **≥ 85%**. | Stops fire-sale that manufactures a “shortfall” and swallows surplus. |
| **64** | **First** auction: physical, **same district** as the lending branch. Only after first failure: adjoining district or online. | Stops shipping packets to a distant auction where the borrower cannot attend. |
| **65** | NBFC and **related parties shall not bid**. | Conflict of interest. Group jewellery entities stay out. |
| **66** | After auction: give the borrower / legal heir **full details** of value fetched and dues adjusted. **Surplus refunded within seven working days** of receiving full auction proceeds. Shortfall recoverable only per loan agreement. | **This is the KLM Axiva charge.** Surplus is a payable. Ageing > 7 working days is a breach, even if you “intend” to pay. |

**G.7–G.8.** Damage → NBFC pays repair (67). Loss / purity discrepancy → compensate (68). Unclaimed gold **> two years after full repayment/settlement** (71) → special drives to find the owner; **half-yearly** report to Customer Service Committee or Board (72). Absorbing unclaimed gold into inventory is how a conduct finding becomes an income-recognition finding (see ACRE ARC ₹27.30L on 11 Sep — RBI reads the income line).

**Previously:** older gold-loan circulars required a public auction and return of surplus, but the **seven-working-day clock**, **90/85 reserve-price floor**, **same-district first auction**, and **₹5,000/day delay compensation** are the 2025 RBC consolidation (live; Annex II deleted 1 Apr 2026). **Now:** DoS is sampling FY25 files against that text. KLM Axiva is the first gold-auction-surplus PR on this desk in the current cycle.

**Layer:** all layers with customer interface. Not UL-only. MFL (ML) and MHFL (HFC) are both in for gold/silver collateral conduct; MHFL’s SARFAESI path is a different statute but the “surplus is not ours” principle is the same.

**Why It Matters**

For a gold NBFC, auction is the **main recovery tool**. It is also the place RBI, the civil court, and the ombudsman all look when gold prices are high and surplus is large. A rising gold price **increases** surplus risk: more auctions clear above dues, more payables, more “we will pay when the customer walks in” ageing. That ageing is exactly what para 66 forbids.

FP&A angle:

- Auction surplus is **not NII and not other income**. Credit a payable; pay it; reverse only against proof of payment or a lawful unclaimed process.
- A cluster of unpaid surplus is a **contingent compensation + penalty** item (para 70: statutory compensation is without prejudice to other law — including a Chola-style court award).
- Inspection year in both of today’s NBFC PRs is **FY25** (position 31 Mar 2025). Assume MFL’s FY25 gold-auction files are in the same sample universe.

**Muthoot Fincorp Context**

MFL’s recovery *is* the auction packet. Practical checklist for FP&A / ops, using today’s PR as the test:

1. **Payable ledger, not “other income.”** Every auction since 1 Apr 2025: proceeds, dues, surplus, date proceeds received, date surplus paid, days elapsed. Anything > 7 working days is a KLM-style finding waiting to be sampled.
2. **Notice file.** Para 60 acknowledgement or, if untraceable, public notice + 1-month wait. Map this onto the 16 Sep SC overlay: possession of the pledge does not skip notice.
3. **Reserve price evidence.** 90% of **current** value at auction date (not the original pledge valuation). Two failures before 85%.
4. **Same-district first auction.** Packets should not debut in another city.
5. **Related-party bid ban.** Group jewellery / employee bidding is para 65.
6. **Unclaimed > 2 years.** Half-yearly CSC/Board paper. Do not net into inventory.
7. **Hero Fincorp side-lesson.** Interest-start date on bullets, foreclosure, and part-payment must match days outstanding. Excess interest is refundable; it is not “yield.”
8. **Muthoot Finance is UL and a different group.** Their auction disclosures are a peer benchmark, not MFL’s rulebook. MFL stays **ML**.

**Quiz yourself:**

1. Auction proceeds hit the account on Tuesday. The branch says the borrower will come next month to collect surplus. When is the last day you can still be para-66 compliant? What do you do if you cannot find the borrower?
2. First auction in Ernakulam (lending branch) fails. Can you run the second auction only online, at 80% of current value, with a group jewellery company as a bidder?
3. Hero Fincorp was fined for excess interest. A ₹2 lakh gold bullet is sanctioned on Day 0, disbursed on Day 3, foreclosed on Day 40. Interest was charged for 40 days from sanction. What is wrong, and which line of the P&L is it?

*(Answers: (1) Seven **working** days from Tuesday — not calendar, not “when the customer walks in.” If untraceable: para 60 public-notice path does **not** let you keep the surplus; it is still a payable, then unclaimed process under G.8 if years pass after settlement. (2) After first failure, adjoining district **or** online is allowed (64); reserve may drop only to **≥ 85% after two failures** (63) — 80% is a breach; related-party bid is banned (65). (3) Interest runs from **disbursement** (Day 3) to foreclosure (Day 40) = 37 days, not 40 from sanction; the extra 3 days is excess interest — a reduction of NII / a payable refund, which is the Hero charge.)*
