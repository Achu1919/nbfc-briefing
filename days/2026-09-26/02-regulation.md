### Personal guarantors to corporate debtors — the interim moratorium is off

**What is it?**

When a company borrows, the promoter often signs a **personal guarantee**. If the company defaults, the lender has two debtors: the company and the human. IBC Part III lets the lender file against that human (the “personal guarantor to a corporate debtor”). For years, **the mere filing** of that application froze other recovery against the guarantor — an **interim moratorium**. Parliament turned that freeze **off** from **26 May 2026**. High Courts then said the turn-off also covers applications **already pending**. IBBI wrote that down on **21 Sep**. Today’s II/108 only gives insolvency professionals more time to upload monitoring forms. It does not put the freeze back.

Think of three clocks, not one:

1. **IRAC clock** — 90-day NPA, provisioning. Unchanged. Pursuing a PG does not upgrade or downgrade the loan by itself.
2. **Recovery clock** — SARFAESI, arbitration, civil suit, IBC s.95. This is what s.96(4) unblocked.
3. **IP compliance clock** — PGIRP-1 to 6. This is II/108. Not yours.

**The Technical Details**

**Who is a “personal guarantor to a corporate debtor”?**
A natural person who has guaranteed the debt of a **corporate debtor** (a company / LLP that can go into CIRP). A gold-loan customer who pledged her own jewellery is **not** this person. A MSME promoter who guaranteed the firm’s working-capital or term loan **is**. A housing-loan co-borrower is usually a **co-obligor**, not a guarantor — read the agreement; do not assume.

**The 2026 amendment (the actual law)**

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 inserted **sub-section (4)** into **sections 96 and 124**:

- **s.96** = interim moratorium the moment a **s.95** application (insolvency resolution of a PG) is filed.
- **s.124** = the equivalent freeze in the **bankruptcy** process of a PG.

New s.96(4) / s.124(4): that interim moratorium **shall not apply** in respect of a personal guarantor to a corporate debtor, **with effect from 26 May 2026**.

**Previously:** file s.95 → all other proceedings against the PG stay, even before NCLT admits. That is why lenders parked SARFAESI / arbitration and waited. **Now (from 26 May 2026):** filing s.95 does **not** freeze those other proceedings. You can run IBC against the PG **and** enforce the guarantee in court / arbitration / SARFAESI at the same time.

**Retroactive to pending filings — the two High Courts IBBI relied on**

IBBI/II/106/2026 (21 Sep) quotes both, then instructs IPs:

| Court | Case | Date | Holding IBBI adopted |
|-------|------|------|----------------------|
| Bombay HC | *Tata Capital Financial Services Ltd v. Neel Motors LLP & Ors.*, Comm. Arb. Pet. 620 of 2021 | **24 Jul 2026** | “Is filed” includes applications **already filed and still pending**. Freeze on the PG **ceased 26 May 2026**. |
| Delhi HC | *IDBI Trusteeship Services Ltd v. Manish Jain & Ors.*, CS(COMM) 800/2025 | **19 Aug 2026** | s.96(4) is **retroactive** (“quasi retroactivity”) and applies to **pending** applications. Amendment Act dated **6 Apr 2026**. |

IBBI’s own sentence (para 4 of II/106): the interim moratorium under s.96 (and correspondingly s.124) **has ceased to operate with effect from 26 May 2026** in respect of applications pending before the Adjudicating Authority as on that date.

**What II/106 did *not* say:** it did not repeal the **post-admission** moratorium under **s.101** (once NCLT actually admits the PG insolvency). Do not tell the Board “all IBC stays against PGs are dead.” The stay that died is the **interim** one — the freeze that used to start on **filing**. If a PG IRP has been **admitted**, take specific legal advice on s.101 before launching a parallel suit.

**What II/108 did *not* say:** it did not move 26 May, it did not qualify the High Courts, it did not tell creditors to pause. It only moved the **IP form** deadline **30 Sep → 31 Dec 2026**, and the late-fee trigger with it. Forms PGIRP-1 to 6 were mandated by IBBI/II/92/2026 (6 Mar) and first extended by IBBI/II/104/2026 (9 Jul).

**The PG process, in order (so the clocks make sense)**

| Stage | Section | What happens | Moratorium? |
|-------|---------|--------------|-------------|
| FC (or PG) files | **s.95** | Application to NCLT against the personal guarantor | **Interim s.96 — OFF for PG-to-CD from 26 May 2026** |
| Interim RP | **s.97–99** | RP examines; report to AA | Still no interim freeze |
| Admission / rejection | **s.100** | AA decides | If admitted → **s.101** moratorium *may* apply (not covered by II/106) |
| Repayment plan | **s.105–114** | Plan, creditor vote, AA order | Plan binds |
| Failure → bankruptcy | **s.121 onwards** | Bankruptcy of the individual | **s.124 interim — also OFF for PG-to-CD** |

**IBC as a tool *for* MFL (creditor), not *against* MFL (debtor)**

- **Section 7 CIRP** against a corporate borrower: default threshold **₹1 crore** (s.4, as notified). MFL as financial creditor can file. Gold-loan customers are almost never corporates of this size.
- **Part III against a PG:** use when the MSME/corporate loan has a promoter guarantee and ordinary recovery is stuck. From 26 May you no longer lose SARFAESI by filing s.95.
- **MFL itself as debtor:** financial service providers are a **separate** track (IBC **s.227** + FSP Rules 2019; RBI is the regulator that must apply). That is not this morning’s circular and is not a live MFL issue. Do not mix it with PG recovery.

**SEBI overlay (why Saturday is “SEBI/IBC”)**

If MFL has listed NCDs, LODR **Reg. 52** (financial results for debt-listed) and **Reg. 51** (material events) still apply. Q2 FY27 ends **30 Sep 2026**; 45-day results clock for listed equity is **14 Nov**, debt-listed timelines follow the LODR chapter that actually applies to MFL’s listing. A material IBC filing **by or against** MFL is a disclosure event. A routine s.95 against a ₹2 crore MSME promoter is not.

**Why It Matters**

For three years the working rule in many recovery cells was: “don’t file s.95, it freezes everything else.” That rule is wrong from 26 May 2026, and IBBI has now told every IP in the country. If MFL’s MSME lawyers are still standing down “until the RP files PGIRP forms,” they are reading II/108 as if it were II/106. It is not.

IRAC is a different statute. Un-staying recovery does **not** let you upgrade an NPA. It **does** improve the chance that a provisioned MSME account recovers something from the promoter’s personal assets, which is a **cash** event, not a classification event.

**Muthoot Fincorp Context**

MFL is **ML**, gold-heavy. The PG overlay is a **MSME / MHFL** legal project, not a gold-auction SOP.

- **Gold:** borrower is the principal; ornament is a **pledge**. Enforcement path is RBC Chapter III G (auction, 7-working-day surplus — yesterday’s lesson) and the 16 Sep SC repossession overlay for any vehicle/hypothecation book. IBC Part III is the wrong drawer.
