## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. Market crash — worst day in months, NBFCs hit hard (Monday 28 Sep).** Driven by surging crude (~$107/barrel, US–Iran stalemate over the Strait of Hormuz after President Trump rejected Iran's 7-day ceasefire proposal), US 10-year yields above 5.2% (highest since 2004), and heavy FII selling. **Sensex plunged 1,124.02 points (−1.52%) to 72,771.72 — its lowest close since 30 March 2026**; Nifty fell 360.25 (−1.56%) to 22,780.25 (47 of 50 stocks down). Nearly **₹7.5 lakh crore of market cap was wiped** in a single session. Financials led the selling: Nifty Bank −1.99% to 54,471.65, PSU Bank index −3.24%, Private Bank index −1.51%. **Jio Financial Services hit a 52-week low, down 3.35% to ₹219.40.** Bajaj Finance and other NBFCs were among the day's biggest index decliners. FIIs sold a net ₹3,694 crore on Friday; a Geojit strategist flagged total equity exchange outflows of ₹25,682 crore so far in September. Rupee eased to 95.89/USD. *Implication for you:* expect gold-loan peers (Muthoot Finance, Manappuram) to open weak amid the risk-off, though gold strength is a countershock. Fast-moving situation — watch crude and FII flows.

**2. Gold-loan competition heats up + Jefferies doubles down on the sector.** The two themes that matter most to Muthoot Fincorp this week:
- **Jefferies (28 Aug note, reiterated 10 Sep):** added **Manappuram Finance (Buy, TP ₹430 ~34% upside)** to its India model portfolio as the preferred play on gold monetisation; kept **Muthoot Finance on Hold** (strongest franchise, but larger base = growth pressure if gold stays range-bound). India's household gold worth ~**$3.9 trillion (≈25,000 tonnes)**; organised gold-loan AUM at **$197 billion (Mar 2026), +73% in 2 years, ~7% of total bank+NBFC credit**. A 10% gold-price rise ≈ $400 bn of household wealth + $20–25 bn of fresh gold loans; FY26 gold-loan AUM growth alone contributed ~130 bps to GDP. **Only ~15% of household gold is currently monetised.**
- **New-entrant gold lenders grew 2.5x YoY to ₹61,900 crore (June 2026), 7.6% of retail gold loans**, and could add ~3,700 branches in FY27 vs ~1,500 in FY26 — essentially +20% of the top-4 NBFCs' branch network (Jefferies). **Tata Capital's 88.6% Yogloans acquisition** (₹318 cr valuation, ₹93 cr infusion; 162 branches across Kerala/Karnataka/TN/AP; completion expected within ~8 months, RBI approval pending) is the marquee entry.

**3. Tata Sons forced down the listing path — the biggest NBFC-sector corporate story.** RBI **rejected (letter dated 11 Sep)** Tata Sons' application to surrender its Core Investment Company (NBFC) registration (filed Mar 2024 after repaying ₹21,000+ cr of debt), directing it to comply as an **Upper Layer (UL) NBFC — including mandatory stock listing**. Under revised UL rules (effective 24 June 2026), the threshold is a clean **₹1,00,000+ crore of assets** — Tata Sons' ₹2.01 lakh cr (Mar 2026) puts it well over. The board resolved on 17 Sep to initiate the listing process; RBI has since written twice seeking a compliance roadmap. RBI is treating any entity-splitting to duck the threshold as "avoidance." Tata Sons is the only unlisted name on the 17-member UL list. A listing would settle the long-running Tata Trusts (Noel Tata, ~65%+) vs Shapoorji Pallonji (~18%) contest by force, and is a template for other large NBFCs' own listing clocks. *(Regulatory depth → NBFC Guru's 7 AM report.)*

## 📉 NPA & Asset Quality

- **ARCs pivoting to retail/NBFC stress:** retail-loan security receipts rose to **35% of FY26 ARC issuance** (vs 25% in FY25). **ARCIL acquired ~₹5,959 cr of assets in FY26 — non-bank financiers contributed ₹4,466 cr (~75%) vs banks' ₹1,493 cr.** In Q1FY26, ARCs bought **₹26,304 cr of bad loans (+56% YoY)**. Sector ARCs describe NBFC assets as "recent-vintage NPAs" with shorter redemption windows — a structural shift in resolution.
- **MFI/Microfinance asset quality** — improving at the front end, stress still building at the back: PAR 1-90 fell to **2.4% in Q3 FY26 (lowest since FY24)**; NBFC-MFIs at 2.1% vs banks 3.4%; but **PAR 180+ (incl. write-offs) at 19.3% and rising**, with the **sub-₹50k ticket segment at 33.5%.** Reported stage-3 improvements are partly write-off/ARC-sale driven, not genuine recovery.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Microfinance & SHG Lending (Monday)

**The sector has turned the corner — into a *measured*, quality-first recovery.**

- **SIDBI–Equifax Microfinance Pulse Report (Vol. XXVIII, Sep 2026):** industry portfolio outstanding of **₹2.69 lakh crore across 7.15 crore active loans** (as of June 2026). Portfolio **declined ~12% YoY**, but **fresh disbursements rose ~17% YoY** in Q1 CY2026 — a clear sign of cautious re-expansion after five quarters of consolidation. Higher-ticket lending is gaining ground: **₹75,000+ loans now 41% of disbursements.** Equifax framed it as lenders "prioritising portfolio quality and borrower discipline over aggressive credit expansion."
- **MFIN data (Sep 1):** an even stronger **43% jump in disbursements** even as portfolio contracted 6.9% YoY — re-affirming the rebound.
- **Rating-agency view (ICRA):** muted AUM growth in FY26, **rebound of 15–17% in FY27**; profitability (RoMA) seen at **1.9–2.2%** as credit costs moderate. 90+ dpd stabilised; early-bucket (0+) delinquency fell ~60 bps.
- **The caveat:** NBFC-MFIs' apparent NPA improvement is **heavily write-off/ARC-sale driven**; PAR 180+ keeps climbing (19.3% overall, 33.5% in sub-₹50k tickets). Recovery is **uneven** — originators with tighter underwriting and 30–60-day collection triggers are "separating from the pack."
- **Sociopolitical risks:** the **Bihar MFI Bill** (Feb 2026), upcoming state elections and farm-loan-waiver talk keep NBFC-MFIs exposed to collection disruption in Bihar (ICRA expects temporary impact). Banks increasingly channel microfinance **through MFI institutions rather than direct-to-borrower**, reclassifying MFI loans into retail portfolios (ET).
- **Watch list:** Arohan Financial is targeting a **₹20,000-crore portfolio under "Vision 2030"** (MD Manoj Nambiar), a proxy for resumed long-run MFI growth ambitions.
- **SHG angle:** with Bihar-Kerala stress easing but latch on waivers/elections, NBFC-MFIs and banks are expected to lean on **high-ticket, disciplined SHG/JLG lending** in FY27 rather than aggressive low-ticket expansion.
