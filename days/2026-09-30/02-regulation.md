### Gold-loan LTV, valuation, and auction surplus — Q2-close overlay

**What is it?**

A gold loan is a loan against **jewellery, ornaments or coins** — not against primary gold (bars/bullion), not against gold ETFs / gold MFs, and not against silver unless the credit policy expressly covers silver under the same chapter. RBI’s philosophy is simple: let a household monetise idle jewellery for short-term liquidity, without turning NBFCs into gold speculators.

Three numbers run the book, and they are **not** the same number:

1. **LTV** — how much you may lend against the metal.
2. **Valuation** — the rupee you put in the denominator.
3. **Auction surplus** — what you must pay the borrower after you sell the pledge.

Get any one wrong and you either (a) breach a hard prudential cap, or (b) write a cheque to RBI the way KLM Axiva just did.

**The Technical Details**

**Source of law (current):** *Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Credit Facilities) Directions, 2025*, Chapter IV – Lending against Gold and Silver Collateral (updated as on **15 July 2026**). Conduct overlay sits in the *Responsible Business Conduct* Directions, 2025. Capital overlay (do **not** re-teach today): gold-jewellery-heavy NBFCs hold **CRAR 15% and Tier 1 12%** where gold jewellery assets are ≥ 50% of assets [EFFECTIVE:2026-06-24]. MFL is **ML**, not UL.

**What you may lend against**

- Eligible collateral = jewellery / ornaments / coins of gold or silver, as the Board policy defines.
- **Prohibited:** purchase of gold in any form; loans against primary gold/silver; loans against gold ETFs / gold-MF units; financing speculation.
- Credit policy must set: single-borrower and portfolio caps, **maximum LTV**, what happens on an LTV breach, valuation standards, purity standards, and PSL documentation if you claim priority-sector.

**Valuation — the denominator [Credit Facilities, Chapter IV]**

Gold/silver is valued at **actual caratage**, using the **lower of**:

| Input | What you take |
|-------|----------------|
| (a) | Average closing price of that purity over the **preceding 30 days** |
| (b) | Closing price of that purity on the **preceding day** |

Source: **IBJA** or a **SEBI-regulated commodity exchange**. You do **not** pick yesterday because it is higher. You do **not** use London AM if IBJA/exchange is available. Physical verification and purity (XRF or equivalent) are mandatory at sanction.

**LTV — the cap [EFFECTIVE:2026-04-01]**

The old uniform 75% cap is gone. Consumption gold loans now sit on a **tiered** cap, and the cap is **ongoing**, not a day-1 photograph.

| Total consumption gold-loan amount per borrower | Maximum LTV |
|-------------------------------------------------|-------------|
| ≤ ₹2.5 lakh | **85%** |
| > ₹2.5 lakh and ≤ ₹5 lakh | **80%** |
| > ₹5 lakh | **75%** |

Two traps that bite at quarter-end:

1. **Ongoing LTV.** Price falls after disbursal → LTV rises. The cap must still hold. A 30 Sep close that only checks origination LTV is the wrong close.
2. **Bullet loans.** LTV is computed on the **total amount payable at maturity** (principal **+** interest that will have accrued), not on the amount disbursed. A 12-month bullet originated at 84% on principal is already over 85% on the maturity amount.

**Bullet vs EMI**

| Loan amount | Repayment |
|-------------|-----------|
| ≤ ₹2.5 lakh | Bullet permitted (typically ≤ 12 months) |
| > ₹2.5 lakh | **EMI only** |

Renewals / top-ups: only on **standard** accounts; for bullets, only **after accrued interest is paid**; **no renewal** if LTV would breach on the maturity date. That last rule is the NPA machine in a gold-price downcycle — the loan cannot be rolled, interest is unpaid, 90-day IRAC clock starts. (Yesterday’s teaching; do not re-open it.)

**Auction and surplus — why KLM Axiva is on the watchlist**

When the borrower does not redeem, you auction. The metal is the borrower’s residual property until the hammer falls. After costs and the outstanding, **surplus belongs to the borrower**. Keeping it is not “income”. RBI fined **KLM Axiva Finvest ₹2.70 lakh on 24 Sep 2026 (PR 1181)** for not remitting gold-auction surplus. That is a gold-NBFC conduct case, not a capital case.

Pair this with the **1 Jan 2027** recovery-agent directions (hours, IIBF, call recording) **and** the **16 Sep SC repossession judgment** (no RBI circular yet — enforce now). Auction **notice** + **surplus return** are the two gold-specific conduct items the Board should hear before January.

**Why SNFA tomorrow does not hit jewellery**

Specified Non-Financial Assets (IRAC 3rd Amendment, **live 1 Oct 2026**) are immovable / non-banking assets **taken over in satisfaction of a claim** (SARFAESI, bilateral, etc.). **Pledged gold jewellery sitting in the strong-room is collateral, not an SNFA.** Do not dump the gold book into the SNFA inventory. Do dump MHFL / MSME SARFAESI take-overs as at **today’s** 30 Sep stock-take (para 40C).

**MFI sidecar (Wednesday slot, short)**

A microfinance loan is a **collateral-free** loan to a household with annual income ≤ ₹3 lakh, FOIR cap **50%** of monthly household income (*NBFC-MFI Directions, 2025* / Credit Facilities Chapter V). Gold-backed credit is **not** microfinance — hypothecation of jewellery takes it out. MFL’s gold book does not sit in the MFI FOIR machine.
