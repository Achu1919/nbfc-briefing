## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. IRDAI's proposed insurance-commission caps threaten NBFC fee income — sector rattled.** The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) released its two-part consultation paper *"Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution"* on **23 September 2026**, and the shadow it casts over NBFC profitability is still the dominant sector theme. Proposed caps would slash credit-linked/credit-life commissions from current payouts of **42–67% (first-year)** and **22–57% (single-premium credit-protect)** to just **2% (single-premium)** and **2.5% (first-year, multi-year)**. Compulsory bundling of insurance with loans would be prohibited. Insurance commissions accounted for **3–25% of FY26 PBT** for select NBFCs (Kotak), so the hit is concentrated and significant. **Most exposed:** L&T Finance (~26% of FY26 PBT / 80bps of avg assets — Street's highest flag), Poonawalla Fincorp (17.8%), Cholamandalam (15.5–16%), HDB Financial and M&M Financial (~13.4% each). Least: Shriram Finance (~2.5%). NBFCs' credit-life book (~93% of their insurance business) is the target. **Why it matters to you:** any lender with heavy loan-bundled insurance book sees a potential 3–12% PAT drag if caps land as proposed. Comments to IRDAI close **25 October**; final rules likely FY2028, could be softened — this is not a done deal. **This is a REGULATORY story — full circular-level analysis is in NBFC Guru's 7:00 AM report.**

**2. Bajaj Finance board meets TODAY (1 Oct) to consider a major fund-raise.** The board will evaluate a capital raise via **QIP and/or preferential issue** (prior intimation filed 23 Sep; Quantum not disclosed). Jefferies estimates a raise of ~10% of FY28 net worth ≈ **₹15,000 crore**, which would lift FY28 BVPS ~7% with only ~1% EPS dilution. The stock sold off ~5% on 24 Sep to ₹991 — less on the raise itself, more on the IRDAI fee-income fears and dilution optics. Bajaj carries strong capital (CAR ~20.9% in Q1FY27) and keeps its ₹10-lakh-crore AUM ambition.

**3. Gold price rebound — relief for collateralised [gold-loan] books.** Domestic 24K gold rebounded **₹700 to ₹1,49,300/10g** in Delhi on 30 Sep (from ₹1,48,600), ending a two-session slide, tracking firmer global spot (~$4,190/oz). Note September was a corrective month: 24K fell from ₹15,708/g (1 Sep) to ₹14,894 (month-end), a **~5.2% drop**. Still, prices remain near historically elevated levels, which keeps gold-loan demand healthy even as it trims collateral-value tailwinds. Watch China golden week (from 1 Oct) for seasonal jewellery demand.
