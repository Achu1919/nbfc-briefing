## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. Brokerages turn defensive on NBFCs — Nomura flags up to 12% EPS cuts as funding costs rise.** In an Oct 1 sector note, Nomura said rising cost of funds could shave **1–12% off NBFC earnings for FY27-FY28**. Bond rates have already moved up **29–70 bps since the RBI's hawkish turn**, and H1 FY27 bond issuance was lower YoY for select NBFCs. Nomura's house view is for **two rate hikes in Oct–Dec 2026**; its scenario analysis implies a **1–8% hit to FY27 EPS** from that path alone. It is explicit that the **regulatory landscape is a bigger worry than funding** — i.e. this isn't just a cyclical call. Crucially for a margin-sensitive lender: the cost of new borrowing resets immediately while the asset book reprices with a lag, and the size of that gap (and the EPS hit) varies widely depending on fixed-rate mix. **Whom Nomura still likes:** diversified lenders **Bajaj Finance, Tata Capital and L&T Finance**; vehicle financier **Shriram Finance**; and housing financier **Aadhar Housing Finance (Buy; closed ₹436 on Wednesday)**. Separately, Nomura assumed the full 50–60 bps hike lands in **October 2026**, lifting cost-of-funds from Q3, with pass-through to end-customers only from FY28.

**2. Macquarie lifts 9 Indian financials — upgrades M&M Finance and LIC Housing to Outperform.** In an Oct 1 India Financials strategy note, Macquarie expects **~18% FY28 EPS growth for banks** (~15 bps margin expansion) and ~75 bps of rate hikes over the next 9–12 months. Upgraded to **Outperform**: Kotak Mahindra Bank (TP ₹500), Bank of Baroda (TP ₹280), PayTM, and — NBFC-relevant — **Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services and LIC Housing Finance**, plus ICICI Prudential Life. Upgraded to **Neutral from Underperform**: **Bajaj Finance, SBI Cards and Cholamandalam Investment & Finance**. Downgraded: **PB Fintech (Neutral from Outperform, TP cut 41%)**. Bank top picks: ICICI Bank (₹1,730), Kotak (₹500), SBI (₹1,220), City Union (₹265). Contrast: Nomura is cautious on the funding cycle while Macquarie is structurally constructive on banks' margin recovery.

**3. Market stress continues — Nifty falls 0.9%, 4th straight down day, worst Sept since 2018.** On Oct 1, the **Nifty fell 198 pts (−0.88%) to 22,421.95** and the **Sensex fell 571 pts (−0.79%) to 71,909.70**, with support watched near 22,200–22,000. September closed at 22,620.45, **down 6.1% — its weakest September since 2018** — leaving the Nifty **down ~13% YTD, on course for its worst year since 2011** (Sensex −15% YTD). Drivers: relentless FII selling, elevated US Treasury yields, a weak rupee and crude above $100/bbl on West Asia tensions. **For anyone at a finance company with market-linked exposure, this risk-off tape and the auto-sector sell-off (below) are the day's most relevant risks.**

**4. CBI files 2nd RHFL chargesheet — ₹7,429 crore alleged loan-diversion case.** On Thursday the CBI filed its **second chargesheet** in the Reliance Home Finance Ltd (RHFL) case, naming **11 accused including three senior Reliance ADAG executives** — RHFL Director & CFO Amit Bapna, Reliance Capital Group MD & Vice-Chairperson Amitabh Jhunjhunwala, and Reliance Power CFO Suresh Nagarajan — plus RHFL's statutory auditor Vijay Napawaliya, and **7 corporate entities** (Reliance Infrastructure, Reliance Power, Reliance Big Entertainment, Reliance Broadcast Network, Reliance Business Broadcast News Holdings, Reliance MediaWorks Financial Services, Kunjbihari Developers). RHFL allegedly borrowed from banks and **siphoned ₹7,429 crore through intermediary/conduit entities** diverted to ADAG group companies. It's the 7th chargesheet across the broader SC-monitored ADAG probe (9 FIRs; 7 arrests); 15 total accused in RHFL case.

## 🏛️ RBI & Regulatory — Headlines Only

- **RBI (NBFC – Resolution of Stressed Assets) 2nd Amendment Directions, 2026 — effective Oct 1, 2026:** bars NBFCs from selling Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFAs — immovable assets acquired against stressed loans) **back to the defaulting borrower or related parties** (IBC-defined); requires a 7-year max disposal window, board-approved acquisition/disposal policy, and excludes SNFAs from Gross/NPA/provisioning metrics. Legacy SNFAs must comply by Sept 30, 2027. *(Banks/SFB versions also effective Oct 1.)*
- **RBI NBFC IRACP 3rd Amendment Directions, 2026 — effective Oct 1, 2026:** income-recognition treatment for acquired SNFAs (accrued unrealised interest on extinguished exposure not recognised as income; reversal by Sept 30, 2027).
- **RBI (NBFC – Credit Facilities) Directions, 2025 (updated Jul 15, 2026):** consolidated digital-lending, gold/silver-collateral and project-finance framework (board credit policies, DLG rules, digital-lending LSP discipline).
- **RBI NBFC Responsible Business Conduct 2nd Amendment Directions, 2026 — effective Jan 1, 2027:** new advertising/marketing/suitability-and-appropriateness code for all customer-facing NBFCs (anti-mis-selling, consent norms, no bundling).
- **Draft: RBI NBFC Miscellaneous Supervisory Directions, 2026** — proposes Core Financial Services Solution (CFSS) akin to CBS for Upper/Middle-layer NBFCs with 10+ fixed-point outlets.
- **Microfinance:** ICRA upgraded its **sector outlook to Stable from Negative** (see Ratings section).

## 💻 Digital & Fintech

- **Digital NBFC personal loan book ₹1.54 lakh crore, +28% YoY (FACE/CRIF High Mark report, Oct 1):** digital NBFCs sanctioned **3.4 crore personal loans worth ₹64,656 cr in Q1FY27** — **70% of sanction volume but only 22% of sanction value**, underscoring the small-ticket digital model. Average ticket ₹18,802 (vs ₹70,025 for other NBFCs and ₹4.52 lakh for banks); industry average ₹61,112. Sanctions +14% YoY but just +2% QoQ; tilt toward larger tickets (60% of value in >₹50k loans; customers with 5+ yrs credit history). **90+ days-past-due stable at 1.4%.** Borrowers under 35 = ~58% of value; ~40% from Tier-III+; women ~18%.
- **UPI Merchant Discount Rate from Oct 15, 2026:** 0.4% MDR on person-to-merchant UPI transactions >₹2,000 (capped ₹300 on ≥₹75,000; flat ₹5 for essentials/fuel/insurance/telecom). ~96% of P2M transactions unaffected. ETBFSI notes the real risk for lenders is **behavioural** — if merchants alter payment habits, cash-flow-based lending models that rely on UPI as a sales proxy could see distorted data.
- **Credit Line on UPI (CLOU):** a new NLS white paper argues NBFCs — despite deep retail/rural penetration and SBR harmonisation — remain **excluded from participating in UPI credit lines**, proposing a calibrated "Financially Sound & Well-Managed NBFC" category to widen access.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Auto Finance & EV Lending (Friday focus)

- **September auto sales (released Oct 1) were the day's auto-sector catalyst — and they read mixed.** The Nifty Auto index fell **3.5%** as Maruti Suzuki, Eicher and Bajaj Auto disappointed headline expectations, even as aggregate numbers showed PV/CV strength. Detail: **Eicher/Royal Enfield +8%** (best-ever month at **1,33,958 motorcycles**); **Bajaj Auto total +5% to 5.38 lakh units** — but that's entirely export-driven (**exports +32%**, **domestic −9%**); **VE Commercial Vehicles +58% (10,479 units)** on strong domestic truck/bus demand; tractors under pressure. Weakness in domestic two-wheeler and tractor demand flows directly into the wheels of vehicle-finance books.
- **The financing angle for lenders:** the **CV cycle is the soft spot** — Choice expects CV AUM growth of just **~5.5% YoY** and flags a "modest inch-up in NPAs across CV financiers" tied to the West Asia conflict and fuel prices; Nomura still prefers **Shriram Finance among vehicle financiers** (largely insulated on its liability profile). Shriram's Q1FY27 shows **CV AUM ₹1.47 lakh cr (+19.4% YoY)** and **PV AUM ₹68,650 cr (+21.2%)**. NBFC credit data has retail/vehicle loans growing robustly but slower than gold/jewellery.
- **EV financing is the structural story:** RMI research (cited in an SMEStreet op-ed) identifies **NBFCs as key providers of organised credit to India's electric 2- and 3-wheeler market**, with commercial three-wheelers among the fastest-growing EV-finance segments. Context: **32 lakh+ EVs registered** under PM E-DRIVE phases, and **alternative-fuel passenger vehicles outnumbered petrol cars in August 2026**. Specialised NBFCs may hold the edge in first-time-borrower, small-fleet-owner and commercial 3-wheeler underwriting.
