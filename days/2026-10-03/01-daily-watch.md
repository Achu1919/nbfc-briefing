## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. The rate cycle is about to turn — MPC meets Mon 5 to Wed 7 October; a 25 bps hike to 5.50% is now the base case.**
The RBI's six-member Monetary Policy Committee meets 5–7 October, with the decision due **Wednesday 7 October at 10:00 AM** (Governor Sanjay Malhotra's press conference at 12:00 PM). This would be the **first repo hike since February 2023** (when the rate went to 6.50%). The repo has sat at 5.25% through the last four reviews, after cumulative cuts of 125 bps in 2025.
- **Business Standard poll:** 8 of 10 economists expect +25 bps to 5.50%. **Reuters poll (18–28 Sept):** ~60% expect +25 bps; a majority also expect another hike in December.
- **Barclays** (Aastha Gudwani, Amruta Ghare): first hike on 7 October, terminal rate 5.75%. **BofA** now expects 100 bps of cumulative tightening, taking the repo to 6.25% by H1 2027 (50 bps by December 2026, 50 bps more in H1 FY28) — doubled from its earlier 50 bps call.
- Triggers: CPI 4.82% in August (third straight month above the 4% target), WPI 9.92% in August, **Brent above $100–105**, rupee past 96 (now 96.3150), and a US Fed hike of 25 bps in September to 3.75–4% (its first in three years).
- **Why it matters for us:** every NBFC's incremental borrowing cost resets first and the asset book reprices with a lag. For a gold-loan-heavy, largely fixed-rate, short-tenor book, the transmission gap is shorter than for vehicle financiers — but it is still a squeeze.

**2. Nomura: funding-cost pressure alone can cut FY27–28 EPS by 1–12% for large NBFCs.**
- **AAA and AA+ NBFC bond yields have already risen 29–70 bps** since the RBI's hawkish MPC minutes of 19 August. AAA 3-, 5- and 10-year NBFC paper was at **8.2–8.4% as of 29 September**.
- Cost of funds for covered NBFCs rose **6–42 bps QoQ in Q1 FY27**. A 50–60 bps hike scenario takes **1–8% off FY27 EPS**, with the hit easing through FY28–29 as lending rates are reset. Structural EPS compounding stays healthy at **16–28% over FY27–29**.
- **The FCNR(B) rescue is not coming:** banks are expected to first use low-cost FCNR(B) inflows to cut their *own* cost of funds and then lend to mortgages and large corporates. Only select NBFCs get a pass-through in Q2 FY27; **if the hiking cycle starts in October, none of it flows to the covered NBFCs.**
- Bond issuance by **Bajaj Finance, L&T Finance and HDB Financial Services** has run below last year in H1 FY27 — meaning more of the FY27H2 funding will be raised into a higher-yield market.
- Nomura's constructive list: **Bajaj Finance, Tata Capital, L&T Finance** (diversified), **Shriram Finance** (vehicle), **Aadhar Housing Finance** (Buy, closed ₹436 on Wednesday). Macquarie, by contrast, upgraded six financials — including Bank of Baroda and Kotak Mahindra Bank — on an expected 18% FY28 EPS growth for banks.
- Nomura's sharper warning: **the regulatory landscape worries it more than funding** — specifically the credit-life insurance distribution reforms and the flexi-loan restrictions.

**3. Bajaj Finance board approves a ₹17,500 crore raise — the largest NBFC capital raise of the cycle.**
On 2 October the board approved **₹11,700 crore via a qualified institutions placement (QIP)** plus **₹5,800 crore of warrants on a preferential basis to promoter Bajaj Finserv**. The company will seek shareholder approval at an Extra Ordinary Meeting. The promoter warrants are designed to hold Bajaj Finserv's stake — and limit dilution — through the QIP. Read together with the funding-cost notes above, this is the market's biggest NBFC pre-funding its balance sheet ahead of the tightening cycle.

**4. The secured-credit shift is now visible in hard numbers.**
RBI sectoral data shows Indian households moving decisively to secured borrowing: **gold loans against jewellery rose 68.5% YoY in July 2026** (NBFC gold loan growth 68.5% versus a moderating pace at banks), while credit card dues and consumer-durable loans slowed sharply and personal loans kept growing. Gold lending — not consumer credit — is now the engine of retail NBFC growth. **Directly relevant to our book.** (Note: the same RBI Financial Stability commentary flags that much of the gold-loan surge comes from *existing* borrowers rolling over larger loans at higher gold prices, with lower LTV ratios in practice — worth watching as gold prices correct.)

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Infrastructure & Project Finance

*Saturday focus. Infrastructure lenders are, right now, the cleanest balance sheets in the sector — and the ones most exposed to the bond market as the rate cycle turns.*

**1. The state-owned infra finance map is being redrawn by two mergers.**
- **REC–PFC:** the PFC board approved the merger with REC on **28 June 2026**. REC shareholders get **88 PFC shares for every 100 REC shares**. The merged entity will have a **combined loan book exceeding ₹11 lakh crore** (₹11 trillion), will be dissolved-on-merger REC, must continue to qualify as a **Government Company**, and is expected to close **by 1 April 2027** subject to SEBI, NCLT and other approvals. PFC already owns 52.63% of REC from the 2019 government stake sale. The combined borrowing mix was ~**57% domestic bonds, 25% foreign currency, 18% domestic bank/FI borrowings**.
- **NaBFID–IIFCL:** the Centre is **reviving the five-year-old plan to merge NaBFID with IIFCL**, with momentum from the REC–PFC transaction. A combined platform would have a **lending portfolio of nearly ₹1.85 trillion** and generate **annual profits above ₹5,200 crore** — India's largest dedicated infrastructure financing institution. The stated aims: reduce overlap between the two government-backed institutions, strengthen lending capacity, and create a single platform able to mobilise domestic and overseas long-term capital. NaBFID FY26: balance sheet **₹1.44 trillion**, lending portfolio **₹1.15 trillion** (nearly doubled), **PAT ₹3,037 crore, zero gross NPAs**. IIFCL FY25: outstanding loan portfolio **₹69,904 crore**, record sanctions **₹51,124 crore**, disbursements **₹28,501 crore**, **PAT ₹2,165 crore**, net worth ₹16,395 crore, gross NPA 1.11% and net NPA 0.35%.

**2. The paradox: power-sector credit is booming, but the specialists are not capturing it.**
Bank credit to the power sector grew **23.8% YoY in Q1 FY27** (from 7.7% a year earlier), while system non-food credit growth rose to 18.3%. Yet **PFC's loan growth slowed to 4% YoY** (from 16%) and **REC's to 1%** (from 10%). Morgan Stanley responded on **21 August** by cutting PFC's target to **₹410 from ₹510** and REC's to **₹360 from ₹430**, moving both from Overweight to **Equal-weight**, and cutting FY28/FY29 EPS estimates by 5–7%. Its framing: the growth-and-margins trade-off means PFC and REC can defend share by pricing more competitively, but that limits margin upside and NII growth. Trigger for a more constructive stance on PFC: **loan growth returning to at least low double digits**. (Both books remain pristine — PFC gross NPA 1.11%, REC 0.23%.)

**3. New capital is coming in from outside the banking system.**
- **IIFCL secured fresh capital from a marquee development institution** to fund climate projects.
- **NaBFID is in the process of setting up a self-regulatory organisation (SRO) for infrastructure lenders**, per MD Raj Kiran Rai.
- **TPG-led consortium got CCI clearance to acquire Aseem Infrastructure Finance** — a sign that private capital sees value in the platform layer of infra lending.

**4. Why this matters to a gold-loan/diversified NBFC book.**
- The **RBI Project Finance Directions (June 2025)** created a harmonised framework for financing infrastructure and non-infrastructure projects by regulated entities, institutionalising stronger credit discipline, implementation monitoring and risk governance — and meaningfully higher provisioning through the construction phase. *(Detailed regulatory mechanics will be covered by NBFC Guru.)*
- **Competition for the same rupee is intensifying.** If the October MPC hike lands, bond yields at **8.2–8.4%** (AAA NBFC 3/5/10-year, as of 29 September) raise the cost of every long-tenor infrastructure loan — while the same tightening cycle lifts deposit rates and pulls household savings toward term deposits. For retail NBFCs the second-order effect is direct: **infra and project lending is not competing for our collateral, but it is competing for our funding.**
- The **National Infrastructure Pipeline** envisages over **$1.5 trillion** of investment across roads, railways, airports, ports, renewables, logistics, urban infrastructure and digital connectivity. Long-gestation projects need patient capital that commercial banks structurally struggle to supply because of ALM mismatches — which is precisely why NBFC/DFI balance sheets matter, and why their funding cost is a national policy variable, not just a company P&L line.

**Bottom line for the deep dive:** infrastructure project finance in India is consolidating into fewer, larger state-backed platforms (REC-PFC, possibly NaBFID-IIFCL), while private capital (TPG/Aseem) and development capital (IIFCL) step into gaps. Asset quality in these books is the best in the sector. The binding constraint is not credit demand — power-sector credit is growing at 23.8% — it is **funding cost and execution**, exactly the twin pressures now bearing down on the entire NBFC sector.
