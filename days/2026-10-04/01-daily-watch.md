## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. Muthoot Fincorp: Brickwork affirms BWR AA+/Stable and assigns AA+ to a fresh ₹3,600 crore public NCD issue.**
Brickwork Ratings' rationale dated **29 September 2026** confirms the group's credit standing ahead of the next fund-raise:
- **New:** BWR **AA+/Stable** assigned to **₹3,600 crore of public-issue NCDs** (to be raised in tranches of ₹200–500 crore; tenors 24/36/60/72 months; face value ₹1,000).
- **Reaffirmed:** ₹1,500 crore subordinated debt (AA+/Stable), ₹3,000 crore public NCDs (AA+/Stable), ₹300 crore sub-debt (AA+/Stable). **IPDI of ₹374 crore stays at BWR AA-/Stable** — the lower notch reflects capital-instrument features, and Brickwork says it expects **CRAR maintained at 18–20%**, with a positive trigger if CRAR moves above 22%.
- **Financials cited:** AUM **₹57,580.7 crore** as at Q4FY26 (vs ₹49,267.9 crore at Q3FY26, including off-book), of which the gold-loan book is **₹48,005.9 crore (~88% of AUM)**. **Q1FY27 revenue ₹3,159 crore, PAT ₹705 crore.** Asset quality is strong and improving: **GNPA 1.34% → 1.03% → 0.77%** (Dec-25 → Mar-26 → Jun-26) and **NNPA 0.69% → 0.57% → 0.40%**. **CRAR 18.06%** in Q1FY27 (18.71% in Q1FY26) and **gearing 5.78x** as at 30 June 2026 (6.10x in FY26).
- **Liquidity described as STRONG:** 12-month debt obligations Jan–Dec 2026 of ~₹7,980 crore against average quarterly collections of ~₹18,076 crore, plus ₹1,645 crore cash, ₹331 crore liquid investments and ₹1,655 crore undrawn bank lines. No cumulative negative ALM mismatches up to 3 years.
- **Watch items flagged:** ~55–60% of the portfolio sits in South India (geographic concentration) and ~88% in gold (price-volatility sensitivity); rating monitorables are GNPA <2%, NNPA <1%, and CRAR not slipping below 16.50%. Positive triggers: scaling beyond ₹1,00,000 crore AUM and GNPA below 1%.
- **Why it matters:** this is a ₹3,600 crore retail/HNI NCD shelf being rated into a bond market where **AAA/AA+ NBFC yields have already risen 29–70 bps** and the MPC may hike on Wednesday. The AA+ stamp keeps the issue marketable, but pricing will be the story.

**2. Bajaj Finance's Q2 FY27 business update (filed Saturday 3 October) is the strongest datapoint of the quarter so far.**
- **AUM ₹5.85 lakh crore, up 26.5% YoY** (from ₹4.62 lakh crore). **Customer franchise 128.85 million** (vs 110.64 mn). **New loans booked 13.45 million, up 11%.** **Deposit book ₹69,750 crore** as at 30 September 2026.
- Context from Q1 FY27: NII ₹12,571 crore (+23% YoY, marginally below the ₹13,102 crore poll), **PAT ₹5,986 crore**, GNPA **0.96%** (from 1.03%) and NNPA **0.39%** (from 0.50%), provisions ₹1,993 crore (including ₹296 crore of management/economic overlays).
- Combined with the board's **₹17,500 crore raise (₹11,700 crore QIP + ₹5,800 crore promoter warrants to Bajaj Finserv)**, the largest NBFC is pre-funding growth just as the rate cycle turns. Bajaj's own gold-loan push is a direct competitive datapoint for us: gold AUM was **₹21,200 crore in June 2026**, with a **₹30,000 crore FY27 target** and ~110 branches a month.

**3. Rate risk is now the sector's number-one overhang — decision due Wednesday.**
- **MPC meets 5–7 October; decision Wed 7 October at 10:00 AM.** Consensus: **+25 bps to 5.50%**, the first hike since February 2023 (Reuters poll: ~60% expect a hike; a majority also expect another in December).
- **Nomura (1 October):** cost-of-fund pressure alone implies **1–12% EPS cuts across FY27F–28F** for covered NBFCs; the 50–60 bps hike scenario takes **1–8% off FY27F EPS**. AAA 3/5/10-year NBFC paper at **8.2–8.4% on 29 September**; Q1 FY27 cost of funds already up **6–42 bps QoQ**. Nomura calls the **regulatory** landscape a bigger worry than funding (credit-life insurance distribution reform; flexi-loan restrictions).
- **Nuvama (3 October), the counterweight:** a hike alone is unlikely to trigger broad-based asset-quality stress. In FY22–24 the repo rose 250 bps while NBFC GNPA *fell* from 5.7% (Mar-22) to 4.6% (Mar-23). Current stress is "pocketed" in low-ticket unsecured personal/business loans, micro-LAP and CV/CE; capital and provision buffers plus abundant liquidity should cushion headline ratios. **El Niño is the wildcard** — impact would show with a lag via winter crops.

**4. Q2 FY27 business updates confirm the pivot to secured retail — and gold is where everyone is going.**
- **L&T Finance (3 Oct):** retail disbursements **₹24,000 crore (+27% YoY)**; retail book **₹1,34,500 crore (+29%)**; retailisation **99%**. **Gold Finance disbursements more than tripled to ₹3,230 crore** (from ₹983 crore); urban finance ₹10,320 crore (+27%); SME ₹1,820 crore (+24%); rural ₹6,350 crore and farmer finance ₹1,640 crore broadly flat.
- **Bajaj Housing Finance (filing dated 3 Oct):** gross disbursements **₹19,930 crore (+25.2%)**; AUM **₹1.58 lakh crore (+25%)**; loan assets ₹1.40 lakh crore (from ₹1.13 lakh crore).
- **M&M Financial (2 Oct):** Q2 disbursements **₹16,490 crore (+22%)**; business assets **₹1,46,300 crore (+15%)**; collection efficiency **97%**; **Stage-3 3.35–3.45%** (from 3.94% a year ago) and Stage-2 4.7–4.8% (from 5.8%); liquidity chest >₹16,200 crore.
- **Reading:** gold-loan competition is no longer a theoretical threat — a AAA diversified NBFC has tripled gold disbursements in a year while the market leader's yields compress (Muthoot's standalone gold yield fell ~300 bps QoQ to ~17.9% in Q1 FY27; NIM ~10.4–10.6%).

## 📉 NPA & Asset Quality

- **Nuvama (3 Oct)** — the sector's headline asset-quality read: a rate hike alone has historically not triggered broad deterioration (GNPA fell 5.7% → 4.6% through the 250 bps FY22–24 tightening; write-offs, better underwriting and provision buffers did the work). Current stress is confined to **low-ticket unsecured personal loans, business loans, micro-LAP and CV/CE**; **El Niño** and a prolonged tightening cycle are the two things that could widen it.
- **A loud caveat on reported NPAs:** Capitalmind's **Deepak Shenoy** warned that **low gross NPA ratios can hide heavy technical write-offs** — his example: a ₹120 crore problem on a ₹1,000 crore book shown as 2% GNPA after a ₹100 crore write-off, when effective defaults were 12%. He wants write-offs measured against the appropriate prior-year book vintage.
- **Where the write-offs are:** the microfinance clean-up is being driven as much by ARC sales as by recovery — e.g. **ESAF SFB sold ₹1,019 crore of NPAs to ARCs in FY26**, cutting GNPA from 8.54% (Sep-25) to 5.40% (Jun-26).
- **RBI's own numbers:** NBFC GNPA 2.9% and NNPA 1.0% at March 2025, with credit growth of 19.4% for FY25; MFI GNPA rose to 4.1% — the only NBFC category to deteriorate.
- **Microfinance: recovery, but a two-layer picture.** Industry **PAR 1–90 days fell to 2.4%** (best since FY24), with NBFC-MFIs at 2.1% vs banks' 3.4%; **PAR 180+ including write-offs is still rising at 19.3%**, and the **sub-₹50,000 segment is at 33.5% PAR 180+**. CRISIL expects the MFI recovery to help small finance banks' gross NPAs decline in FY27.
- **Structural change worth flagging:** banks are increasingly lending **to MFIs rather than to individual borrowers**, which reclassifies microfinance exposure into retail portfolios and flatters segment-level NPA optics.
- **Recovery infrastructure:** RBI's new SNFA norms (in force 1 Oct) formalise how lenders acquire and must dispose of assets taken over in settlement — a public-auction route, no sale-back to defaulters, and a 7-year outer limit.

**Three things that define the sector's position this week**

1. **Growth is real, but it is secured-credit growth.** Households are moving from unsecured to secured borrowing: gold loans against jewellery rose ~68.5% YoY in July 2026, and gold lending has displaced personal loans as the **second-largest retail asset class (15.7% of consumption loans)**. Volume growth is also increasingly price-driven — Muthoot/Manappuram tonnage has stalled at ~260–265 tonnes while loan values climbed.
2. **Everyone is now a gold lender.** L&T Finance tripled gold disbursements in a year; Bajaj Finance is at ₹21,200 crore gold AUM targeting ₹30,000 crore; Aditya Birla Capital wants 1,000 gold branches in three years; Tata Capital bought its way in; Cholamandalam plans 360 more gold branches and ₹5,000 crore AUM; Shriram wants to double gold to 5% of book. **Consequence: yields and NIMs compress for incumbents** (market leader's gold yield ~17.9%, NIM ~10.4–10.6%, up to 300 bps QoQ compression) while the RBI's April 2026 gold-loan framework (tiered LTVs, mandatory credit assessment above ₹2.5 lakh, standardised auctions) raises the compliance floor.
3. **The cost of money is turning against the sector right when growth needs funding.** Nomura's 1–12% EPS-cut scenario, rising bond yields, and record FPI selling (₹2.5 trillion in CY2026) all point the same way: balance-sheet expansion, not margin expansion, will drive FY27–28 earnings.

Large-cap NBFC results follow from mid-October; Bajaj Finance's QIP EGM and Shriram Finance's NCD issuance window (open to 31 October) are the market-access events to track.
- **Deep-dive watch-list:** the draft **flexi-loan/revolving-credit** restrictions (Crisil has already flagged impact on some product segments of large NBFCs), **credit-life insurance distribution reform**, **SNFA compliance** (legacy assets by 30 Sep 2027), **El Niño and the winter crop**, a possible **gold-price correction** hitting LTV headroom and repeat-borrower leverage, and **CGSMFI 2.0 utilisation** — only ₹8,000–9,000 crore of the ₹20,000 crore guarantee pool is expected to be used, with smaller MFIs still starved of funding.
