# 📋 NBFC Weekly Analysis — Week of 28 Sep – 4 Oct 2026

## Executive Summary
- **Markets bled into an expected rate hike.** The Nifty and Sensex logged their 8th consecutive weekly decline (Nifty −3.1% to 22,421.95, its longest weekly losing streak in ~25 years), dragged by FPI outflows, a 2.5-year-high 10-year G-Sec yield (7.21%) and crude above $100/bbl. The RBI MPC meets **5–7 Oct** with consensus now expecting a **25 bps hike to 5.50%** — the first since Feb 2023 — after August CPI hit 4.82%.
- **Cost of funds is the sector's #1 theme.** AAA/AA+ NBFC bond yields have risen 29–70 bps since the Aug hawkish turn; Q1 CoF rose 6–42 bps QoQ. Nomura sees **1–12% FY27–28 EPS cuts** from funding pressure across NBFCs and flags the **regulatory landscape** (insurance-distribution reform, flexi-loan scrutiny) as the bigger concern.
- **Brokerages stayed constructive despite the selloff.** Macquarie upgraded 6 financials (Kotak, BoB, M&M Financial, LIC Housing, ICICI Pru, Paytm) on 18% bank EPS growth in FY28; Nuvama argued a rate hike won't trigger broad NBFC asset stress (GNPA already ~4.6% vs 5.7% peak); Choice and Motilal Oswal previewed a strong Q2FY27.
- **Gold financiers diverge.** Manappuram's gold AUM almost doubled YoY and it targets 25–30% FY27 gold growth; Muthoot Finance's sharper NIM compression (~300 bps, to ~10.4%) has seen its stock lag (−24% CY26 vs Manappuram +13%). Capri Global is the preview-season darling (gold AUM +102.7% YoY).
- **Housing finance is quietly reshaping itself.** LIC Housing slashed FY27 AUM growth guidance to 8–10% but is pivoting hard into LAP/LRD and developer finance (project loans +400% YoY) to defend margins; Macquarie and Citi both upgraded the stock.

## 🏛️ Regulatory & Policy Watch
**Rate-hike pivot is imminent.** The MPC meets 5–7 Oct (decision 7 Oct). BofA, ICRA, SBI Research and Nomura all pulled their first-hike call forward to October, expecting +25 bps (to 5.50%) with a stance change to "withdrawal of accommodation"; Union Bank sees 5.75–6.00% by FY27-end. The case rests on August CPI of 4.82% (20-month high), oil >$100 (Indian basket ~$116/bbl in Sept vs $82 in July), rupee near 97, and the Fed/ECB/BoJ all hiking. **What it means for NBFCs:** CoF will inflect only with a 3–6 month lag because funding is term-loan heavy, so Q3/Q4 NIMs carry the risk, not Q2. The FCNR(B) swap that pulled in $127 bn in dollars is being used by banks to cut their *own* cost of funds first — so the liquidity windfall many hoped for is only partially passing through to NBFC liabilities.

**Risk framework is the under-appreciated story.** Beyond rates, Nomura flags regulatory headwinds: insurance-distribution reforms hitting credit-life (a fee-income lever NBFCs have milked) and RBI/Judicial scrutiny of flexi-loan structures. This is a second-order margin/fee story at exactly the wrong moment in the cycle.

**RBI liquidity management.** RBI announced a ₹2,00,000-crore variable-rate reverse repo auction for 5 Oct to mop surplus liquidity — reinforcing that even with FCNR inflows, the RBI is normalising rather than easing. For Muthoot Fincorp specifically: the gold-loan regulatory framework, LTV tiering and the prior-approval regime for branch expansion remain live watch-items as gold loan demand stays strong.

## 💳 Credit & Lending Trends
- **System credit growth eased to 18.1% YoY** (fortnight ending 15 Sep; released 2 Oct) from 19.1%, with aggregate deposits at +17.3% and M3 at +16.6%. Momentum remains strong, but the trend is rolling over as rates turn.
- NBFC-Retail (ex-HFC) AUM expected to grow **16–18% in FY27** (ICRA), with FY27 incremental funding needs of ₹4.2–4.4 trillion — bank flows covered ~45% of FY26 incremental needs and remain the anchor; market/ECB funding stays tight.
- **Term sheets are hardening:** AAA/AA+ 3/5/10-yr NBFC bond yields at 8.2–8.4% (29 Sep) are *above* yields on maturing NCDs, signalling refinancing at higher cost. September NBFC bond fundraising was strong (~₹300 bn) and front-loaded, as issuers rushed to lock FCNR-funded bank demand before an Oct hike.

## ⚠️ Asset Quality Deep Dive
- **Nuvama's key message (3 Oct):** a rate hike alone will NOT trigger broad NBFC asset stress — the FY22–24 cycle saw GNPA **improve** from 5.7% (Mar-22) to 4.6% (Mar-23) despite 250 bps of hikes. Stress is concentrated in low-ticket unsecured PL/BL, micro-LAP and CV/CE; it said deterioration will stay "pocketed and segment/player specific" unless hikes drag on with an external shock.
- El Niño/monsoon (SW monsoon ~13% below LTA, 4th-lowest since 2001) is a lagged rural-transmission risk — exposure is limited except some MFI lenders.
- **Microfinance is consolidating:** RBI data (Jun) showed MFI credit growing again after 7 quarters, with better collection efficiency and lower borrower leverage; Crisil sees SFB GNPAs falling through FY27 on MFI recovery. This is the asset-quality recovery story feeding the Q2 preview optimism.
- **HFC resolutions:** LIC Housing sold a ₹180 cr legacy NPA to NARCL for ₹140 cr in Q1 and expects further big resolutions through FY27 — healthy enforcement/ARC activity.

## 🌍 Macro Context
- **Growth is resilient but decelerating boundary:** IIP +8% YoY in Aug; RBI still projects FY27 GDP at 6.7% (some houses peg 7%+). But external drags — oil >$100/bbl, U.S. tariff actions, a 5%-plus U.S. 10Y, and ~₹2.69 lakh cr of FPI outflows in 2026 — are compressing valuations and pushing bond yields up.
- **Inflation trajectory is the pivot:** August CPI 4.82%, expected to peak in the festive quarter (Q3FY27 ~5.9% projected by MPC). Core remains benign (<3%), which is why some argue for a hold — but oil pass-through (OMC fuel-price hikes of ~₹8–9/litre flagged) risks broad second-round effects.
- **Funding-cost impact on NBFCs:** a 50–60 bps hike would lift CoF ~10–15 bps for most NBFCs and cut FY27 EPS 1–8%; with an FY28 pass-through, the impact fades. Rising-yield regime materially raises the bar for new-gold/growth pricing — directly relevant to gold-NBFC NIMs.

## 💡 Analyst's Take
**The surface noise this week was a risk-off equity tape; the underlying signal is an orderly funding-cost transition, not a credit event.** Eight straight weekly declines and a 2.5-year-high 10-year yield look alarming, but the selloff is macro-led (global yields, FPI rotation, rupee) rather than NBFC-fundamental-led. The broker-bullish consensus is striking: Nuvama's historical evidence (NBFC GNPA fell through the FY22–24 hiking cycle), ICRA's credit-cost downgrade, positive Q2 previews and Macquarie's upgrades all point to an asset-quality-led earnings upgrade cycle that is intact. The real risk to that narrative is not a 25 bps hike — it is a *prolonged* tightening that tips vulnerable segments (unsecured PL/BL, micro-LAP, CV/CE) and squeezes NIMs as liability costs out-run asset repricing.

**Three trends are accelerating beneath the noise.** (1) *Margin protection is now the strategy of record* — LIC Housing is restructuring around LAP/LRD and developer finance; diversified NBFCs lean on floating-rate asset mixes and operating leverage. (2) *The credit-cost outlook is turning friendlier* — collections up, write-offs high, fresh slippage moderating — which is what underpins estimate upgrades and (per Macquarie) an 18%-EPS FY28 bank/sector view. (3) *Distribution is becoming the decisive competitive asset* — ULI, co-lending, and fintech partnerships let balance-sheet-light players grow without taking rate-cycle duration risk.

**For Muthoot Fincorp's leadership, four things matter this week.** First, brace the ALM/debt calendar for a 25 bps repo hike: term funding costs will inflect with a 3–6 month lag, so opportunistically locking FCNR-supported bank lines before Oct matters. Second, gold is the portfolio's strongest horse, but the *yield* cycle is turning — Muthoot Finance's ~300 bps Q1 NIM compression is the sector canary for pricing power; watch whether new-gold and balance-transfer pricing hold as competitive intensity rises. Third, the regulatory agenda (insurance-distribution reform hitting credit-life fees, flexi-loan scrutiny, gold-loan LTV/branch rules) directly touches earnings levers FinCorp has relied on — treat compliance readiness as a P&L item, not a back-office cost. Finally, in a rising-rate, FPI-negative tape, cheap, high-ROE, secured book-value (Muthoot trades ~9–10x, ~20%+ CRAR) is a defensive allocation story — the contrarian read is that the crowd is selling exactly the resilient asset. The sector is being repriced *down* on rates before earnings *upgrade* on credit appears; leadership that manages funding cost through the lag window and keeps gold pricing stable will look cheap by Q3.
