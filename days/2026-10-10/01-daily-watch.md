# 📊 NBFC Daily Watch — Saturday, 10 October 2026

*Covering: Friday 09 Oct 2026 (market close) + overnight developing news. Published Sat 10 Oct morning.*

## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

1. **RBI ended its 3-year rate-cut era — repo hiked 25 bps to 5.5%, stance turned to "calibrated tightening."** The MPC unanimously raised the repo rate to 5.5% (Oct 7), first hike since early 2023, and FY27 GDP growth forecast was raised to 7.1%. Governor Malhotra signalled near-term rate cuts are off the table. **Why it matters at Muthoot:** NBFC funding costs are repricing faster than loan yields (CareEdge), squeezing net interest margins — especially for wholesale-funded lenders with slower-repricing portfolios (vehicle finance, microfinance, gold, small-ticket consumer). Jefferies flags LIC Housing, Bajaj Housing and Aditya Birla Capital as better-placed (higher floating-asset exposure), while Mahindra Finance and SBI Cards are more exposed. Housing lenders with fixed liabilities may actually gain.

2. **RBI spinning off an NBFC "credit-facility" bomb — revolving-credit ban still live.** Governor Malhotra (post-policy, Oct 7) called the Aug-6 draft that would limit NBFCs to term loans a *"clarification," not a new rule*, but said final directions are still being assessed at board level and could affect products worth **over ₹2 lakh crore**. NBFCs and FISME warn a blanket ban on flexi/revolving/OD lines could choke MSME working-capital finance. Final rule not yet issued — watch for it.

3. **GST fitment committee recommended 18% GST on NBFC services to banks in co-lending arrangements** (interest stays exempt; taxable value aligned to RBI methodology). The 57th GST Council met Oct 8 but the co-lending levy was a committee recommendation — no confirmed final approval yet; a Finance Ministry circular would follow if cleared. Analysts (Finbox/The Pattern) expect NBFCs to keep a **bigger slice of each co-lending loan** (more of their own capital) to dodge the tax, pressuring thinly-capitalised fee-driven lenders.

4. **NBFC credit re-accelerated — gold loans are the rocket fuel.** RBI Sectoral Deployment data (released Oct 6) showed NBFC credit +15.8% YoY to ₹60.64 lakh cr in August (vs 10% a year ago); retail +22%; **gold loans +69.1% YoY to ₹3.65 lakh cr**; consumer durables +56.4%; housing +12.1%. Gold prices trading near ₹1.5 lakh/10g. RBI's June Financial Stability Report flags that a prolonged gold-price correction could weaken collateral and raise delinquencies.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — *Infrastructure & Project Finance* (Saturday rotation)

- **Capital relief is here for quality infra lending.** RBI's finalised (Jan 2026) framework for NBFC infrastructure loans **lowers capital requirements** on lending to *operational, high-quality* projects by linking risk weights to project performance and repayment history — a major relief flagged by Morgan Stanley for infra-focused NBFCs. Earlier it had (Oct 2025) proposed risk-weight cuts, then eased them post-feedback while keeping lender-protection norms.
- **But concentration is being reined in.** In Aug 2026 RBI brought **Upper-Layer IDF-NBFCs under the same exposure limits as infrastructure finance companies** — tightening large-exposure rules to curb concentration risk. Latest Sept 2026 updates sharpen the large-exposure regime for IDF-NBFCs.
- **Infra lending is the soft spot in the credit data.** RBI showed NBFC infrastructure credit grew just **5.1%** in June 2026 (vs 9.7% a year ago) — the reason industrial credit growth moderated to 6.7%. In Aug 2026, aggregate NBFC industry credit slowed to 8.4% while retail (22%) and services (16.2%) outpaced it. Commercial real estate was described as "buoyant."
- **State-owned infra financiers are restructuring for scale.** PFC and REC (both now in the RBI Upper Layer) are merging into a ~₹11 lakh cr power-sector financer; HUDCO, IRFC, REC, PFC are the state NBFCs most exposed to the shift to an **asset-size (>₹1 lakh cr) Upper-Layer test** and to RBI's ownership-neutral push to include government-owned NBFCs in the Upper Layer.
- **Institutional backdrop:** NaBFID remains the dedicated long-tenor infra DFI under the National Infrastructure Pipeline; funding cost pressure post the 25-bps hike is a live concern for long-dated infra books because their assets reprice slowly.
- **Why it matters:** Construction-equipment financing (a proxy for infra activity) is still ~31% below its peak for Shriram; project/infra finance lenders face a genuine tension — cheaper capital for good projects versus tighter concentration limits and rising wholesale funding costs.
