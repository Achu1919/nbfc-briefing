## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. Muthoot Fincorp gets CARE AA; Stable assigned to ₹2,800 crore of new debt — but sits one notch below Brickwork's view**
CareEdge Ratings (CARE Ratings Ltd) assigned **CARE AA; Stable** to **₹1,200 crore subordinated debt** and **₹1,600 crore non-convertible debentures** of Muthoot Fincorp Ltd in its rationale dated **5 October 2026** — both were *assigned* (new), not reaffirmed.
- Standalone gearing **6.16x** with net worth **₹7,899 crore** as on **30 June 2026** (vs 6.53x / ₹6,679 crore at 31 March 2026).
- Standalone **CAR 18.06%** as on 30 June 2026 (CareEdge reports 18.00% CAR with Tier-1 13.20% at FY26-end vs 19.50%/15.85% at FY25-end — i.e. capital has been drifting down, which is exactly why the IPO matters).
- The rationale explicitly leans on the **₹3,000 crore fresh-equity IPO** (DRHP filed with SEBI in August 2026) plus internal accruals to repair gearing. Negative rating triggers: consolidated ROMA below 2.50% sustained, CAR below 18% sustained, material asset-quality deterioration.
- **Watch the split:** Brickwork rates the same issuer **BWR AA+/Stable** (reaffirmed/assigned on ₹8,774 crore of instruments on 29 September), while Carey rates it **AA** and CRISIL rates it **AA/Stable** (reaffirmed 17 September). A one-notch gap between agencies on the same credit is worth flagging internally — it affects pricing on any issue placed across the investor base.
- Also on 6 October, ICRA assigned **Provisional [ICRA]AA+(SO)** to Alpha 2026 PTC Series A1 (₹42.13 crore) and **Provisional [ICRA]AA−(SO)** to Equity PTC (₹2.49 crore), backed by a pool of unsecured QR-code-based merchant loans originated by the group. That is a securitisation of the small-business/merchant book — evidence the group is monetising non-gold assets.

**Why it matters for us:** this is the parent's funding cost being set for FY27. Muthoot Fincorp's cost of funds shows up directly in the benchmark we are judged against.

**2. RBI MPC hiked the repo rate 25 bps to 5.50% and switched the stance to 'calibrated tightening'**
The six-member MPC voted **unanimously** on **7 October 2026** to raise the repo rate to **5.50%** from 5.25%, and changed the stance from *neutral* to ***calibrated tightening***. FY27 GDP growth forecast was raised to **7.1%**. Governor **Sanjay Malhotra** flagged "rising financial risks" and warned against complacency, with the West Asia conflict adding to inflation pressure.
- **The market has already priced more:** 10-year G-sec yields are up **25–40 bps since August**, and 3- and 5-year **AAA NBFC bond yields are up 50–60 bps since June**. Kotak's economist expects an additional **50–75 bps** of hikes; Emkay and JM Financial both model ~50 bps more in H2FY27.
- **Transmission to NBFCs:** funding cost is now *the* swing factor for H2FY27 earnings. Brokerages expect NBFC/HFC spreads to compress **5–15 bps** on stable-to-moderating asset yields.

**3. PNB Housing Finance upgraded to Crisil AAA — now AAA across all four agencies; management flags 10–15 bps funding benefit**
CRISIL upgraded PNB Housing's long-term bank facilities and debt instruments to **Crisil AAA/Stable** from AA+/Stable (rationale **6 October 2026**), reaffirmed short-term at **Crisil A1+**, and withdrew the rating on ₹500 crore of NCDs that have been redeemed.
- Instruments upgraded: ₹9,000 crore long-term bank facilities, ₹3,410 crore NCDs, ₹20,000 crore fixed deposits, ₹500 crore lower Tier-II bonds. Commercial paper of ₹26,000 crore reaffirmed A1+.
- The upgrade is driven by a **change in Crisil's analytical approach** to explicitly factor in support from **Punjab National Bank** — sole promoter — which has stated intent to provide "unconditional need-based timely funding support in case of exigency."
- Balance sheet: AUM ₹93,021 crore (30 Jun 2026), housing + LAP = ~99.5% of book, **gross Stage III just 0.9%**, **CAR 28.3%**, net worth ₹19,794 crore, FY26 PAT ₹2,291 crore.
- MD & CEO **Ajai Kumar Shukla** told Moneycontrol the lender expects a **10–15 bps cost-of-funds benefit over the long term**, does not plan fresh capital, and sees the 25 bps repo hike having **limited impact on housing demand**. Total borrowings were ₹73,699 crore at 30 June 2026 (term loans 38%, deposits 24%, NHB refinance 16%). Shares rose 2.8% to ₹1,133 on the news.

**4. GST Fitment Committee recommends 18% GST on NBFC services to banks in co-lending arrangements**
Reported 7–8 October 2026: the GST Fitment Committee has recommended **18% GST on the taxable service supplied by an NBFC to a bank** under a co-lending arrangement. **Interest on the underlying loans remains exempt.** The valuation of the NBFC's service will be aligned with the **RBI-prescribed methodology** — which is the key bit, because it resolves the long-running dispute over what the taxable "service fee" actually is.
- **Status: proposal, not law.** The **57th GST Council meeting (8 October 2026)** made no rate changes; the Council's decisions were on arrest powers, penalties, refunds, ITC and registration timelines. This item needs a Council decision before it bites.
- **Why it matters:** co-lending is a material share of how mid-size NBFCs fund retail books. An 18% levy on the NBFC's share of the spread compresses net co-lending economics unless it is passed through.

**5. RBI cancelled the Certificate of Registration of 13 NBFCs (press release 9 October; orders dated 7–21 September)**
RBI cancelled CoRs under **Section 45-IA(6) of the RBI Act, 1934** for 13 companies — **11 registered in West Bengal**, 2 in Tamil Nadu — barring them from carrying on NBFC business. A further **10 NBFCs surrendered** their registrations. Named companies include Nakodar Finance Private Limited (Chennai), Ajitnath Steels, Astrol Dealcom, Bhagyashri Trading, Booh Finance Company, Britex Financial Services and Sajili. **None of the names touch the large listed NBFC universe** — this is a cleanup of small/dormant shells, plus JPT Securities' voluntary surrender (negative net worth, nil income).
- **Read:** regulatory hygiene action, not a sector credit event. But the *pattern* — 13 cancellations + 10 surrenders in one release — tells you RBI is actively pruning the register.

**6. Bajaj Finance's ₹17,500 crore capital raise is the single biggest signal of the week**
Alongside its Q2FY27 business update, the board approved a **QIP of up to ₹11,700 crore** and a **preferential issue of warrants up to ₹5,800 crore** (₹17,500 crore total), subject to approvals. When the largest NBFC in the country raises equity at this scale while its AUM is growing 26.5%, it tells you management expects the growth window to stay open and wants to be funded for it.

## 💻 Digital & Fintech

**UGRO Capital taps USD debt markets**
UGRO Capital's Investment and Borrowing Committee approved (resolution by circulation, 9 October 2026) allotment of up to **1,000 senior, unsecured, rated, listed, USD-denominated NCDs of USD 10,000 face value each — USD 10 million total** — via private placement at **450 bps over Term SOFR**, 48-month tenure, maturing 27 September 2030 (pay-in 29 September 2026). A mid-tier, MSME-focused NBFC accessing dollar funding is a meaningful diversification signal.

**Co-lending tax clarity (see Urgent section)**
The GST Fitment Committee's recommendation to levy **18% GST on the NBFC's service to a bank** in co-lending, with **interest exempt** and **valuation aligned to RBI methodology**, is the most important pending development for digital/embedded lending economics. Final decision rests with the GST Council; the 57th Council meeting (8 Oct) did not take it up as a rate decision.

**Industry consensus from ETBFSI NBFC Leaders' Retreat 2026 (Lonavala)**
Industry leaders argued NBFCs must **prioritise governance, profitability and technology integration over aggressive loan growth** to build sustainable lending models. Reporting line: "The next chapter of NBFC growth will not be defined by scale alone, but by the quality of decisions behind it." The CEO Forum brought together leaders from lending, housing finance, microfinance and technology.

**Structural data point (TransUnion CIBIL + FIDC)**
Of all loans originated in India, **82% were below ₹2 lakh by number** as of June 2026. **NBFCs accounted for 47%** of those originations, versus **17% for banks** and 36% for other lenders. Nearly **97% of all originations** were below ₹10 lakh by number. Credit-monitoring borrowers cured at **52%** vs **48%** for non-monitors. This is the clearest available picture of where NBFCs actually sit in the credit stack — and why the digital/small-ticket rails matter so much.

**RBI digital lending framework still bedding in** — LSP rules, Key Fact Statement requirements, DLA registration and the 2026 transparency/fair-practice changes remain the operating compliance perimeter for any NBFC-fintech partnership.

NBFC NIM moved **up** even as bank NIM eased (3.22% vs 3.26%) — a real divergence. RBI's summary: NBFC parameters "are also sound."

**Governor Sanjay Malhotra, 7 October post-MPC press conference**
"We don't see any issue regarding asset quality for NBFCs amid high liquidity conditions in the banking system." **Where the pockets of stress are**
- **Nuvama (October report):** an RBI rate hike alone is **unlikely to trigger broad-based NBFC asset-quality stress** — during the FY22–24 tightening cycle, GNPAs *fell* from 5.7% (Mar 2022) to 4.6% (Mar 2023) despite 250 bps of hikes. ## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Sunday: Weekly Preview + Sector Health Check

### Health check: the sector enters H2FY27 in good shape, with one clear vulnerability

**Growth — strong, and accelerating**
- **NBFC credit outstanding ₹60.64 lakh crore**, **+15.8% YoY in August 2026** — versus just **+10% a year earlier**. Provisional RBI data.
- **Retail credit grew faster at +22% YoY.** Within retail: **gold loans +69.1%**, vehicle finance +15%, housing +12%.
- **Services sector lending within NBFC books contracted** — a possible early signal of corporate/services borrowers migrating to bank credit. Mix improving in quality terms, but it narrows top-line growth.
- HDFC Securities (sector update, 9 Oct 2026): NBFC credit **₹61 trn o/s**, Aug-26 **+16% YoY**; retail is **44%** of NBFC credit; gold loans described as growing at "astronomical" industry levels (**+62% YoY in Jun-26**).
- **Structural driver:** NBFCs now write nearly **half of India's sub-₹2 lakh new-to-credit loans** (47% of originations, vs 17% for banks). ~11 crore borrowers have active NBFC relationships. NBFC customer growth is running ~2.5x the broader credit industry.

**Asset quality — better than it has been in years**
- NBFC gross NPA **2.50%** (from 3.09%), net NPA **0.84%** (from 1.00%); RoA **3.37%** (from 3.01%); NIM **5.39%** (from 4.99%); CAR **25.50%**. RBI: parameters "sound."
- Retail delinquency **1.1%**, below the industry's 1.3%.
- Reported Q2FY27 updates are uniformly stable-to-improving: CreditAccess PAR 90+ at a **10-quarter low of 1.1%**; MMFS GS2+GS3 ~8.2% improving with 97% collection efficiency; Can Fin GNPA below 1%; Bajaj Housing and Shriram steady.

**Profitability — strong headline, shrinking margin**
- Brokerage consensus: **PAT +34% YoY** (Jefferies, ex-Piramal/IIFL) to **+41.8% YoY** for 7 NBFCs (Nirmal Bang, ₹14,210 crore). AUM +20% (JM Financial). Sector ROA ~3%, ROE 15.1%.
- **But margins are compressing 5–15 bps** (Emkay) as cost of funds rises. The cushion has been that a falling cost of funds through FY26 more than offset falling asset yields; that cushion is now exhausted and reversing.

**Funding — this is the vulnerability**
- Repo **+25 bps on 7 October** with the stance shifted to *calibrated tightening*; brokers model **another ~50 bps (Kotak: 50–75 bps)** in H2FY27.
- **AAA NBFC 3- and 5-year bond yields are up 50–60 bps since June**; the 10-year G-sec is up 25–40 bps since August.
- It hits everyone, but **hits prime/AAA issuers on the incremental side first**, per Kotak. **Shriram Finance is the exception** — its recent rating upgrade means incremental borrowing costs are coming down.
- **Countervailing factor:** HFCs get immediate asset repricing upward (repo-linked loans) — Bajaj Housing and LIC Housing are the named beneficiaries.
- **Relief valve:** **NHB is planning to double refinance** to the Affordable Housing Fund and HFCs in FY27, after disbursing ₹32,308 crore of refinance in the year to June 2025 (including ₹5,791 crore from the Affordable Housing Fund). Several large HFCs asked for additional funds; a decision may be approved soon. NHB refinance could rise to **30–35% of total borrowings** for some major HFCs, from 20–25% last year. Beneficiaries named: LIC Housing, Bajaj Housing, PNB Housing, Aadhaar Housing, TrueHome.

**Sector composition notes**
- **HFCs:** HFCs hold **₹7.44 lakh crore of personal home loans** (end-July 2026) — **18.6%** of the ₹40.07 lakh crore market, up from 18.3% a year ago. Total HFC liabilities to the financial system were ₹7.35 lakh crore at March 2026 (RBI FSR). **Most margin pressure in the sector is on HFCs**, which are "reeling under competitive pressure" (Kotak). Bank share of HFC funding is rising while mutual fund and insurance share declines.
- **Gold loans:** the fastest-growing major segment (+69.1% YoY). This is both the sector's biggest tailwind (collateral values) and its most obvious concentration risk — a gold price correction would create collateral-adequacy pressure capable of triggering fast portfolio deterioration. Muthoot Finance and Manappuram Q2FY27 earnings are the granular read on gold loan yields and NPA quality; both are pending.
- **Microfinance:** the sector's loan book was **₹3.34 trillion, down 11% in FY26** (Sa-Dhan *Bharat Microfinance Report 2026*), with loan accounts down 21% to **10.40 crore**. But the current book has healed sharply: **PAR 30–179 days fell to 2.34% from 6.63%**, and PAR 90–179 to 1.45% from 3.92%. **Legacy stress worsened**: PAR 180+ days rose to **17.04%** from 10.67% — an ageing pool of old defaults. Guardrails worked: **92.6%** of unique active borrowers now have ≤2 lenders (from 90.5%), and borrowers with 5+ lenders collapsed to **0.1%** from 1.6%. **ICRA upgraded its microfinance sector outlook to stable**; expected credit costs falling from a **7.1% peak in FY25 to 2.4–2.8% in FY27**.
  - **Caution flags:** ANI (9 Oct) — the sector "must moderate its growth and focus on responsible lending to prevent borrowers from falling into debt distress." Sa-Dhan warns the FY26 improvement rested on better harvests, food disinflation, strong employment and remittances — **none of which can be assumed to persist**; inflation is rising again, the 2026 monsoon was below normal in high-borrower-density districts, and the **CGSMFI-2.0 credit-guarantee facility has expired** (its validity was extended only to 31 August 2026 or ₹20,000 crore of guarantees, whichever is earlier).
  - **Weather is the live variable:** CreditAccess Grameen specifically attributes its PAR accretion uptick to flooding in **Bihar, UP, Chhattisgarh, MP**; ET reports credit-risk hurdles from erratic rainfall and drought in **Karnataka and Maharashtra**.

### Week ahead: 12–16 October 2026

**1. Q2FY27 earnings season goes into full flow.** Business updates are in; the actual P&L — NIM, credit cost, fee income — lands over the next two weeks. The two numbers that matter most: **NIM trajectory** (did the 5–15 bps compression materialise?) and **management guidance on H2 funding costs** post the repo hike. Gold financiers' Q2 results (Muthoot Finance, Manappuram) are the single most-owned data point for the group — watch gold loan yield, LTV discipline and NPA quality.

**2. RBI's October data and any follow-through on the 18% co-lending GST.** The Fitment Committee recommendation needs a Council decision; any signal on timing will move co-lending-heavy lenders.

**3. Festive season demand.** Navratri, Dussehra and Diwali sit in H2FY27, and the quarter-on-quarter growth pattern will be distorted by festive demand shifting from Q2 into Q3. Bajaj Finance's management has signalled it may **revise up** its growth guidance on the back of it.

**4. Rate-hike transmission into incremental funding.** Watch 3/5-year AAA NBFC bond spreads and any fresh NCD/CP issuance pricing — the HFC and diversified NBFC issues this week will show in real time how much of the 25 bps has been absorbed.

**5. Watch list for asset quality:** tractor, CV and rural MSME portfolios; flood-affected microfinance geographies; Q2 disclosure of any fresh management overlays.

### Bottom line for the week ahead
The sector is in its **best fundamental shape in several years** — NPAs at multi-year lows, credit growth re-accelerating to 15.8%, PAT growth guided to 34%+. **The single risk that can undo the earnings story is funding cost.** The repo has been hiked once and more is priced; prime issuers absorb it first. Everything in the next fortnight's results should be read through that lens — and PNB Housing's 10–15 bps AAA benefit is the counter-example proving how much the rating actually matters.
