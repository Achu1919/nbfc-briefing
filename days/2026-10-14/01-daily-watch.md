## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. MUFG weighing $10 bn+ to take MAJORITY control of Shriram Finance — stock jumped 6.3%**
Bloomberg reported on 13 October that Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group (MUFG), Japan's largest bank, is studying raising its Shriram Finance stake to **more than 50% from ~20%** now. The additional investment could exceed **$10 billion**, on top of the **₹39,618 cr ($4.4 bn)** MUFG already paid in April 2026 for its 20% — taking total commitment past **$14 bn**. If executed it would be the largest foreign investment ever into India's financial sector. **Caveats:** talks are at an early stage, no firm timeline, no definitive agreement, and details could change. Shriram Finance closed **+6.31% at ₹988.00**, the third-biggest Nifty 50 gainer of the day. *[Bloomberg via Moneycontrol / Livemint / newzchain]*

**2. September CPI hit a 23-month high of 5.54% — December rate-hike odds up**
Retail inflation for September, released 12 October, came in at **5.54%** (vs 4.83% in August), the highest in 23 months, with **food inflation at 7.74%** (onions alone +108% YoY). This lands on top of the RBI's 7 October **25 bps repo hike to 5.50%** and the shift to a **"calibrated tightening"** stance. SBI Research already expects the repo at **6.00% by December**. For lenders this means: funding costs stay elevated into H2FY27 and the window for rate cuts has closed for now. *[GoVitt / Business Standard / RBI MPC]*

**3. Funding cost is now the single biggest swing factor for NBFC earnings**
The 10-year G-sec yield is up **25–40 bps since August**, and 3-yr/5-yr **AAA NBFC bond yields are up 50–60 bps since June** (JM Financial). Jefferies (12 Oct) said it **prefers banks over NBFCs** post-hike because NBFCs depend on bank/debt-market funding and cannot reprice assets as fast as banks. JB **raised the daily minimum CRR maintenance requirement from 90% to 99%**, effective the fortnight beginning **16 October 2026** — it does not change the 3% CRR level but absorbs ₹40,000–80,000 cr of banking-system liquidity, nudging up banks' cost of funds. *[ETBFSI / News Karnataka / Jefferies]*

**4. PNB Housing completes "quadruple AAA" — CRISIL upgrades to AAA/Stable, expects 5–8 bps NIM gain from the hike**
On 13 October CRISIL Ratings upgraded PNB Housing Finance to **'CRISIL AAA/Stable'** from 'AA+/Stable'. That gives the HFC the top **AAA** rating from **all four** domestic agencies (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, Ind-Ra). MD & CEO Ajai Kumar Shukla told PTI that because only part of the borrowing book is repo-linked (MCLR, CP, NHB refinance, NCDs), the 25 bps hike is **net positive by 5–8 bps of NIM**. Bank term loans are ~40% of its funding. The stock closed **+1.94% at ₹1,222.00**. *[Finsamudra / ET]*

**5. Gold loans: NBFC gold book at ₹3.65 lakh cr, +69.1% YoY — the fastest-growing NBFC asset class**
RBI August data: **loans against gold jewellery at NBFCs grew 69.1% YoY to ~₹3.65 lakh crore**, versus overall NBFC credit growth of 15.8% (to ₹60.64 lakh crore). Gold-loan growth has held near 70% for months (69.3% June, 68.5% July). Retail loans overall grew **22% YoY**. This is the core read-across for gold-loan-heavy books. *[RBI via CNBC-TV18 / MetalsCost]*

**6. Q2FY27 results season starts — Bajaj Finance reports 19 October**
Bajaj Finance will report Q2FY27 results on **Monday 19 October 2026** with an earnings call at 6:00 pm IST (Rajeev Jain, Sandeep Jain). Consensus (JM Financial, 23 NBFCs): **AUM +20% YoY**, RoA ~3%, RoE ~15.1%. *[ScanX / ETBFSI]*

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — HOUSING FINANCE

**Where the market is:**

**1. Growth is intact but the slowest-growing NBFC vertical.** JM Financial projects HFC/AHFC **AUM +13% YoY** in Q2FY27, with disbursements up a much stronger **~24%** — i.e. book growth is being dampened by repayments/prepayments while fresh origination is healthy. Emkay is marginally more cautious at ~12% YoY. PNB Housing Finance and Aadhar Housing are both in JM's preferred-picks list.

**2. Affordable housing is the growth engine.** CRIF High Mark (Oct 2026, Aug-2026 data): affordable housing accounts for **nearly 68% of active housing loans**, with **one in five originations from new-to-credit borrowers**, and growth shifting rapidly to **Tier-II and Tier-III** markets. The segment's affordable housing market stands at roughly **₹19.48 trillion in 2026**, about 46% of all housing loans. ICRA expects AHFC on-book portfolios to grow **20–22% in FY2026**.

**3. Rate transmission is asymmetric — and that's good news for HFCs.** PNB Housing's MD explained that because the industry funds itself through MCLR, commercial paper, NHB refinance and NCDs (not just repo), a 25 bps repo hike does not fully pass through. PNB Housing expects a **net +5–8 bps NIM** benefit. PNB Housing's bank term loans are ~40% of total resources; the CRISIL AAA upgrade should compress that cost.

**4. Borrowers are feeling the EMI pinch.** On an ₹80 lakh, 20-year floating loan, each 25 bps adds roughly **₹1,200/month**. PNB raised its repo-linked lending rate to **8.35% from 8.10%**; Indian Bank to 8.20%, Bank of Baroda to 8.15%, Tamilnad Mercantile to 8.50%. Note HDFC Bank *cut* its one-month MCLR by 15 bps — transmission is not uniform.

**5. Festive demand is holding up.** Developers and industry leaders (13 Oct) said a 25 bps hike won't materially dent demand, especially in the premium/luxury segment, and that Navratri bookings are building. PNB Housing's Shukla: momentum picked up from Ganesh Chaturthi, and "H2 is going to be much better than H1." Godrej Properties reported a **22% rise in Q1FY27 booking value to ₹8,651 cr** though PAT fell 42% YoY to ₹350 cr.

**6. Household leverage is the watch item.** Home loans' share of retail credit slipped ~2 pp to 25.5% (CRIF, Jun-26), the fastest-growing ticket being loans above ₹75 lakh (41% of origination value). With household debt at 45.8% of GDP and housing affordability under pressure from higher rates, watch for a slowdown in the salaried first-time-buyer cohort — while affordable/NTC demand stays robust.

**Read-across:** for any housing book, the near-term mix is favourable (asymmetric repricing + AAA funding + festive disbursement momentum), but the 12-month risk is affordability-driven volume cooling if the repo goes to 6.00% in December as SBI expects.
