## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. RBI's CRR maintenance tightening knocked NBFCs and HFCs down up to 5% on Wednesday — funding-cost risk is the story of the week.**
The RBI has raised the **minimum daily CRR maintenance requirement for banks to 99% from 90%** of the prescribed requirement (statutory CRR itself unchanged at 3% of NDTL), effective **16 October 2026**. Jefferies estimates this could pull **₹40,000–₹80,000 crore (₹400–800 bn)** of liquidity out of the banking system if banks lift their RBI balances by 5–10%, adding **1–2 bps** to banks' cost of funds. The brokerage now **prefers banks over NBFCs** in a rising-rate cycle, since NBFCs' borrowing costs reprice with a lag and their ability to pass on costs is weaker.
👉 **Why it matters at Muthoot Fincorp:** any structural firming of bank funding costs feeds into NBFC cost of funds over the next 2–4 quarters. Watch how much of Wednesday's selloff is sentiment versus real repricing on your next borrowing round.

**2. Muthoot Fincorp has been assigned CARE AA; Stable on its new debt — with the ₹3,000 crore IPO and leverage explicitly flagged.**
CareEdge assigned **CARE AA; Stable** to **₹1,600 crore of NCDs** and **₹1,200 crore of subordinated debt** (rationale dated 5 Oct, published 12 Oct). Positives: two-decade track record, leadership in gold loans, adequate capitalisation, diversified resource profile, **improving consolidated profitability aided by recovery in the microfinance business**. Drags: moderate (though improving) asset quality, gold price risk, intensifying bank competition in gold loans, and geographic concentration in southern states. Notably, CARE says that despite **high leverage**, it takes into account MFL's near-term plan to raise **₹3,000 crore of fresh equity (DRHP already filed)**. Negative rating triggers: consolidated **ROMA below 2.50%** on a sustained basis, **CAR below 18%**, or significant asset-quality deterioration.

**3. Gold price risk is the sector's biggest swing factor — and the sell-side is now writing about it openly.**
CreditSights' 12 October report (*Indian Gold Loans: Rising Risks, Resilient Buffers*) notes **gold prices had fallen ~23% from their Jan–Mar 2026 peak** at the time of writing. It estimates **average portfolio LTVs at Muthoot Finance, Manappuram and IIFL Finance were ~66–70% at June-end 2026**, with lenders applying a further **3–5% haircut** to gold value and excluding ~20% of value for gems/workmanship. The RBI's June FSR had already flagged that a prolonged gold correction could weaken collateral cover and lift delinquencies.

LIC Housing Finance **-5.14%**, HDB Financial **-4.02%**, Shriram Finance **-3.85%**, Cholamandalam **-3.56%**, L&T Finance **-3.29%**, Tata Capital **-3.28%**, Bajaj Finance **-2.62%**. Gold-loan specialists were relative outperformers: **Muthoot Finance -1.75%** at ₹2,580.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Thursday: MSME & Commercial Lending

**Where the growth is:** India has **8.7 crore registered MSMEs** but only **3.6 crore have ever accessed formal credit** — only **41% of registered MSMEs**. Combined commercial credit outstanding across individuals and enterprises stood at **₹65.8 lakh crore as of March 2026**, with **individuals contributing 28%** of that balance. Delinquency at the portfolio level is stable at **~1.8% (90+ balance level)**, but **stress is emerging in unsecured business loans and the ₹2–10 lakh exposure segment**. New-to-credit (NTC) share of originations has fallen from 52% in FY2023 to 42% in FY2026.

**Who is winning:** NBFCs have gained share in the **₹10 lakh–₹2 crore segment** by scaling secured, longer-tenured products and graduating individual borrowers into enterprise borrowers. PSBs dominate entry-level (<₹10 lakh) lending; private banks own >₹2 crore exposures. FlexiLoans projects a **₹2.4–2.6 lakh crore MSME lending opportunity in H2 2026, of which NBFCs account for ~₹36,000 crore**.

**The regulatory cross-currents to watch:**
1. **The draft RBI (NBFCs) Credit Facilities Amendment Directions, 2026** would restrict **revolving credit** — the biggest live friction. FIDC has argued that a borrower who repays should retain access to the sanctioned limit, and that permitting **redraw within the original repayment schedule and maturity** would reduce both indebtedness and interest cost without creating evergreen credit. UGRO's Shachindra Nath has made the same point.
2. **Co-lending directions (effective Jan 2026)** cut the minimum retention to **10% from 20%** and require banks to commit their share upfront — the structural unlock that finally made bank–NBFC partnership work at scale.
3. **MSME (Amendment) Act, 2026 + TReDS** (over **₹8.7 trillion cumulative transactions by FY26**) are slowly making **cashflow-based underwriting** viable, with 30 NBFCs on the platform.
4. **The GST co-lending fee recommendation (18%)** may force NBFCs to hold larger shares of each loan — good for balance-sheet-heavy lenders, hard for fee-driven originators.

**Flip side — digital MSME lenders are retrenching:** Lendingkart and NeoGrowth **shrank their MSME loan books in FY26** to cut defaults, which is why NeoGrowth's fresh ₹85 crore is aimed at stabilising rather than accelerating growth.

**Bottom line for an NBFC balance sheet:** the MSME opportunity is real and policy-supported, but the next 12 months reward **secured, cashflow-underwritten, co-lent** books over unsecured scale — the opposite of the 2023–24 playbook.
