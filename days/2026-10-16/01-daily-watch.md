## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. Gold down ~23% from its Jan–Mar 2026 peak — CreditSights flags gold-lender exposure.** CreditSights' 12 October report *"Indian Gold Loans: Rising Risks, Resilient Buffers"* estimates that average portfolio LTVs at **Muthoot Finance, Manappuram Finance and IIFL Finance were ~66–70% at end-June 2026** — a reasonable buffer on average, but loans originated when gold was near its peak are the most exposed. Gold futures on MCX traded around **₹1.47–1.49 lakh/10g on 15 October**, after a fourth straight session of declines in domestic spot (24K fell ₹900 to ₹1,49,900/10g in Delhi). The killer fact: a 20% fall in gold pushes a 70% LTV loan to ~87% LTV, wiping out the cushion. **Why it matters to Muthoot Fincorp:** this is the single biggest read-across for the gold-loan book — watch for margin-call volumes, auction activity and any LTV re-pricing language in gold lenders' Q2 commentary.

**2. RBI issues No-Objection Certificate for the REC–PFC merger (15 Oct).** Power Finance Corporation and REC Ltd both disclosed on Thursday that RBI has given its NOC for the scheme of **REC merging into PFC**, subject to conditions. This is the largest consolidation in the Indian NBFC/term-lending space in years — REC and PFC are both newly-minted **NBFC-Upper Layer** entities (added to the list earlier this year alongside IRFC and HUDCO). Still pending: shareholder, creditor and other statutory approvals.

**3. RBI's tighter daily CRR maintenance is still driving NBFC funding-cost anxiety.** The RBI raised the share of prescribed CRR that banks must maintain *daily* to **99% from 90%**, leaving the headline CRR unchanged at 3% of NDTL. It doesn't raise the reserve ratio, but it removes banks' flexibility to run down surplus liquidity within the reporting fortnight — which feeds directly into banks' willingness to fund NBFCs. NBFC/HFC stocks sold off on 14 October (LIC Housing −5.13%, Shriram Finance −4.03%, HDB Financial −4.02%) before stabilising on 15 October.

**4. Q2 FY27 NBFC earnings season is delivering big beats.** IIFL Finance PAT (pre-NCI) **₹805 cr, +93% YoY**; L&T Finance consolidated PAT **₹972.8 cr, +32.4%**; HDB Financial Services PAT **₹780 cr, +34.2%**; IRFC PAT **₹1,958 cr, +10%**. The exception: Jio Financial Services PAT **fell 51% YoY to ₹340 cr** — though on a much bigger income base.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Auto Finance & EV Lending

*(Friday rotation)*

### The demand backdrop is the strongest in years
- **FADA: September 2026 vehicle retail hit a record 25,36,920 units, +31.82% YoY** — the best September on record. **Passenger vehicles +32%, two-wheelers +32%.**
- **SIAM: PV dispatches up 21.8% YoY to 4.54 lakh units in September**, capping the industry's **highest-ever quarterly sales** across passenger, commercial and two-wheelers.
- **Vahan: 17,98,635 two-wheelers registered in September 2026, +33.7% YoY and +4.1% MoM — 10 consecutive months of YoY growth.**
- GST rate cuts are the visible catalyst — the Q4FY26 and subsequent demand bump followed the GST reforms, with new-vehicle demand rising alongside used.

### But financing is getting more expensive and more stretched
- **RBI's 25 bps repo hike to 5.50%** (first hike since early 2023) has begun feeding into lending rates; several lenders have already raised repo-linked benchmark rates by up to **25 bps**.
- **TransUnion CIBIL's "Shifting Gears 2026" report:** the average **passenger vehicle loan rose 49% to ₹8.2 lakh** between H1 2019 and H1 2026, and average tenure lengthened by five months to **62 months**. That combination — bigger ticket, longer tenure — is precisely what gets tested when rates rise.
- **51% of higher-value car loan borrowers now come from semi-urban and rural India**, up 6 percentage points from 2019. Rural origination by two-wheeler-to-car upgraders has compounded at **15% CAGR** since 2019, almost double the 8% for overall auto loans.
- **Portfolio quality is genuinely good:** 90+ days-past-due balance-level delinquency **fell 95 bps to 0.5%**. Active PV borrower base **2.5 crore** (1.7x of June 2019). Two-wheeler outstanding balances **₹1.76 lakh crore**, 3.2x the June 2019 level, with 43% of borrowers now entering with >2 years of credit history (vs 27% in 2019).

### Where NBFCs sit — and it's dominant
- **NBFCs write 37% of passenger vehicle loans** (private banks 26%) — up 4 percentage points since 2019.
- **NBFCs write 73% of two-wheeler loans** — more than double their PV share.
- **Auto NBFC disbursements in Q1FY27: ₹1.04 lakh crore, +20.7% YoY, but −4.6% QoQ** as CV volumes normalised after an unusually heavy Q4FY26 pre-buying quarter. Sector AUM grew ~17% YoY (Centrum).
- CV financing cooled sequentially almost everywhere: **Cholamandalam −14.7%, Shriram Finance −13.8%, Mahindra Finance −24.9%, Sundaram Finance −3.5%**. Sundaram was the **only lender with positive overall QoQ disbursement growth (+11.1%)**, aided by its retail franchise.
- **Diversification is the durable story:** Cholamandalam's non-vehicle mix crossed **40% of disbursements**; Shriram's non-CV vehicle finance rose 25% YoY. SME/LAP, gold, construction equipment, personal and home loans all gained share.
- **Shriram Finance** is targeting **new-vehicle financing at 20–25% of disbursements** (currently 16–17%, up from ~10%), with a longer-term goal of ~30% of the book. MUFG's ~20% strategic stake is expected to cut borrowing costs by around 1 percentage point over 2–3 years, funding an aggressive new-vehicle push and possibly opening doors to Suzuki, Toyota, Honda, Yamaha, Denso and Hitachi supply-chain finance. Guidance: ~18% AUM growth FY27, with Q2 likely 15–16%.

### EV lending — the fastest-moving niche in NBFC finance
- **EV volumes are surging:** Shriram Finance cited **EV sales growth of 94.8% YoY** in its Q1 FY27 call. FY26 EV sales: PV +82.4% to 1.89 lakh units, three-wheelers +18.84% to 8.31 lakh, two-wheelers +21.72% to 13.93 lakh.
- **New licences:** **Astranova Mobility's NovaFusion Capital** received RBI approval as an NBFC focused on **commercial EV finance**, targeting ₹5,000 cr AUM in five years. Management frames it around India's ~$100 bn commercial EV transition need.
- **Specialist green lenders scaling:** **ev.fin** (Greaves Cotton's EV NBFC) raised **₹223 cr** of institutional debt from AK Capital, Northern Arc, AU Small Finance Bank, Ambit Finvest, MAS Financial and Maanveeya; managed AUM ~₹522 cr, cumulative disbursements >₹774 cr, 74 cities. Its underwriting differentiator is evaluating **battery health and residual value** alongside borrower income — an OEM-agnostic model embedded with Ather, Ampere, River, Hero, Bajaj, TVS, Suzuki and Ultraviolette dealer networks.
- **Ecofy Finance** partnered with **Ather Energy** to deploy **₹100 cr** in green loans covering vehicle loans, leasing, assured buyback and Battery-as-a-Service.
- **OEM–NBFC tie-ups accelerating:** Muthoot Capital signed an Ampere **Preferred Financer Agreement** and has **₹245 cr of co-lending with Greaves Finance** (49% of its co-lending portfolio); Kinetic EV onboarded **IDFC FIRST Bank** for two-wheeler retail finance; Ashok Leyland tied up with **Union Bank of India** for CV financing.
- **VinFast** signed LOIs for **50+ e-motorcycle showrooms across 34 Indian cities in 15 states/UTs** — a fresh distribution channel NBFCs can finance.
- **Green capital inflows:** **Finnfund** extended a **$12 mn senior secured loan** to **Mufin Green Finance**, which finances EVs, charging infrastructure, battery swapping and solar for MSMEs.

### What to watch
1. **The rate cycle vs. 62-month loan tenures** — the affordability math for large car loans gets tight if another 50 bps lands.
2. **CV normalisation** carrying into H2FY27 — festive restocking is the swing factor.
3. **Monsoon deficit** hitting tractor and rural CV demand, which Shriram has already flagged as a Q2 guidance reassessment trigger.
4. **Battery and residual-value risk** in EV books — the reason most legacy NBFCs are entering EV via co-lending or OEM partnerships rather than building books solo.
5. **Bank competition** — banks are increasingly entering gold and vehicle retail segments, exactly where NBFCs earn their spread.
