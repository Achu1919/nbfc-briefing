## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

1. **RBI gives No Objection Certificate to the PFC–REC merger** — the single biggest structural event in Indian infrastructure lending this cycle. REC informed the exchanges on Friday that it received the RBI NoC on **15 October 2026**, subject to conditions. The merger of REC into PFC will create a single financing entity with an **aggregate loan book of over ₹11 lakh crore**. Swap ratio: **88 PFC shares (₹10 each) for every 100 REC shares (₹10 each)**. Boards approved on 28 June 2026; the scheme still needs shareholder, creditor and other statutory approvals. PFC has earlier guided to completing the merger by **April 2027**. Deloitte is transaction/tax advisor, Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas is legal advisor. → *Direct read-across for Muthoot Fincorp: this sets the template for PSU NBFC consolidation and, more importantly, the pricing benchmark for long-tenor infrastructure credit in India.*
2. **RBI raised banks' daily CRR maintenance requirement to 99% from 90%** (week of 13–14 Oct). CRR itself is unchanged at 3% of NDTL, but banks now have almost no intra-fortnight liquidity flexibility. NBFC and HFC stocks sold off on 14 Oct (LIC Housing −5.13%, Shriram Finance −4.03%, HDB Financial −4.02%) on funding-cost concerns. The 10-year benchmark G-sec yield hit a **three-year high** and government bonds logged a **ninth consecutive weekly loss** on Friday — the transmission channel from RBI's liquidity tightening straight into NBFC cost of funds. This is the key margin risk for H2FY27.
3. **Q2FY27 NBFC results landed in force on Friday** — L&T Finance posted its **highest-ever quarterly PAT of ₹975 crore, up 33% YoY**, with the loan book up 28% to ₹1,36,635 crore; **IIFL Finance PAT jumped 93% YoY to ₹805 crore** with AUM up 38% to ₹1,24,268 crore; **Bajaj Housing Finance PAT rose 17.7% to ₹756.97 crore**; **Fedbank Financial Services PAT rose 63.1% to ₹130.8 crore** with AUM up 36.6% to ₹22,035 crore. The season is confirming the "growth intact, margins under watch" thesis.

- **RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra (7 Oct, post-MPC):** "We don't see any issue regarding asset quality for NBFCs amid high liquidity conditions in the banking system." He noted **bank credit to NBFCs growing ~37%**, and said the RBI stays alert to asset-quality, demand and price risks building.
- **Nuvama Institutional Equities (Oct report):** a rate hike alone is unlikely to trigger broad-based NBFC asset-quality stress; risks should stay **pocketed and segment/player-specific** unless tightening is prolonged and accompanied by a macro shock. Historical evidence: GNPAs fell from 5.7% (Mar 2022) to 4.6% (Mar 2023) even through a 250 bps repo hike cycle.
- **Microfinance stress is easing but not gone:** the **Bharat Microfinance Report 2026** recorded FY26 as a contraction year — active clients **−13% YoY**, outstanding loans **−11% to ₹3.34 trillion**, loan accounts −21%. PAR>180 days stayed high at **4.73%** (from 4.83%). - **Climate risk is now treated as credit risk:** NABARD Chairman Shaji Krishnan warned of **rabi crop stress** after a monsoon season that ended with a **13% rainfall deficiency** (second-lowest since 2001) and urged lenders to build climate-vulnerability indices into underwriting. ## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Saturday: Infrastructure & Project Finance

### The PFC–REC merger is now RBI-sanctioned. What it actually creates

PFC's Q1FY27 investor presentation gives the clearest picture of the combined entity. PFC's **standalone loan asset book stood at ₹5,70,045 crore** as on 30 June 2026, with the **consolidated (PFC + REC) book at ₹11,60,133 crore**. The group is **India's largest renewable-energy financier**, with a renewable loan book of **₹1,63,184 crore** — about **14% of the total book** — split across large hydro (₹19,109 cr) and solar/wind/other RE (₹65,479 cr) at the PFC level. Scheme-wise PFC standalone lending: generation ₹2,70,117 cr, transmission ₹44,161 cr, distribution ₹1,90,021 cr, infra & logistics ₹50,742 cr.

**Asset quality in infra lending remains best-in-class:** PFC's gross Stage III is ₹6,315 crore (86% provided), translating to a **blended net NPA of ~0.15% for PFC and 0.11% for REC** in Q1FY27. The government sector book (₹4,36,836 cr) carries zero Stage III.

**But growth is decelerating, and that is the real story.** PFC's loan book **shrank 2% QoQ** and REC's grew just **1%** in Q1FY27. Brokerages have cut hard:
- **CLSA** trimmed FY27 PAT estimates for both by 2–3%, citing the run-down of the Revolving Bill Payment Facility (RBPF) book and modest segment growth; target for REC cut to ₹420.
- **Bernstein** cut its FY26–28 loan-book CAGR estimate for both to **~7% from ~10%**, flagging three structural pressures: (i) **banks competing aggressively in renewable financing**, (ii) the shift in solar capacity additions toward **rooftop and KUSUM** segments where PFC-REC have limited presence, and (iii) **improving DISCOM finances** reducing loss-funding needs. Both retained Outperform.
- **Next catalysts:** REC's **Q2FY27 results on 27 October 2026**; PFC/REC Q2 due late October.

### IRFC: the quiet compounder delivered again

IRFC reported Q2FY27 PAT of **₹1,958.48 crore, +10.22% YoY**, on revenue of **₹7,866.41 crore (+23.45%)**; H1 FY27 PAT **₹3,885.69 crore**; net worth **₹60,573 crore**. It declared a **₹1.15/share interim dividend** with a **record date of 23 October 2026**, and management flagged preparation for **substantial financing in the rail and energy sectors**. Revenue growing faster than PAT (23.45% vs 10.22%) is consistent with the margin compression being seen across the fixed-rate PSU lender cohort.

### NaBFID is building the offshore funding template

NaBFID raised **$750 million** in its **debut US dollar bond** (10-year, priced at **105 bps over US Treasuries, coupon 6.122%**), the first drawdown under its **$4 billion Global Medium-Term Note programme**. Demand was **55% US, 30% EMEA, 15% Asia**; the bond is listed on both GIFT IFSC exchanges and is expected to be index-eligible (EMBI, JACI). Rated **BBB+ by CareEdge** and **BBB/A-2 Stable by S&P** — equalised with the sovereign given the "almost certain" likelihood of government support. Currency risk is hedged via an RBI swap for five years. The context matters: NaBFID's **loan book grew from ₹97 billion in FY23 to ₹1,027 billion in FY26** and it needs **$10–12 billion annually**. Its top three segments are roads, renewable energy generation and power generation.

### Policy & structural items for infra financiers

- **InvIT/REIT debt financing:** RBI's final framework for **bank lending to REITs and InvITs took effect 1 October 2026** — banks may lend only to **SEBI-registered, listed trusts with ≥80% cash-generating assets**, with a **49% exposure cap** and prescribed security/repayment norms. This formalises a new, bankable asset class for infra sponsors and is a direct competitive consideration for NBFCs that finance these trusts.
- **The financing gap is enormous:** India faces a **~$35 billion annual renewable financing gap** en route to 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; **InvITs are being positioned as the primary capital-recycling mechanism**. The National Infrastructure Pipeline covers ~9,000 projects across 34 sub-sectors, totalling **over $1.5 trillion**.
- **RBI launched the 51st round of the Quarterly Services and Infrastructure Outlook Survey (SIOS) for Q3:2026-27** (12 Oct) — the forward-looking sentiment read on infra order books and input costs is worth tracking when results publish.
- **Cost of funds is the swing factor:** with the RBI having hiked rates for the first time in ~3.5 years (7 Oct) and daily CRR maintenance now at 99%, infrastructure lenders with **large fixed-rate, long-tenor books and offshore borrowings** (PFC, REC, IRFC, NaBFID) face the sharpest repricing risk — while those with floating-rate assets have more flexibility. Analysts note infra/PSU NBFC valuations are near cycle lows, and Bernstein/CLSA both keep Outperform ratings despite growth downgrades: the market is pricing in a 7% grower, not a 10% one.

**Why it matters to Muthoot Fincorp:** the PFC–REC entity at ₹11+ lakh crore becomes the price-setter for long-tenor Indian infrastructure credit and the reference credit for the entire PSU NBFC complex. Any reset in PSU NBFC cost of funds or lending spreads will move benchmark pricing for the broader NBFC market, including gold-loan and retail NBFC funding programmes.
