### IBC for NBFCs — two hats, and why a creditor cannot s7 MFL

**What is it?**
The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code does **not** treat an NBFC like a trading company. Against a defaulting **borrower**, MFL is an ordinary **financial creditor** and can file **s7**. Against **MFL itself**, creditors cannot DIY a CIRP. Only the **appropriate regulator** (RBI) can put a notified NBFC into insolvency, under **s227** of the Code and the **FSP Rules, 2019**. Personal guarantors are a third, recently rewritten, track.

**The Technical Details**

**A. Hat 1 — MFL as financial creditor of a borrower**

**Source:** IBC 2016, ss. 7, 14, 30–33. [EFFECTIVE: ongoing]

| Piece | Rule |
|-------|------|
| Who files | Financial creditor (MFL) against a **corporate debtor** |
| Default floor | Currently **₹1 crore** for ordinary corporate debtors (MCA notification under s4 — verify before a board note if a later S.O. has moved it) |
| Forum | NCLT |
| Clock | Moratorium Day 1 → IRP in 14 days → CoC → plan in **180** days (extendable to **270**) → else liquidation |
| Waterfall (s53) | IRP costs → workmen (24 months) → employees (12 months) → **secured financial creditors** → government (12 months) → unsecured financial → trade → equity |

Gold loans almost never go here: the jewellery is in the branch, SARFAESI / auction is the working remedy, and KLM Axiva (PR 1181) is the surplus-return lesson. MSME, housing, and **personal-guarantee** files are the ones that actually reach NCLT.

**B. Hat 2 — MFL as the entity in stress (FSP track)**

**Source:** IBC **s227**; *Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Insolvency and Liquidation Proceedings of Financial Service Providers and Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2019*, G.S.R. 852(E), **15 Nov 2019**. [EFFECTIVE:2019-11-15]. Primary: [FSP Rules PDF](https://ibbi.gov.in/uploads/legalframwork/cb1d53c7fe47f8f22ab36a40f441db2c.pdf).

**Previously:** a creditor could, in theory, analogise an NBFC to any corporate debtor and try s7. **Now (from 15 Nov 2019):** for a **notified** FSP, that door is closed.

The Rules do four things that matter:

1. **Only the regulator files.** Rule 5(a)(i): CIRP of an FSP that has committed a default under s4 **shall not be initiated except on an application by the appropriate regulator**. The application is then processed *as if* it were a s7 (Rule 5(a)(ii)). Fee: **₹25,000**, Form 1 (Rule 6).
2. **Administrator, not IRP.** On admission, NCLT appoints the person **proposed by RBI** as Administrator (Rule 5(a)(iii)). “Corporate debtor” reads as “financial service provider”; “IRP / RP / liquidator” reads as “Administrator” (Rule 4).
3. **CoR is not pulled during CIRP.** Rule 5(b): an **interim moratorium** starts on the date the regulator files, until admission or rejection. The **licence / registration** that lets the FSP carry on financial services **shall not be suspended or cancelled** during the interim moratorium **or** the CIRP. That is the opposite of a bank licence being killed on day one. Borrowers can still be served; the book is not supposed to freeze because the regulator knocked.
4. **RBI keeps a veto on who ends up running it.** After CoC approval under s30(4), the Administrator must seek RBI’s **NoC** that the resolution applicant will satisfy **fit-and-proper** to control / manage the FSP (Rule 5(d)). Deemed NoC if RBI does not refuse within **45 working days**. An advisory committee of **≥3** members *may* be constituted within **45 days** of commencement (Rule 5(c)).

MCA, by S.O. dated **18 November 2019**, notified NBFCs (including HFCs) above an **asset-size cut-off** as FSPs for these Rules, with RBI as the appropriate regulator. The cut-off in that S.O. is **₹500 crore** of assets as per the last audited balance sheet — confirm the current S.O. on [ibbi.gov.in notifications](https://ibbi.gov.in/en/legal-framework/notifications) before quoting the rupee figure in a board note. MFL as an ML ICC is **well above** that historic cut-off, so the FSP track is the relevant one **if** RBI ever chose to use it. It is **not** a DIY filing, and it is **not** s70A of anything — do not use that label.

RBI still has its **own** Act tools alongside IBC: **s45-IA(6)** CoR cancellation (yesterday’s teaching; PRs 1294/1295), **s45-ID** removal of directors, **s45-IE** reconstruction / amalgamation. IBC is the last-resort resolution tool, not the first.

**C. Hat 3 — personal guarantors (the live line)**

**Source:** IBC ss. 95–96, 124; IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 inserting **s96(4)** and **s124(4)**; IBBI Circular **IBBI/II/106/2026 dated 21 Sep 2026**. [EFFECTIVE:2026-05-26] for the statute; [EFFECTIVE:2026-09-21] for the circular. Primary: [II/106 PDF](https://ibbi.gov.in/uploads/legalframwork/778e13b82df050c20c0c13dfa3e8ea16.pdf).

**Previously:** filing a s95 application against a personal guarantor triggered an **interim moratorium** under s96 — all other debts of that individual froze, which is why counsel loved saying “wait, IBC stay.” **Now (from 26 May 2026):** s96(4) / s124(4) say the interim moratorium **shall not apply** in respect of a personal guarantor to a corporate debtor.

II/106 records two High Courts holding that this is **retroactive to pending applications**, not only to filings after 26 May:

- Bombay HC, *Tata Capital Financial Services Ltd v. Neel Motors LLP*, 24 Jul 2026 — “is filed” includes applications **already pending**; moratorium on the PGs **ceased from 26 May 2026**.
- Delhi HC, *IDBI Trusteeship Services Ltd v. Manish Jain*, 19 Aug 2026 — s96(4) is **retroactive** / quasi-retroactive to pending applications.

IBBI’s instruction to all IPs / IPEs / IPAs (para 4): the interim moratorium under s96 (and correspondingly s124) **has ceased to operate w.e.f. 26 May 2026** even for applications pending on that date.

**Do not confuse with II/108 (24 Sep 2026).** That only extends the clock for IPs to file **PGIRP monitoring forms**, to **31 Dec 2026**. It is not a stay, not a moratorium, not a reason to pause recovery.

**Why It Matters**
- Against **borrowers**, IBC is a recovery tool. Against **MFL**, IBC is a regulator-only nuclear option. Mixing the two in a board note is how people invent a “creditors can drag MFL to NCLT for ₹1 crore” story that is **false** on the FSP track.
- Listed NCD holders of MFL would vote in a CoC **as financial creditors** *if* RBI ever filed. Until then, their rights are **LODR + the trust deed**, not IBC. That is why last Saturday’s Reg 52 clock and this Saturday’s FSP track sit next to each other.
- PG recovery is **open**. Counsel quoting a pre-May-2026 stay is quoting repealed law. Put II/106 on the table.
- Gold-auction surplus (KLM Axiva) and SC repossession conduct remain the **working** recovery overlay. IBC is for the MSME / PG tail, not the jewellery vault.
