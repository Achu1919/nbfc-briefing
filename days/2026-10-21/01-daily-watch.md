## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

1. **NBFCs reopen today at 52-week lows, with the Q2 results flood resuming.** Nifty NBFC closed Monday at **52,026.81 (−1.50%)** — that's −8% over a month and right at the bottom of its 52-week closing range (low 51,777.87). The overhang: RBI's 7 Oct repo hike to **5.50%** (analysts price up to 50 bps more), the **99% daily CRR rule** effective mid-October (Jefferies: ~₹40,000–80,000 cr liquidity lock-up), and a global risk-off mix — Brent near **$110**, US 10-yr at **5.13%**, >90% odds of a Fed hike in December. **Today's batch: Mahindra Finance and Aye Finance results** (evening), markets trading for the first time in three days.

2. **Muthoot Fincorp (your employer): ₹4,000 cr NCD shelf filed; demand firm.** A Draft Shelf Prospectus for up to **₹4,000 cr of secured NCDs** was filed with BSE on **14 Oct** (rated CRISIL **AA/Stable** + BWR **AA+/Stable**). Meanwhile the September Tranche V (₹350 cr base + ₹350 cr greenshoe) closed **fully subscribed 2.47x** (retail 1.65x, HNI 2.58x, institutions 2.01x), and secondary bonds quote ~**10.4–10.5% YTM** (Dec-2033 line, 20 Oct). FP&A read: retail NCD funding is flowing well even in a rate-hike cycle — a useful data point for FY27 cost-of-funds planning.

3. **Gold slid a 4th straight session — watch the gold-loan franchise.** MCX gold (Dec) settled **₹1,47,300 Mon (−0.42%)**, then **₹1,46,482 Tue evening (−0.56%)**; spot ~$4,120 this morning. Driver is a firmer dollar + high US yields, not demand damage — domestic gold is still ~**+₹12,000 YoY**. With Dhanteras/Diwali ahead, festive demand is the swing factor for gold-loan AUM growth and LTV cushions (Muthoot Finance / Manappuram / new entrants).

4. **IRDAI commission-cap risk gets a number: Piramal quantified the hit.** Piramal Finance (coverage 20 Oct) said the proposed insurance distribution commission caps could cost **~18–24 bps of FY28 RoA** (it earns ~₹200 cr from insurance distribution). Bajaj Finance says it has contingency plans ready. This is now the sector's most quantified pending regulatory earnings risk.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Housing Finance (Wednesday focus)

**State of play:** A barbell market — solid loan growth against thinning spreads. The Nifty Housing Finance index has de-rated hard (down ~8% since mid-June; P/E normalized from ~112× to ~32× as earnings caught up), but operating momentum held up through Q2.

- **Growth:** Bajaj Housing — AUM **+25% to ₹1.58 lakh cr**, disbursements +25%, GNPA 0.29%. Can Fin's book +9.8% (tech-migration drag). Management view: housing demand tracks property-price expectations more than marginal rate moves.
- **Margins:** Bajaj Housing guides **20–25 bps NIM compression** for FY27 — ~80% of repo hikes transmit to its cost of funds (three 25 bps hikes → ~61 bps CoF impact by Mar'27). Prime-segment price competition from banks + balance-transfer attrition are the two watch items; affordable/near-prime is the pivot (Sambhav crossed ₹500 cr/month disbursements, target ₹600 cr+).
- **Funding tailwind:** **NHB set to double refinance/affordable-housing allocations** (PLR 7.80%); securitisation is having a record year (₹1.47 tn in H1, ICRA) — HFCs' funding menu is broadening.
- **Festive push:** VTP Realty × Bajaj Housing launched a 12-month EMI-holiday scheme in Pune — demand stimulation is back in the market.
- **Group angle (keep the two Muthoots separate):** Muthoot Finance's housing arm **Muthoot Homefin** — AUM ₹3,496 cr, CARE AA+ reaffirmed, GS3 2.52% (Jun'26). Muthoot Fincorp group's housing subsidiary (**MHFCL**) carries ~₹3,254 cr AUM consolidated (Mar'26, per CareEdge), alongside Muthoot Microfin's ₹13,996 cr. Neither is in the big-HFC bracket that NHB's expansion targets.

**Why it matters at Muthoot Fincorp:** (1) NHB's expanded refinance helps bank-adjacent HFCs — gold-heavy NBFC funding still rides on NCDs/banks, where the CRR-driven cost pressure and firm AA demand (Fincorp's 2.47×-subscribed September tranche) are the two push-pull forces. (2) The housing market's shift down-market (banks squeeze prime → HFCs go affordable) mirrors exactly what's happening in gold loans with new entrants — collateral quality and service economics keep mattering more than headline rates.
