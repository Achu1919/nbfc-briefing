**2. Gold-loan stress is now the sector's main event — and Muthoot FinCorp sits right in the middle of it.**
- Three lenders in four days: **Bajaj Finance** (gold-loan GS3 **0.70% vs 0.35% QoQ**; Stage-2 gold ₹1,099 cr, +₹1,047 cr in the quarter; short-tenor schemes withdrawn in August), **Piramal Finance** (15–23% of industry gold disbursements — roughly **₹4.7 lakh crore in FY26** — went to borrowers who were 90+ days overdue elsewhere; 35% of its customers hold a gold loan with another lender, up from 20–25% two years ago), and now **Shriram**.
- Collateral math: gold is **~23% below its early-2026 peak** (CreditSights, Oct 12); average portfolio LTVs at Muthoot Finance / Manappuram / IIFL were **66–70% at end-June**, so loans written at the top carry the thinnest cushion. Segment NPAs are still low but moving: **IIFL Finance 0.9% (from 0.6% QoQ, 0.1% a year ago)**, **Fedbank Stage-3 0.5%** (from 0.1%).
- **Fincorp specifics:** CARE assigned **CARE AA/Stable** to ₹1,200 cr sub-debt and ₹1,600 cr NCDs (rationale Oct 5) — MFL is now **AA from CRISIL, AA+ from BWR, AA from CARE**. - **The PSU power lenders are the funding benchmark — and they are cheap for a reason.** PFC holds **52.63% of REC** after the February 2026 purchase; combined books exceed **₹11 lakh crore**; the **Budget 2026 restructuring of PFC/REC** is the structural overhang. Both fell with the market on Oct 23 — **PFC −3.07% to ₹316.00, REC −2.33% to ₹293.65** — in a week when **PSU issuers pulled back from the bond market** as investors demanded higher yields while **private NBFCs raised ₹2.58 trillion**.
- **Regulatory tailwind for takeout finance:** RBI's **Concentration Risk Management (Fourth Amendment) Directions, 2026 (Aug 25)** extended **NBFC-IFC large-exposure limits to IDF-NBFCs in the Upper Layer** — more headroom for post-COD refinancing and toll-operate-transfer projects.
- **IRFC** shows the other side of the model: a **₹1.15/share dividend with a record date of Oct 23**, a **₹13,527 cr refinance of Hyderabad Metro debt** at lower rates (September), and a ₹4,200 cr loan to DVC for clean-energy projects — sovereign backing, spread compression, and a stock around ₹76.50, near its 52-week low of ₹74.06.
- **Read for Muthoot Fincorp:** infrastructure financiers set the **marginal price of bond-market money**. When NaBFID pays 105 bps over UST for 10-year dollars and PSU lenders step back from domestic issuance, corporate spreads for everyone else — including gold-loan NCD shelves — stay wide. That is precisely why Fincorp's 8.56–9.25% retail NCD is attractive to investors and expensive for the lender. The strategic lesson holds: **secured, short-tenure, high-yield assets (gold, MSME) remain the only reliable way to out-earn a 5.50% repo.**
