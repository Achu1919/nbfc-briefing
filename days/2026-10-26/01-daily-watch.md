## 🔴 URGENT / High Impact

**1. Rural lending is being tightened across the sector — published 06:00 IST today.** NBFCs are moderating disbursements and tightening monitoring in drought-affected western and southern India after the 2026 southwest monsoon ended at **87% of the long-period average — the weakest since 2015**. Four large lenders flagged it on their Q2 calls:

- **Shriram Finance cut FY27 AUM growth guidance to 15–17% from 18%.** AUM ₹3.29 trillion (+17% YoY). Gross stage 3 **4.65%** (vs 4.64% QoQ, 4.57% a year ago); gross stage 2 **7.04%** (vs 6.96% QoQ, 6.92% YoY); credit cost **2.03% of assets, up from 1.68%**. Executive vice-chairman **Umesh Revankar** named **Maharashtra, Karnataka and Telangana** as worst-hit, called the stress "not widespread", kept long-term credit-cost guidance at 2% but flagged a temporary **30–40 bps** rise in select geographies and products.
- **Mahindra Finance** (AUM ₹1.47 trillion): **282 districts** classified drought-prone. MD & CEO **Raul Rebello** said collections have been strengthened in those locations and funding reduced in some stressed markets. Tractor disbursements fell **20% QoQ**, steeper than expected (InCred Equities, Oct 22). Offsetting positive: gross stage 3 improved to **3.4%** (from 3.9%), stage 2 to **4.7%** (from 5.8%), credit cost to **1.6%** (from 2.2%).
- **L&T Finance:** rural disbursements **₹6,356 crore, −9% QoQ** (+1% YoY); rural book **₹33,465 crore** (+22% YoY, +3% QoQ). MD & CEO **Sudipta Roy**: "we are also being careful… near-flattish QoQ." Underwriting safeguards tightened in vulnerable districts; expects Kharif harvest to support Q3 disbursements.
- **HDB Financial Services** also flagged geographically concentrated risk.

**Why it matters for you:** rating agencies are explicit that the damage lands in Q3–Q4, not Q2. **Jinay Gala (India Ratings):** "The next two quarters could be challenging for the rural economy… Microfinance, unsecured business loans and, to some extent, tractor financing could face pressure… stress may not show up immediately, but from December onwards." **Sanjay Agarwal (CareEdge):** "adverse impact on asset quality because of the drought… likely to show up more in the fourth quarter than the third… tractor loans and small-ticket unsecured personal loans." Backdrop: September CPI **5.54%** (from 4.83%), food inflation **7.74%** (from 5.97%) — real rural incomes are being squeezed from both sides.

**2. Gold's 29% slide is now a documented collateral story — not just a stock-price story.** Spot gold closed at **$3,993.96/oz on 23 October**, lowest since mid-July, against a record **$5,608.35 in January** — a **29% drawdown**; the metal lost ~4% in the week. Two things follow:

- **Stress is showing in the newer gold books.** **Bajaj Finance's** stage 2 + stage 3 assets rose **₹1,601 crore** in Q2, of which **₹1,047 crore came from gold loans** — driven by short-tenure (3-month) schemes discontinued in August. **Piramal Finance** MD **Jairam Sridharan** disclosed that **15–23% of gross gold-loan disbursements** over the last year went to customers who had been 90+ days past due at least once in their lifetime — "a shockingly high number" — and that the share of customers with gold loans beyond their primary lender has risen to **35%** from 20–25% two years ago. **Bajaj** now plans to cut originations to customers with 3+ lending relationships to **3.5% of new loans by March 2027** from 7% today.
- **The established players' cushion math is public.** **Muthoot Finance's average LTV was 66.68%** at end-June (management, 1 Aug call). On that book, gold can fall roughly **11%** (to ~$3,550) before the *average* loan breaches the RBI's lowest 75% cap for loans above ₹5 lakh; ~22% (to ~$3,130) before it hits 85%; ~33% (to ~$2,660) before principal is uncovered. **Fitch's Siddharth Goel** notes credit cost "will not be meaningful, given the LTVs" but that lenders could face higher provisioning or auction activity — and that newer entrants (Piramal entered this year, Bajaj in 2016) lack Muthoot's and Manappuram's auction/recovery muscle. Note the direction of travel: gold in India is **down 3% since April**, versus **+65% in FY26** — the growth tailwind has become a headwind.

**3. NBFCs ask IRDAI for a 5-year runway on insurance commissions — reported 00:56 IST today.** FIDC has told IRDAI that the proposed caps — **2% on credit life, 5% on credit health** — make credit-linked insurance distribution unviable, since distribution costs are ~**32% of premium**. It has proposed a phased cut from the current **45–49%** to **25% over five years**. IRDAI's consultation window **closed 25 October**; caps could be notified as early as **January 2027**. Nuvama's read: the earnings hit lands mostly in **FY28**, depending on final guidelines. For a gold-loan-led lender with a large branch network that monetises credit protect, this is the single biggest known fee-income risk on the horizon.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Microfinance & SHG Lending

**Monday rotation: microfinance.** The sector is two years past its worst stress cycle and the data now says recovery — but the recovery is narrow, expensive to fund, and about to be tested by a weak monsoon.

**Portfolio: bottomed, growing weakly.** MFIN's Micrometer (Q1 FY27, as of 30 June 2026) put the industry gross loan portfolio at **₹3,28,708 cr — down 6.9% YoY but up 1.1% QoQ**, the second consecutive sequential expansion. Infomerics/CRIF put GLP at **₹3.33 lakh cr in June 2026** (+0.6% QoQ) with the annual contraction easing from 13.2% (March) to **7.3%**. Disbursements in Q1 FY27 rose **18.6% YoY to ₹61,172 cr**; average ticket size ~**₹62,000**; the **₹1 lakh-plus segment grew ~90% YoY** in origination value.

**The borrower base is still shrinking — this is a consolidation, not a revival.** Active clients fell **11.3% to 3.1 crore**; loan accounts dropped to **99 million from 125 million** a year earlier. Growth is coming from larger tickets to existing, seasoned borrowers, not new customers. That is healthier credit but a smaller franchise.

**Market share has flipped to NBFC-MFIs.** NBFC-MFIs hold **₹1.46 lakh cr = 44.3% of the industry** (from 39% in March 2024) and are the largest provider of micro-credit. Bank microfinance portfolios fell **28.5% YoY** — largely a **reclassification artefact** (banks moving microfinance loans into retail), which flatters MFI share. Within MFIN's 40 members, the **12 large MFIs control 93.8% of AUM and 95.7% of disbursements**, and took **98% of incremental borrowings in FY26** — small and mid MFIs are still funding-constrained at higher cost. Coverage: 36 states/UTs, 718 districts.

**Asset quality has genuinely repaired.** PAR 31–180 is **1.6%** (from 5.6% a year earlier); PAR 1–30 **0.5%**; PAR 31–90 **0.7%**. ICRA: industry **90+ dpd 3.3% (June 2026) vs 4.7% (FY25)**. The guardrails did the work — **₹2 lakh cap on total borrower indebtedness** (including unsecured retail) and **max three MFI lenders per borrower**, enforced by MFIN and Sa-Dhan.

**Profitability is snapping back — that is the trade this quarter.** CareEdge expects sector RoTA of **~2.4% in FY27** versus **0.5% in FY26 and −1.2% in FY25**, with credit cost falling to **5.4% of average total assets** from 8.7%. ICRA projects NBFC-MFI **RoMA of 1.9–2.2% in FY27** and AUM growth rebounding to **15–17%**. The Q2 prints show it: CreditAccess Grameen's PAT nearly quadrupled to ₹502 cr and it *raised* guidance and *cut* lending rates 50 bps; Muthoot Microfin's AUM rose 22% to **₹15,323 cr**, disbursements +28%, and — notably — **cost of funds fell below 10% for the first time (9.93%)**, with collection efficiency at 98.11%.

**The live risk is geographic and seasonal.** Maharashtra declared drought in **265 of 358 talukas**; Karnataka declared **177 taluks** drought-affected. Together those two states carry **₹53,076 cr of microfinance exposure — over 16% of the industry** (June 2026). MFIN has advised members to monitor and lend carefully in both. ICRA's K Ravichandran warns lenders with concentrated exposure could see higher delinquencies; MFIN CEO **Alok Misra** notes microfinance loans aren't crop loans but are linked to the same rural livelihoods. CreditAccess has flagged a mild rise in PAR accretion from August–September flooding in Bihar, UP, Chhattisgarh and MP. CareEdge's Agarwal thinks microfinance may escape the worst of the drought hit *for now* precisely because it is only just recovering.

**Structural read for a gold-loan-led lender:** microfinance is the segment where a gold loan book's customer overlap is highest and where the collateral logic of a gold lender becomes an advantage — Muthoot Microfin's **₹453 cr of gold loans disbursed through referral/co-lending with Muthoot Fincorp** in Q2 is exactly this play, and it is growing from a small base. Migration of stressed unsecured/MFI customers into gold loans is being observed across the market (IIFL says "some of those customers are also moving towards gold loans") — good for secured volume, but it imports a weaker credit cohort into the gold book at the precise moment gold prices are falling. That combination is the thing to watch in Q3.
